
B.2 Estimates for the reflection and half-turn operators

We begin with the reflection operator 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m1 RAW[758680,758884) \mathsf{U}
. Let

MATH A2.SS2.p1.m2 RAW[758890,759192) f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
, so that

MATH A2.SS2.p1.m3 RAW[759202,759662) f(x,y)=f(y)
. Although 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m4 RAW[759673,759856) f
 initially depends only on the auxiliary
coordinate 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m5 RAW[759908,760091) y
, reflection gives 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m6 RAW[760110,760853) (\mathsf{U}f)(x,y)=f(2x-y)
, which generally depends on both coordinates. Its macroscopic and microscopic
components are 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m7 RAW[760947,761290) \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f
 and 
MATH A2.SS2.p1.m8 RAW[761295,761670) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f
, respectively. The following
probabilistic representation identifies the squared norm of the microscopic
component created by reflection with an expected conditional variance, thereby
enabling quantitative estimate by the conditional Poincaré estimates used in
Appendix C.

Concretely, under 
MATH A2.SS2.p2.m1 RAW[762286,762512) \pi_{\eta}
, write

MATH A2.E8.m1 RAW[762739,764015) Y_{+}\coloneqq X+\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,,\hskip 20.00003ptY_{-}\coloneqq X-\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\,,\hskip 20.00003ptX\sim\mu\,.

(B.8)

The pair 
MATH A2.SS2.p2.m2 RAW[764297,764639) (Y_{+},Y_{-})
 is exchangeable. We henceforth identify

MATH A2.SS2.p2.m3 RAW[764680,764939) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
 with 
MATH A2.SS2.p2.m4 RAW[764945,765360) L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})
 and write 
MATH A2.SS2.p2.m5 RAW[765371,765662) f(y)
 for a
macroscopic observable. Under this identification,

MATH A2.E9.m1 RAW[765939,767896) (\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f)(y)=\mathbb{E}[f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Var(f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+})\,.

(B.9)

To quantify the microscopic component 
MATH A2.SS2.p3.m1 RAW[768252,768627) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f
 created when reflection
acts on 
MATH A2.SS2.p3.m2 RAW[768660,768962) f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
, define

MATH A2.E10.m1 RAW[769191,770180) \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})^{1/2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\,.

(B.10)

By (B.5),

MATH A2.SS2.p3.m3 RAW[770788,771515) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}
, and hence

MATH A2.E11.m1 RAW[771747,773415) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|\,,\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\,.

(B.11)

Thus a lower bound for 
MATH A2.SS2.p3.m4 RAW[773712,774036) \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}
 quantifies how much of a macroscopic
observable reflection transfers into the microscopic subspace.

Lemma B.1 (Uniform macroscopic coercivity).

Assume 
MATH A2.Thmtheorem1.p1.m1 RAW[774715,775032) \beta\eta\leq 1
, and set

MATH A2.E12.m1 RAW[775269,776608) \rho_{\rm mac}\coloneqq\frac{1-\alpha\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma_{\rm gap}\coloneqq\sqrt{1-\rho_{\rm mac}^{2}}=\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha\eta}}{1+\alpha\eta}\,.

(B.12)

Note that 
MATH A2.Thmtheorem1.p1.m2 RAW[776966,777476) \gamma_{\rm gap}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\alpha\eta}
.
Then the following hold.

(1) For every 
MATH A2.Thmtheorem1.p2.m1 RAW[777689,778157) f\in L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})
,

MATH A2.Thmtheorem1.p2.m2 RAW[778159,778787) \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})
 and

MATH A2.E13.m1 RAW[779019,780803) 4\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}-\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,.

(B.13)

(2) Consider the mean-zero macroscopic subspace 
MATH A2.Thmtheorem1.p3.m1 RAW[781252,781966) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\pi_{\eta})
.
Then, it holds that

MATH A2.E14.m1 RAW[782215,783586) \|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\|\leq\rho_{\rm mac}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\succeq-\frac{1}{2}\,I\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.

(B.14)

Consequently,

MATH A2.E15.m1 RAW[784175,785371) \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}=(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})^{1/2}\succeq\gamma_{\rm gap}I\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.

(B.15)

We prove Lemma B.1 in
Appendix C.1. The identity
(B.9) expresses 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m1 RAW[786758,787212) \|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|
 in terms of the
conditional variance of 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m2 RAW[787253,787577) f(Y_{-})
 given 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m3 RAW[787584,787798) Y_{+}
. Differentiating the
corresponding conditional expectation shows that this variance controls how
quickly 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m4 RAW[787904,788247) \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f
 changes with 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m5 RAW[788261,788444) y
. The Poincaré inequality for

MATH A2.SS2.p4.m6 RAW[788475,788725) \pi_{\eta}^{Y}
 then bounds the norm of a centered 
MATH A2.SS2.p4.m7 RAW[788761,788944) f
 in terms of

MATH A2.SS2.p4.m8 RAW[788957,789411) \|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|
, giving the stated estimate.

Since 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m1 RAW[789530,789854) \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}
 is boundedly invertible on 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m2 RAW[789882,790178) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}
,
(B.11) gives the polar decomposition

MATH A2.E16.m1 RAW[790765,793076) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}\,:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}}\,.

(B.16)

Here 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m3 RAW[793355,793679) \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}
 measures the size of the microscopic component created by
reflection, while the isometry 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m4 RAW[793769,794081) \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}
 records its direction in

MATH A2.SS2.p5.m5 RAW[794107,794355) \mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
.
The next proposition says that the half-turn acts nearly as 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m6 RAW[794417,794627) -I
 on the
corresponding microscopic directions 
MATH A2.SS2.p5.m7 RAW[794672,795289) \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}f
.

Proposition B.2 (Global half-turn bound).

There exist universal constants 
MATH A2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m1 RAW[795898,796172) c,C>0
 such that, if

MATH A2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m2 RAW[796187,796504) \beta\eta\leq c
, then

MATH A2.E17.m1 RAW[796738,798574) \bigl\|(I+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp})\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}v\bigr\|^{2}\leq C\log(1/\gamma_{\rm gap})\,\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}v\|^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptv\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.

(B.17)

We prove Proposition B.2 in
Sections C.2 and C.3.

Together, Lemmas B.1 and B.2
provide the estimates used in the modified 
MATH A2.SS2.p7.m1 RAW[800931,801145) L^{2}
 argument below.
