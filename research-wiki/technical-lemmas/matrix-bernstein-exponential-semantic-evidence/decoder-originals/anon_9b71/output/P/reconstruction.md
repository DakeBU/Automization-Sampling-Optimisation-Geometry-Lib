For every finite nonempty index type \(\mathcal I\), equipped with a finite enumeration and decidable equality, let \(\operatorname{Mat}_{\mathcal I}(\mathbb R)\) denote the real square matrices whose rows and columns are indexed by \(\mathcal I\), and let \(I_{\mathcal I}\) denote its identity matrix. For every \(A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{\mathcal I}(\mathbb R)\) such that
\[
A^{\mathsf T}=A,
\qquad
I_{\mathcal I}-A\succeq0,
\]
and every real number \(s\) satisfying \(0<s\),
\[
I_{\mathcal I}+sA+(e^s-s-1)(A A)-\exp(sA)\succeq0.
\]

Here products of matrices are ordinary matrix products, and scalar multiples are real scalar multiples. For any real square matrix \(M\) on this index type, \(M\succeq0\) means that \(M^{\mathsf T}=M\) and \(x^{\mathsf T}Mx\ge0\) for every real coordinate vector \(x\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\). The matrix exponential is the convergent power series
\[
\exp(M)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{M^n}{n!},
\qquad M^0=I_{\mathcal I},
\]
in the real matrix algebra with its Euclidean \(L^2\) operator norm and ordinary finite-coordinate topology. The scalar \(e^s\) is the real scalar exponential. The index type is required to be nonempty; the proposition does not include an empty index type.
