    Backward interpolation yields gradient contraction

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.StochasticProcesses.backwardInterpolation_contraction_of_expandedBakryEmery · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let P_u be positive real-linear evolution operators and q_u a family of observables. If α>0, the expanded CD(α,∞) inequality holds, Φ(u)=P_uΓ(q_u) is continuous, and its exact right derivative is P_u(LΓ(q_u)−2Γ(q_u,Lq_u)), then Φ(s)≤Φ(t)e^{-2α(t-s)} for s≤t.

    \[\Phi'(u)=P_u(L\Gamma(q_u)-2\Gamma(q_u,Lq_u))\ge2\alpha\Phi(u)\Longrightarrow\Phi(s)\le\Phi(t)e^{-2\alpha(t-s)}\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Turn curvature into a pointwise generator bound

      The expanded CD inequality gives LΓ(q_u)−2Γ(q_u,Lq_u)≥2αΓ(q_u) at every state.

    2. 2Push the inequality through the positive linear evolution

      Order preservation maps the pointwise bound through P_u; linearity pulls out 2α. The supplied derivative identity then gives Φ'(u)≥2αΦ(u).

    3. 3Read the backward interpolation endpoints

      The scalar comparison theorem converts the derivative lower bound into exponential contraction from t back to s.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • X is arbitrary and L is real-linear on real-valued observables.
    • The curvature parameter α is strictly positive and the fully expanded diagonal CD inequality holds for every observable and point.
    • Each P_u is real-linear and preserves pointwise order.
    • The scalar interpolation Φ(u)=P_uΓ(q_u)(x) is continuous.
    • At every u, its exact right derivative within [u,∞) is P_u(LΓ(q_u)−2Γ(q_u,Lq_u))(x).
    • The endpoints satisfy s≤t.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Turn curvature into a pointwise generator bound

    The expanded CD inequality gives LΓ(q_u)−2Γ(q_u,Lq_u)≥2αΓ(q_u) at every state.

    \[L\Gamma(q_u)-2\Gamma(q_u,Lq_u)\ge2\alpha\Gamma(q_u)\]

    2. Push the inequality through the positive linear evolution

    Order preservation maps the pointwise bound through P_u; linearity pulls out 2α. The supplied derivative identity then gives Φ'(u)≥2αΦ(u).

    \[\Phi'(u)\ge2\alpha\Phi(u)\]

    Use hpositive, then simplify with the linear-map laws.

    3. Read the backward interpolation endpoints

    The scalar comparison theorem converts the derivative lower bound into exponential contraction from t back to s.

    \[\Phi(s)\le\Phi(t)e^{-2\alpha(t-s)}\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Apply backward_interpolation_contraction_of_growth with rate 2α.
