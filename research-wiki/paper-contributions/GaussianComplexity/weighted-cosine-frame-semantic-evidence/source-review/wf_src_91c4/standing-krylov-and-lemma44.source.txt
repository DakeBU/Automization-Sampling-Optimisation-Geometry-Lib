We first control the information revealed by adaptive queries via a standard Krylov argument.
The deferred-rotation argument of [22, Lemma 9.2] uses the rotational symmetry
of the undisclosed eigenbasis to represent the algorithm’s
output in a block Krylov space generated by
independent Gaussian vectors.
For vectors ⟦g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}⟧ and ⟦\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})⟧, define
⟦\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda^{j}g_{a}:1\leq a\leq q+1,\ 0\leq j\leq q\}.⟧
Lemma 4.3 (Krylov representation).
Fix an integer ⟦q\geq 0⟧ with ⟦2q+1<d⟧ and let ⟦X⟧ be the output. There exist independent vectors
⟦g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\sim N(0,I_{d})⟧ such that
⟦O^{\top}X\in\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})\qquad\text{almost surely}.⟧
The additional starting vector accounts for the
output, which may lie outside the span of the query points
and oracle replies.
Equivalently, the lemma gives a representation
⟦O^{\top}X=\sum_{a=1}^{q+1}p_{a}(\Lambda)g_{a}⟧,
where each ⟦p_{a}⟧ has degree at most ⟦q⟧.
The polynomial coefficients may depend on the entire
extended experiment.
We therefore control the output through an estimate that
holds simultaneously for every vector in ⟦\mathcal{K}_{q}⟧.
The proof of Lemma 4.3 is deferred to
Appendix C.1.
A uniform bound on the Krylov space.
We next control a high-frequency cosine statistic uniformly over
the space in Lemma 4.3.
For a query budget ⟦q⟧, set
⟦n=2q+1,\qquad D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\cos(n\theta_{1}),\ldots,\cos(n\theta_{d})\bigr).⟧
The choice of ⟦n⟧ places this statistic above all frequencies
arising from products of two degree-⟦q⟧ cosine polynomials.
Lemma 4.4 (Uniform bound on the Krylov space).
There is an absolute constant ⟦C>0⟧ such that the following holds.
Let ⟦q\geq 0⟧ satisfy ⟦2q+1<d⟧, let
⟦g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}⟧ be independent ⟦N(0,I_{d})⟧ vectors, and let
⟦\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})⟧.
Define
⟦\tau_{d,q}=C\left[(q+1)\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+\frac{(q+1)(q+1+\log d)\log d}{d}\right].⟧
If ⟦\tau_{d,q}\leq 1/2⟧, then, with probability at least ⟦0.99⟧,
⟦|u^{\top}D_{n}u|\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}.⟧
