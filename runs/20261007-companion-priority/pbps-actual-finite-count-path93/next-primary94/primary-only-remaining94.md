The source defines strictly ordered 0<t1<...<tn<T and prescribed trajectories made from harmonic arcs and reflections. At event time tj the state is post-reflection. Its A.7 density uses the integrated rate along the whole trajectory and the rate at the pre-jump left limit. Iterating clocks naturally gives completed-arc hazard factors plus final survival, so a mathematical bridge is required before this product can be called the printed path density.

Use t0=0 and t_(n+1)=T, z0 initial and z_j=S Phi_(tj-t_(j-1)) z_(j-1). On [tj,t_(j+1)) the path is Phi_(s-tj)z_j, and at T it is Phi_(T-tn)z_n. Required finite-path statements:

1. The path rate is integrable over [0,T], and its integral equals sum_(j=0)^n Lambda_(z_j)(t_(j+1)-tj). Each arc is continuous; the path differs from an adjacent-arc closed-interval formula at only finitely many endpoints. Finite integral splitting and translations justify this identity; those facts must be proved, not assumed as source hypotheses.
2. For each j=1,...,n, the path left limit at tj exists and equals Phi_(tj-t_(j-1))z_(j-1), before reflection. Therefore its left-limit rate equals the incoming rate used in the clock-product density. Continuity of the deterministic arc and of the residual rate suffices; continuity of the reflection map must not be added.
3. The product of all arc survivals times final survival equals exp(-whole-path integrated rate). Multiplying the incoming rate factors gives the exact printed finite-sector density. This is a pointwise deterministic identity for every prescribed simplex point, independent of actual clock sample exceptional sets.

At n=0, the simplex is the empty tuple, path is a single harmonic arc, no left-limit obligations exist, and density is exp(-Lambda_z0(T)). At T=0,n=0 the integral is zero and density is one. At T=0,n>0 the strict simplex is empty. These are part of the finite statement, not excluded by a positive-time premise. At tj, post-jump and pre-jump values must remain distinct; endpoints can be removed from a time integral because their Lebesgue measure is zero, not because the values are equal.

Full A.7 still needs summation of the count sectors and bounded signed tests. A nonnegative identity follows from a measurable count partition of full probability plus Tonelli. Bounded real tests require measurable compositions, integrability, absolute summability and equality of signed sums; boundedness alone on an unnormalized infinite simplex measure is not enough. The printed proof obtains a per-term absolute bound using a trajectory rate cap L and simplex volume T^n/n!, producing ||Psi||inf*(LT)^n/n!. A probability-mass argument is another sufficient route once finite-sector masses sum to one. These are alternatives, not two mandatory premises.

The source writes bounded measurable functional without fixing a topological path carrier. A coordinate-generated product sigma algebra suffices to formulate a precise bounded functional statement and use measurable actual/prescribed path maps, sector partition and signed integration. It does not justify claims about all Borel functionals for an unspecified Skorokhod or other path topology. For the finite density rewrite, time integrability is proved for each explicitly prescribed piecewise path; generic joint evaluation measurability on an uncountable function product must not be assumed. Source intent about a stronger path topology is not explicit in this excerpt.
