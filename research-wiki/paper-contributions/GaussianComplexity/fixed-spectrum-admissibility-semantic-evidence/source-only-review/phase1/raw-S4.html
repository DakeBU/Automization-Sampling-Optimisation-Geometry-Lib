4 Lower Bound via Hidden Eigenbasis

The preprint [22] proved an Ω⁡(log⁡d)\Omega(\log d) query lower bound for centered Gaussian sampling with Id⪯A⪯2​IdI_{d}\preceq A\preceq 2I_{d} at total variation accuracy ε=110\varepsilon=\frac{1}{10}. We subsequently examine the limitations of this analysis for establishing accuracy-dependent lower bounds in Section 4.4.

Theorem 4.1 (Extension to general κ\kappa).

For every fixed δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1), there exists d0​(δ)d_{0}(\delta) such that, for every integer d≥d0​(δ)d\geq d_{0}(\delta), 2≤κ≤d1−δ2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}, and 0<ε≤1/100<\varepsilon\leq 1/10,

QG​(d,κ,ε)≥δ256​κ​log⁡d.Q_{G}(d,\kappa,\varepsilon)\geq\frac{\delta}{256}\sqrt{\kappa}\log d.

Compared with Theorem 3.1, this bound also covers 2≤κ<42\leq\kappa<4 and 116<ε≤110\frac{1}{16}<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{10}, and it holds even when the spectrum of AA is known to the algorithm. On the other hand, it does not grow as ε→0\varepsilon\to 0 and it requires κ≤d1−δ\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}.

4.1 The Hard Family

We use a construction similar to that in [22, Section 9.2], with a cosine eigenvalue grid (Chebyshev nodes) and a random orthogonal rotation, while extending the spectral interval from [1,2][1,2] to [1,κ][1,\kappa]. For d≥2d\geq 2 and κ≥2\kappa\geq 2, define

θi=(i−12)​πd,λi=κ+12+κ−12cosθi,Λ=diag(λ1,…,λd).\theta_{i}=\frac{(i-\tfrac{1}{2})\pi}{d},\qquad\lambda_{i}=\frac{\kappa+1}{2}+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\cos\theta_{i},\qquad\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}). (11)

Draw O∼Haar⁡(O⁡(d))O\sim\operatorname{Haar}(\mathrm{O}(d)), independently of the algorithm’s private randomness, and set A=O​Λ​O⊤A=O\Lambda O^{\top}. The associated potential and target are fA​(x)=x⊤​A​x/2f_{A}(x)=x^{\top}Ax/2 and πA=N⁡(0,A−1)\pi_{A}=N(0,A^{-1}).

The spectrum is deterministic and may be supplied to the algorithm. In particular, the Gaussian normalizing constant is known. The unknown component is the random eigenbasis represented by OO.

Remark 4.2 (Comparison with κ=2\kappa=2 case).

At κ=2\kappa=2, the spectrum in Eq. (11) recovers the original construction in [22]. More generally, if A2A_{2} denotes the original precision matrix with the same rotation OO, then A=(κ−1)​A2−(κ−2)​IdA=(\kappa-1)A_{2}-(\kappa-2)I_{d}. Thus one matrix–vector reply for either matrix determines the corresponding reply for the other. The proof compares ∑icos⁡(n​θi)​⟨x,O​ei⟩2\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\langle x,Oe_{i}\rangle^{2} with ‖x‖2\|x\|^{2}, where n=2​q+1n=2q+1. For the output of a qq-query algorithm, the ratio is at most d−Ω⁡(1)d^{-\Omega(1)} in absolute value (Lemma 4.4), whereas under the target the ratio of the means has absolute value e−Θ⁡(n/κ)e^{-\Theta(n/\sqrt{\kappa})} (Lemma 4.5). Hence the test works for nn up to order κ​log⁡d\sqrt{\kappa}\log d, rather than log⁡d\log d as at κ=2\kappa=2, which is the source of the factor κ\sqrt{\kappa} in Theorem 4.1.

4.2 Properties of the Hard Family

This subsection states the four lemmas used in the proof of Theorem 4.1. Lemma 4.3 places the output of any qq-query algorithm in a block Krylov space generated by independent Gaussian vectors, Lemma 4.4 bounds a quadratic form with cosine weights uniformly over this space, Lemma 4.5 evaluates the same quadratic form under the target, and Lemma 4.6 verifies that the hard family is admissible. All proofs are deferred to Appendix C.

Krylov representation of the output.

We first control the information revealed by adaptive queries via a standard Krylov argument. The deferred-rotation argument of [22, Lemma 9.2] uses the rotational symmetry of the undisclosed eigenbasis to represent the algorithm’s output in a block Krylov space generated by independent Gaussian vectors.

For vectors g1,…,gq+1∈ℝdg_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and Λ=diag⁡(λ1,…,λd)\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}), define

𝒦q(g1,…,gq+1)=span{Λjga:1≤a≤q+1, 0≤j≤q}.\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda^{j}g_{a}:1\leq a\leq q+1,\ 0\leq j\leq q\}.
Lemma 4.3 (Krylov representation).

Fix an integer q≥0q\geq 0 with 2​q+1<d2q+1<d and let XX be the output. There exist independent vectors g1,…,gq+1∼N⁡(0,Id)g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\sim N(0,I_{d}) such that

O⊤​X∈𝒦q​(g1,…,gq+1)almost surely.O^{\top}X\in\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})\qquad\text{almost surely}.

The additional starting vector accounts for the output, which may lie outside the span of the query points and oracle replies. Equivalently, the lemma gives a representation O⊤​X=∑a=1q+1pa​(Λ)​gaO^{\top}X=\sum_{a=1}^{q+1}p_{a}(\Lambda)g_{a}, where each pap_{a} has degree at most qq. The polynomial coefficients may depend on the entire extended experiment. We therefore control the output through an estimate that holds simultaneously for every vector in 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q}. The proof of Lemma 4.3 is deferred to Appendix C.1.

A uniform bound on the Krylov space.

We next control a high-frequency cosine statistic uniformly over the space in Lemma 4.3. For a query budget qq, set

n=2​q+1,Dn=diag⁡(cos⁡(n​θ1),…,cos⁡(n​θd)).n=2q+1,\qquad D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\cos(n\theta_{1}),\ldots,\cos(n\theta_{d})\bigr).

The choice of nn places this statistic above all frequencies arising from products of two degree-qq cosine polynomials.

4.4 Limitations of the Analysis

Theorem 4.1 is restricted to 2≤κ≤d1−δ2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta} for fixed δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1). Although it applies to every ε≤1/10\varepsilon\leq 1/10, its lower bound does not grow as ε\varepsilon decreases. We discuss two limitations of the analysis that affect extensions to larger condition numbers and higher accuracy.

Limitation on κ\kappa.

Our approach does not extend the Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d) lower bound to condition numbers κ≳d/log2⁡d\kappa\gtrsim d/\log^{2}d. Our proof controls the output of the algorithm by first applying Lemma 4.3 to place O⊤​XO^{\top}X in 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q}, and then bounding the cosine statistic uniformly over this space. However, the enlarged space 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} has (q+1)2(q+1)^{2} generating vectors. Since the eigenvalues of Λ\Lambda are distinct, these vectors span a space of the following dimension.

Lemma 4.7 (Dimension of the Krylov space).

For every integer q≥0q\geq 0 and independent g1,…,gq+1∼N⁡(0,Id)g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\allowbreak\sim N(0,I_{d}),

dim𝒦q=min⁡{d,(q+1)2}almost surely.\dim\mathcal{K}_{q}=\min\{d,(q+1)^{2}\}\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Once (q+1)2≥d(q+1)^{2}\geq d with q=Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)q=\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d), we have 𝒦q=ℝd\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathbb{R}^{d} almost surely. For n=2​q+1<dn=2q+1<d, the definition of θi\theta_{i} gives ∑idcos2⁡(n​θi)=d/2\sum_{i}^{d}\cos^{2}(n\theta_{i})=d/2, so at least one coordinate satisfies |cos⁡(n​θi)|≥1/2|\cos(n\theta_{i})|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}. Taking its coordinate unit vector yields

supu∈𝒦q∖{0}|u⊤​Dn​u|‖u‖2≥12.\sup_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|u^{\top}D_{n}u|}{\|u\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Our separating test in Lemma 4.4 requires this supremum to be at most 1/21/2, which is incompatible with the displayed bound. Thus an argument based on a uniform bound over 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} cannot apply once (q+1)2≥d(q+1)^{2}\geq d.

Limitation on accuracy.

Our test compares the ratio (−1)n​x⊤​O​Dn​O⊤​x/‖x‖2(-1)^{n}x^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}x/\|x\|^{2} with a threshold tt. Lemma 4.4 puts every output of the algorithm below tt only if t≥at\geq a, where aa is the uniform bound on 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} that the lemma provides. Chebyshev’s inequality with Lemma 4.5 puts samples from the target above tt only if t<|μn|/Mt<|\mu_{n}|/M. Thus |μn|/M>a|\mu_{n}|/M>a, whatever the threshold. At the same time, the Bernstein calculation in the proof of Fact C.6 requires a≥c/da\geq c/\sqrt{d} even to certify a failure probability below one. Finally, Lemma 4.5 gives |μn|/M≤e−2n/κ|\mu_{n}|/M\leq e^{-2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}. Since n=2​q+1n=2q+1, we must have

cd≤a<|μn|M≤e−2(2q+1)/κ.\frac{c}{\sqrt{d}}\leq a<\frac{|\mu_{n}|}{M}\leq e^{-2(2q+1)/\sqrt{\kappa}}.

Taking logarithms yields q=O⁡(κ​log⁡d)q=O(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d), independently of ε\varepsilon. A different choice of the threshold or of the constants therefore cannot give a bound that grows as ε→0\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 4.8 (Scope of the limitations).

These limitations concern our proof. We do not know whether the same fixed-spectrum family supports stronger lower bounds. Our analysis therefore leaves open whether the same fixed-spectrum family supports stronger lower bounds.
