For every natural number d with d ≥ 2, every real number κ with κ ≥ 2, and every real orthogonal d × d matrix O (with either determinant sign), index coordinates by i ∈ Fin d, that is, integers i = 0, …, d − 1. Define
θᵢ = (i + 1/2)π/d.
For each real parameter η, define
A(η) = O diagᵢ((η + 1)/2 + ((η − 1)/2) cos θᵢ) Oᵀ,
and define Qη : ℝᵈ → ℝᵈ by Qη(x) = A(η)x, using ordinary matrix-vector multiplication and the usual Euclidean coordinate identification.
Then every x ∈ ℝᵈ satisfies both vector identities
Qκ(x) = (κ − 1) Q₂(x) − (κ − 2)x,
and
Q₂(x) = (κ − 1)⁻¹ (Qκ(x) + (κ − 2)x).
Here scalar-vector products and vector sums and differences have their usual real meanings. The same d, κ, O, and x occur in both identities. Since κ ≥ 2, κ − 1 is nonzero and its displayed reciprocal is the usual real inverse. No probability law on O is specified.
