Independent source derivation and comparison to current module
Example 5.17 fixes F(y)=<y,Ay>/2 with A symmetric on real Euclidean space, then restricts to the unit sphere with its induced inner product. The sphere tangent plane at x is K=(R x)^perp; the inclusion differential has that image. The ambient derivative of F is <u,Ax> by symmetry: the two equal terms from differentiating the pairing cancel the factor 1/2. Orthogonal projection onto K then gives G(x)=Ax-<x,Ax>x for unit x. In Lean, the restricted differentiability witness is explicitly proved using the smooth inclusion and the ambient derivative; the ambient candidate is explicitly in K. The older unhalved pairing/uniqueness theorem is instantiated at B=A/2, not confused with Example 5.17's normalization. Its underlying SphereGradient theorem uses range_mvfderiv_subtypeVal, so the pairing is the induced one and tests all actual tangent directions, not merely an unrelated chart pairing.

The polynomial G(y)=Ay-<y,Ay>y on all E is smooth even without symmetry or finite dimension for continuous linear A. This is a chosen global extension of the genuine source gradient only on the source restriction; away from the unit sphere tangency is not required. The private proof supplies ContDiff infinity and HasFDerivAt at every point, not merely a name or a totalized derivative. In real inner-product notation DG(x)[u]=Au-(<u,Ax>+<x,Au>)x-<x,Ax>u. Corollary 5.16 identifies the source Hessian with the restriction of this derivative to K followed by orthogonal projection to K. In Lean halfRayleighTangentHessian composes K.subtypeL, fderiv, and K.orthogonalProjectionOnto; both domain and codomain are K. K is the complement of a finite-dimensional line, so this projection does not require complete E. In the evaluation proof the private derivative witness rewrites fderiv, the normal term vanishes under projection, and projection fixes u and preserves pairing with u. Thus H_x u=P_x(Au)-lambda u and <u,H_x u>=<u,Au>-lambda ||u||^2, with lambda=<x,Ax>. The latter follows by pairing the source operator formula and is explicit in the PSD convention in Exercise 5.18. All these formula-level operations generalize to arbitrary continuous linear A and real inner-product spaces, but the generalized operator is not generally the Hessian of F. For example, take in R^3 a nonzero skew map rotating the e2,e3 plane and annihilating e1. Then F is zero while at x=e1 the projected derivative is nonzero on K. This is a scope issue, not a source correction.

Exercise 5.18 assumes zero induced gradient and PSD on every tangent direction at the same x. For each unit y set a=<x,y>, u=y-a x. Then u is tangent and a^2+||u||^2=1. Stationarity gives Ax=lambda x; symmetry gives <x,Au>=<Ax,u>=0, and <u,Ax>=0 follows directly. Thus 2(F(y)-F(x))=<u,Au>-lambda ||u||^2=<u,H_x u> >= 0. This yields a global comparison and a genuine nonzero eigenvector x. For any eigenvalue mu choose a nonzero eigenvector v and normalize to z=v/||v||. Then F(z)=mu/2, and the minimum inequality gives lambda <= mu. Since lambda is attained at x this is leastness, not only a lower bound. This proof uses no eigengap, strict PSD, simplicity, nonzero tangent direction, or ambient PSD. Dimension one is included: K={0}, both unit points are on the line through x, the gap is zero, and A is scalar there. The terminal proof does not call the pairing theorem as a proof-body ingredient; pairing and Corollary 5.16 supply source interpretation of the hypotheses. This does not certify a separately named general Lean connection/Hessian API or the universal Corollary.
