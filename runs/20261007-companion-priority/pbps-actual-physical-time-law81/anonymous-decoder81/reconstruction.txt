Let E be any finite-dimensional real inner-product space, equipped with its Borel measurable structure; dimension zero is permitted. Let V:E->R be twice continuously differentiable, let alpha and beta be nonnegative real numbers, and let eta be real. Assume alpha>0, alpha<=beta, and, for every x,v in E, alpha*||v||^2 <= D^2 V(x)[v,v] <= beta*||v||^2. Assume eta>0 and beta*eta<=1. These are the six supplied analytic premises; in particular no initial distribution, trajectory, measurable selection, integration theorem, or probabilistic construction is assumed.

The following objects are literal definitions. P is the countable product of rate-one exponential measures on R, on the space of all real sequences. For every real sequence s, define e_s(k)=max(s(k),0), as a nonnegative real. This definition applies even to exceptional sequences. For y,r in E define c=y-eta*gradient V(r). For a state z=(u,v) and a real time h define
Phi(y,r,h,z)=(c+cos(h)*(u-c)+sqrt(eta)*sin(h)*v, -sin(h)/sqrt(eta)*(u-c)+cos(h)*v).
With d=gradient V(u)-gradient V(r), define S(r,(u,v))=(u,v-(2*inner(v,d)/||d||^2)*d) and rate(r,(u,v))=sqrt(eta)*max(0,inner(v,d)). Real division is total, so when d=0 the quotient is zero and S leaves the state unchanged. eta>0 makes sqrt(eta) strictly positive. For a nonnegative t let Lambda(y,r,z,t) be the real interval integral from 0 to t of rate(r,Phi(y,r,h,z)). Define tau(y,r,z,e), valued in nonnegative reals with an added infinity, as the infimum of nonnegative times t with Lambda(y,r,z,t)>=e; an empty hitting set has infimum infinity. The infimum definition itself must not be replaced by an assumed attained hit or an assumed unique root. In particular e=0 admits time zero.

A record is either a finite pair (accumulated nonnegative time,state) or an absorbing stopped marker with no state. Its next update, with threshold e, leaves a stopped record stopped. From a finite record (a,z), it becomes stopped if tau(y,r,z,e)=infinity; otherwise it becomes (a+tau,S(r,Phi(y,r,tau,z))). Finite tau is extracted by untopD 0; although that extractor returns zero at infinity, the branch at infinity stops the record. Starting at record_0=(0,z0), define record_(k+1) using threshold e(k). eventTime_n is the finite accumulated time for a finite record and infinity for a stopped record. No state is assigned at infinity.

For y in E define q_y as the normalized exponential tilt of volume by x |-> -V(x)-||x-y||^2/(2*eta). Let gamma be the standard Gaussian measure on E, with identity covariance, and let M_y=(q_y product gamma) product P. Thus a draw w=((r,v),s) from M_y consists of an independent reference r with measure q_y, velocity v with measure gamma, and sequence s with measure P. Let t_pi=pi, regarded as a nonnegative real.

Then there exists one total function Z(y,r,z0,t,s), valued in E product E, jointly measurable in (y,r,z0,t,s). For every y,r,z0,s,t,n and finite record a=(a_time,a_state), if eventTime_n<=t<eventTime_(n+1) for thresholds e_s and record_n=a, then Z(y,r,z0,t,s)=Phi(y,r,t-a_time,a_state). Here time comparisons use the extended nonnegative reals, and the subtraction t-a_time is nonnegative-real subtraction, namely max(t-a_time,0). Separately, for every y,r,z0,s,t for which no index n satisfies eventTime_n<=t<eventTime_(n+1), Z(y,r,z0,t,s)=z0. These two requirements define the displayed behavior on all input sequences, including exceptional ones; the finite-interval rule is conditional and does not assert interval coverage for every sequence.

For every fixed y,r,z0, for P-almost every sequence s, simultaneously for every nonnegative real t there are n and a finite record a such that eventTime_n<=t<eventTime_(n+1), record_n=a, a_time<=t, t-a_time<tau(y,r,a_state,e_s(n)), and Z(y,r,z0,t,s)=Phi(y,r,t-a_time,a_state). Also Z(y,r,z0,0,s)=z0 on that same almost-everywhere set. Thus almost surely all finite times are covered by a finite record's half-open interval, including its left endpoint and excluding its next event. The null set may depend on y,r,z0; the theorem does not assert a single P-full set for all those inputs. The universal quantifier over time is inside the almost-everywhere statement.

Moreover there exist a Markov kernel R from E to E and a Markov kernel H from E product E to E. Every fiber R(y) equals q_y. For every y,x, the measure H(y,x) is the pushforward of M_y under w=((r,v),s) |-> first_coordinate(Z(y,r,(x,v),t_pi,s)). The kernel input order is (y,x); x is deterministic, r is sampled, and the sequence is independent of both r and v under the specified product measure. IsMarkovKernel supplies probability normalization of all fibers, while Kernel supplies measurability of the measure-valued families.

For every y,x, pushing M_y forward by w |-> Z(y,r,(x,v),0,s) gives (Dirac x) product gamma. In addition, for each fixed y,x, for M_y-almost every w=((r,v),s), Z(y,r,(x,v),0,s)=(x,v) and there exist n and a finite record a witnessing eventTime_n<=t_pi<eventTime_(n+1), record_n=a, a_time<=t_pi, t_pi-a_time<tau(y,r,a_state,e_s(n)), and Z(y,r,(x,v),t_pi,s)=Phi(y,r,t_pi-a_time,a_state). These last almost-everywhere statements concern the full product M_y, not just the sequence measure P, and their null sets may depend on y,x.

There is no claim here that H preserves q_y, that its terminal law equals q_y, that it contracts, has any error or query-cost bound, or satisfies a time-homogeneous semigroup law. Measurability of Z is a global deterministic conclusion; probability normalization comes from the Markov-kernel conclusions; almost-everywhere time coverage has the exact parameter scopes stated above. No uniform event-count bound or quantitative tail bound is asserted.
