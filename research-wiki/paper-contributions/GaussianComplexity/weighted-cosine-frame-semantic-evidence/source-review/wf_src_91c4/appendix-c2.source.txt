C.2 Proof of Lemma 4.4
Set ⟦r=q+1⟧ and ⟦n=2q+1⟧. For ⟦1\leq i\leq d⟧, define
⟦t_{i}=(1,\sqrt{2}\cos\theta_{i},\ldots,\sqrt{2}\cos(q\theta_{i}))^{\top},\qquad\gamma_{i}=((g_{1})_{i},\ldots,(g_{r})_{i})^{\top}.⟧
The vectors ⟦\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}⟧ are independent
⟦N(0,I_{r})⟧. Let ⟦B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r^{2}}⟧ have
⟦i⟧th row ⟦d^{-1/2}(\gamma_{i}\otimes t_{i})^{\top}⟧.
Our strategy is to write ⟦u=Bc⟧ and control ⟦\|u\|^{2}⟧
through ⟦B^{\top}B⟧, and ⟦u^{\top}D_{n}u⟧ through
⟦B^{\top}D_{n}B⟧. The following fact verifies that the
columns of ⟦B⟧ span ⟦\mathcal{K}_{q}⟧ and shows that these
two matrices have expectations ⟦I_{r^{2}}⟧ and ⟦0⟧,
respectively. We will then prove concentration around
these expectations to obtain a uniform bound on
⟦\mathcal{K}_{q}⟧.
Fact C.5 (Cosine basis and moment identities).
The matrix ⟦B⟧ satisfies
⟦\operatorname{range}(B)=\mathcal{K}_{q},\qquad\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]=I_{r^{2}},\qquad\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]=0.⟧
Moreover,
⟦\|t_{i}\|^{2}\leq 2r,\qquad\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=dI_{r},\qquad\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=0.⟧
Proof.
⟦\boxed{\operatorname{range}(B)=\mathcal{K}_{q}}⟧.
Since
⟦\lambda_{i}=(\kappa+1)/2+(\kappa-1)\cos\theta_{i}/2⟧,
the identity ⟦\cos(j\theta)=T_{j}(\cos\theta)⟧ expresses
⟦\cos(j\theta_{i})⟧ as a degree-⟦j⟧ polynomial in ⟦\lambda_{i}⟧
with nonzero leading coefficient.
For each ⟦a⟧, the change of basis between
⟦\bigl((g_{a})_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\bigr)_{i=1}^{d}⟧
and ⟦\Lambda^{j}g_{a}⟧, ⟦0\leq j\leq q⟧, is therefore
triangular and invertible.
The nonzero column normalizations in ⟦B⟧ do not change
its range, proving the first identity.
⟦\boxed{\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]=I_{r^{2}}}⟧.
Recall that ⟦\theta_{i}=(i-\tfrac{1}{2})\pi/d⟧.
For every integer ⟦k⟧ with ⟦0<|k|<2d⟧,
⟦\displaystyle 2\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{2d}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos(k\theta_{i})⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[\sin\!\left(\frac{ki\pi}{d}\right)-\sin\!\left(\frac{k(i-1)\pi}{d}\right)\right]⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\sin(k\pi)-\sin(0)=0.⟧
Since ⟦\sin(k\pi/(2d))\neq 0⟧, this gives
⟦\sum_{i}\cos(k\theta_{i})=0⟧.
Index the coordinates of ⟦t_{i}⟧ by ⟦0,\ldots,q⟧.
The entries involving the constant coordinate satisfy
⟦\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{0}^{2}=d,\qquad\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{0}(t_{i})_{j}=\sqrt{2}\sum_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})=0\quad(1\leq j\leq q).⟧
For ⟦1\leq j,k\leq q⟧, the product-to-sum identity gives
⟦\displaystyle\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{j}(t_{i})_{k}⟧
⟦\displaystyle=2\sum_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\cos(k\theta_{i})⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\cos((j-k)\theta_{i})+\sum_{i}\cos((j+k)\theta_{i})⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d,&j=k,\\
0,&j\neq k.\end{cases}⟧
Indeed, ⟦0<j+k\leq 2q<2d⟧, while ⟦j-k=0⟧ exactly
when ⟦j=k⟧; otherwise ⟦0<|j-k|<2d⟧.
By symmetry, these calculations cover all matrix entries,
and hence ⟦\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=dI_{r}⟧.
Since ⟦\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]=I_{r}⟧,
the row definition of ⟦B⟧ now gives
⟦\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\bigr)⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\left(I_{r}\otimes\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\right)=I_{r^{2}}.⟧
⟦\boxed{\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]=0}⟧.
Since ⟦n=2q+1⟧, for ⟦0\leq j,k\leq q⟧ we have
⟦1\leq n-j-k\leq n\pm j\pm k\leq n+j+k\leq 4q+1<2d.⟧
Thus the cosine-sum identity above gives
⟦\displaystyle\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\cos(j\theta_{i})\cos(k\theta_{i})⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\quad⟧
⟦\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}\bigl[\cos((n+j+k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n+j-k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n-j+k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n-j-k)\theta_{i})\bigr]⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\quad⟧
⟦\displaystyle 0.⟧
Each entry of ⟦\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}⟧
is a constant multiple of such a sum, so
⟦\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=0⟧.
Consequently,
⟦\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\bigr)⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\left(I_{r}\otimes\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\right)=0.⟧
Finally,
⟦\|t_{i}\|^{2}=1+2\sum_{j=1}^{q}\cos^{2}(j\theta_{i})\leq 1+2q\leq 2r.⟧
∎
We next bound the deviations of the two matrices from
their expectations in operator norm. These simultaneous
bounds will control both ⟦\|Bc\|^{2}⟧ and
⟦c^{\top}B^{\top}D_{n}Bc⟧ for every coefficient vector ⟦c⟧.
Fact C.6 (Simultaneous Gram-matrix concentration).
For a sufficiently large absolute constant ⟦C⟧ in the
definition of ⟦\tau_{d,q}⟧, with probability at least
⟦0.995⟧,
⟦\|B^{\top}B-I_{r^{2}}\|_{\rm op}\leq\tau_{d,q},\qquad\|B^{\top}D_{n}B\|_{\rm op}\leq\tau_{d,q}.⟧
Proof.
Truncation and its bias.
Set
⟦L=2r+3\log(1000d)⟧ and ⟦R_{i}=\|\gamma_{i}\|^{2}⟧.
Since ⟦\mathbb{E}e^{R_{i}/3}=3^{r/2}⟧,
Markov’s inequality gives, for ⟦x\geq 0⟧,
⟦\mathbb{P}(R_{i}>2r+3x)\leq\exp\!\left(\frac{r}{2}\log 3-\frac{2r}{3}-x\right)\leq e^{-x},⟧
where the last inequality uses ⟦(\log 3)/2<2/3⟧.
Consequently, the event
⟦\mathcal{T}=\{R_{i}\leq L\text{ for every }i\}⟧
has probability at least
⟦1-d/(1000d)=0.999⟧.
For ⟦s\geq 0⟧, the same tail bound gives
⟦\mathbb{P}(R_{i}>L+s)\leq e^{-s/3}/(1000d)⟧.
Hence
⟦\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[R_{i}\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}>L\}}]⟧
⟦\displaystyle=L\mathbb{P}(R_{i}>L)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{i}>L+s)\,ds\leq\frac{L+3}{1000d}.⟧
Define the independent truncated matrices
⟦Y_{i}=\frac{1}{d}(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top})\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}\leq L\}}.⟧
By radial symmetry,
⟦\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}\leq L\}}]=aI_{r},\qquad a=\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{E}[R_{i}\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}\leq L\}}].⟧
The scalar ⟦a⟧ is the same for every ⟦i⟧.
Since ⟦\mathbb{E}R_{i}=r⟧, we have ⟦0\leq a\leq 1⟧ and
⟦1-a=\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{E}[R_{i}\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}>L\}}]\leq\frac{L+3}{1000rd}.⟧
Moreover,
⟦\mathbb{E}Y_{i}=(a/d)(I_{r}\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top})⟧.
Fact C.5 therefore gives
⟦\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}Y_{i}=aI_{r^{2}},\qquad\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\mathbb{E}Y_{i}=0.⟧
Thus, the truncated weighted sum has zero expectation.
Bounds for the centered summands.
Put ⟦Z_{i}=Y_{i}-\mathbb{E}Y_{i}⟧.
Using ⟦\|t_{i}\|^{2}\leq 2r⟧, we obtain
⟦\|Y_{i}\|_{\rm op}=\frac{R_{i}\|t_{i}\|^{2}}{d}\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}\leq L\}}\leq\frac{2rL}{d},\qquad\|\mathbb{E}Y_{i}\|_{\rm op}=\frac{a\|t_{i}\|^{2}}{d}\leq\frac{2r}{d}.⟧
Hence
⟦\|Z_{i}\|_{\rm op}\leq\frac{2r(L+1)}{d}=:R_{*}.⟧
The same bound holds for
⟦\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i}⟧, since ⟦|\cos(n\theta_{i})|\leq 1⟧.
The Gaussian fourth moments give
⟦\mathbb{E}[R_{i}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]=(r+2)I_{r}.⟧
Indeed, the off-diagonal entries vanish by sign symmetry,
while each diagonal entry equals ⟦3+(r-1)=r+2⟧.
Since
⟦(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top})^{2}=R_{i}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}⟧
and
⟦(t_{i}t_{i}^{\top})^{2}=\|t_{i}\|^{2}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}⟧,
we have
⟦Y_{i}^{2}=\frac{R_{i}\|t_{i}\|^{2}}{d^{2}}(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top})\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{i}\leq L\}}.⟧
Dropping the truncation indicator in this positive
semidefinite expression yields
⟦\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}Y_{i}^{2}⟧
⟦\displaystyle\preceq\frac{r+2}{d^{2}}\sum_{i}\|t_{i}\|^{2}(I_{r}\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top})⟧
⟦\displaystyle\preceq\frac{2r(r+2)}{d^{2}}\left(I_{r}\otimes\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\right)⟧
⟦\displaystyle=\frac{2r(r+2)}{d}I_{r^{2}}.⟧
Here we used ⟦\|t_{i}\|^{2}\leq 2r⟧ and
⟦\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=dI_{r}⟧.
Since
⟦\mathbb{E}Z_{i}^{2}=\mathbb{E}Y_{i}^{2}-(\mathbb{E}Y_{i})^{2}\preceq\mathbb{E}Y_{i}^{2},⟧
the sequence ⟦(Z_{i})_{i}⟧ has variance parameter at most
⟦v_{*}:=\frac{2r(r+2)}{d}.⟧
The same is true for ⟦(\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i})_{i}⟧, because
⟦\mathbb{E}[(\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i})^{2}]=\cos^{2}(n\theta_{i})\mathbb{E}Z_{i}^{2}\preceq\mathbb{E}Z_{i}^{2}.⟧
Applying matrix Bernstein.
Both sequences consist of independent, centered,
self-adjoint matrices.
The two-sided bounded matrix Bernstein inequality gives, for ⟦t>0⟧,
⟦\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\|\sum_{i}Z_{i}\right\|_{\rm op}>t\right)\leq 2r^{2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(v_{*}+R_{*}t/3)}\right),⟧
and the same bound holds for
⟦\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i}⟧.
Set
⟦z=\log(1000r^{2}),\qquad\Delta=\sqrt{2v_{*}z}+\frac{2R_{*}z}{3}.⟧
A direct expansion gives
⟦\Delta^{2}-2z(v_{*}+R_{*}\Delta/3)=\frac{2R_{*}z}{3}\sqrt{2v_{*}z}\geq 0.⟧
Therefore each matrix bound fails with probability
at most ⟦2r^{2}e^{-z}=0.002⟧.
By a union bound, with probability at least ⟦0.996⟧,
⟦\left\|\sum_{i}Z_{i}\right\|_{\rm op}\leq\Delta,\qquad\left\|\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i}\right\|_{\rm op}\leq\Delta.⟧
On ⟦\mathcal{T}⟧, the original and truncated sums agree.
Using their expectations computed above, we obtain
⟦B^{\top}B-I_{r^{2}}=\sum_{i}Z_{i}-(1-a)I_{r^{2}},\qquad B^{\top}D_{n}B=\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})Z_{i}.⟧
Intersecting ⟦\mathcal{T}⟧ with the two concentration
events therefore gives, with probability at least
⟦1-0.001-0.004=0.995⟧,
⟦\|B^{\top}B-I_{r^{2}}\|_{\rm op}\leq\Delta+(1-a),\qquad\|B^{\top}D_{n}B\|_{\rm op}\leq\Delta.⟧
Simplifying the bound.
Since ⟦2q+1<d⟧, we have ⟦d\geq 2⟧ and ⟦2r\leq d⟧.
Thus ⟦z=O(\log d)⟧ and ⟦L+3=O(r+\log d)⟧,
with absolute implicit constants.
Together with ⟦r+2\leq 3r⟧, these estimates imply
⟦\displaystyle\Delta+(1-a)⟧
⟦\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{r(r+2)z}{d}}+\frac{4r(L+1)z}{3d}+\frac{L+3}{1000rd}⟧
⟦\displaystyle\leq c\left[r\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+\frac{r(r+\log d)\log d}{d}\right]⟧
for an absolute constant ⟦c>0⟧.
Since ⟦r=q+1⟧, choosing the absolute constant
⟦C⟧ in ⟦\tau_{d,q}⟧ so that ⟦C\geq c⟧ gives
⟦\Delta+(1-a)\leq\tau_{d,q}⟧.
This proves both asserted bounds.
∎
Proof of Lemma 4.4.
Choose the absolute constant ⟦C⟧ sufficiently large
for Fact C.6 to apply.
Then, with probability at least ⟦0.995⟧,
⟦\|B^{\top}B-I_{r^{2}}\|_{\rm op}\leq\tau_{d,q},\qquad\|B^{\top}D_{n}B\|_{\rm op}\leq\tau_{d,q}.⟧
On this event, let ⟦u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}⟧.
By Fact C.5, we can write
⟦u=Bc⟧. The two matrix bounds give
⟦\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}⟧
⟦\displaystyle=c^{\top}B^{\top}Bc\geq(1-\tau_{d,q})\|c\|^{2},⟧
⟦\displaystyle|u^{\top}D_{n}u|⟧
⟦\displaystyle=|c^{\top}B^{\top}D_{n}Bc|\leq\tau_{d,q}\|c\|^{2}.⟧
Since ⟦\tau_{d,q}\leq 1/2⟧, combining these inequalities
yields
⟦|u^{\top}D_{n}u|\leq\frac{\tau_{d,q}}{1-\tau_{d,q}}\|u\|^{2}\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|u\|^{2}.⟧
These inequalities hold for every ⟦c⟧ on the same event,
so the conclusion holds simultaneously for every
⟦u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}⟧.
Since ⟦0.995\geq 0.99⟧, the lemma follows.
∎
