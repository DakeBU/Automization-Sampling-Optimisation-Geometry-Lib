4 Lower Bound via Hidden Eigenbasis
[S4.p1; HTML line 1057]
[S4.p1.1; HTML line 1058]
The preprint [22] proved an $\Omega(\log d)$ query lower bound for centered Gaussian
sampling with $I_{d}\preceq A\preceq 2I_{d}$ at total variation accuracy $\varepsilon=\frac{1}{10}$ .
We subsequently examine the limitations of this analysis for establishing accuracy-dependent lower bounds in Section 4.4.
[S4.Thmtheorem1; HTML line 1062]
Theorem 4.1 (Extension to general $\kappa$ ).
[S4.Thmtheorem1.p1; HTML line 1064]
[S4.Thmtheorem1.p1.1; HTML line 1065]
For every fixed $\delta\in(0,1)$ , there exists $d_{0}(\delta)$ such
that, for every integer $d\geq d_{0}(\delta)$ ,
$2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}$ , and $0<\varepsilon\leq 1/10$ ,
[S4.Ex9; HTML line 1068]
$Q_{G}(d,\kappa,\varepsilon)\geq\frac{\delta}{256}\sqrt{\kappa}\log d.$
[S4.p2; HTML line 1077]
[S4.p2.1; HTML line 1078]
Compared with Theorem 3.1, this bound also covers $2\leq\kappa<4$ and
$\frac{1}{16}<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{10}$ , and it holds even when the spectrum of $A$ is known to
the algorithm. On the other hand, it does not grow as $\varepsilon\to 0$ and it requires $\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}$ .

4.1 The Hard Family
[S4.SS1.p1; HTML line 1085]
[S4.SS1.p1.1; HTML line 1086]
We use a construction similar to that in [22, Section 9.2],
with a cosine eigenvalue grid (Chebyshev nodes) and a random orthogonal rotation, while extending the spectral
interval from $[1,2]$ to $[1,\kappa]$ .
For $d\geq 2$ and $\kappa\geq 2$ , define
[S4.E11; HTML line 1090]
$\theta_{i}=\frac{(i-\tfrac{1}{2})\pi}{d},\qquad\lambda_{i}=\frac{\kappa+1}{2}+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\cos\theta_{i},\qquad\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}).$
(11)
[S4.SS1.p1.2; HTML line 1098]
Draw $O\sim\operatorname{Haar}(\mathrm{O}(d))$ , independently
of the algorithm’s private randomness, and set $A=O\Lambda O^{\top}$ .
The associated potential and target are
$f_{A}(x)=x^{\top}Ax/2$ and $\pi_{A}=N(0,A^{-1})$ .
[S4.SS1.p2; HTML line 1103]
[S4.SS1.p2.1; HTML line 1104]
The spectrum is deterministic and may be supplied to the algorithm.
In particular, the Gaussian normalizing constant is known.
The unknown component is the random eigenbasis represented by $O$ .

Remark 4.2 (Comparison with $\kappa=2$ case).
[S4.Thmtheorem2.p1; HTML line 1110]
[S4.Thmtheorem2.p1.1; HTML line 1111]
At $\kappa=2$ , the spectrum in
Eq. (11) recovers the original construction in [22].
More generally, if $A_{2}$ denotes the original precision matrix
with the same rotation $O$ , then
$A=(\kappa-1)A_{2}-(\kappa-2)I_{d}$ .
Thus one matrix–vector reply for either matrix determines
the corresponding reply for the other.
The proof compares $\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\langle x,Oe_{i}\rangle^{2}$ with $\|x\|^{2}$ , where $n=2q+1$ .
For the output of a $q$ -query algorithm, the ratio is at most $d^{-\Omega(1)}$ in absolute value
(Lemma 4.4), whereas under the target the ratio of the means has absolute value
$e^{-\Theta(n/\sqrt{\kappa})}$ (Lemma 4.5).
Hence the test works for $n$ up to order $\sqrt{\kappa}\log d$ , rather than $\log d$ as at
$\kappa=2$ , which is the source of the factor $\sqrt{\kappa}$ in
Theorem 4.1.

4.3 Proof of Theorem 4.1
[S4.SS3.p1; HTML line 1323]
[S4.SS3.p1.1; HTML line 1324]
We compare the probabilities of a single test event
under algorithmic outputs and target samples.
[S4.SS3.2; HTML line 1327]
Proof of Theorem 4.1.
[S4.SS3.p2; HTML line 1329]
[S4.SS3.p2.1; HTML line 1330]
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ , $2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}$ ,
and $0<\varepsilon\leq 1/10$ .
Suppose that an algorithm uses at most
$q\leq(\delta/256)\sqrt{\kappa}\log d$ queries and satisfies
$\operatorname{TV}(\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}},\pi_{A})\leq\varepsilon$
for every admissible $A$ .
Lemma 4.6 makes the hard family
admissible.
[S4.SS3.p3; HTML line 1339]
[S4.SS3.p3.1; HTML line 1340]
Set $n=2q+1$ and $\tau=\tau_{d,q}$ .
Define statistic $S_{A}$ and the associated
test event $E_{A}$ as
[S4.EGx2; HTML line 1343]
$\displaystyle S_{A}(x)$
$\displaystyle:=(-1)^{n}x^{\top}T_{n}\!\left(\frac{2A-(\kappa+1)I_{d}}{\kappa-1}\right)x-2\tau\|x\|^{2},$
$\displaystyle E_{A}$
$\displaystyle:=\{x:S_{A}(x)>0\},$
[S4.SS3.p3.2; HTML line 1356]
where $T_{n}$ is the degree- $n$ Chebyshev polynomial of the
first kind.
[S4.SS3.p4; HTML line 1359]
[S4.SS3.p4.1; HTML line 1360]
Geometrically, for $x\neq 0$ , the weights
$w_{i}(x)=|\langle x,Oe_{i}\rangle|^{2}/\|x\|^{2}$
describe the fractions of its energy $\|x\|^{2}$ carried by the
eigenvectors $Oe_{i}$ of $A$ .
Using $T_{n}(\cos\theta_{i})=\cos(n\theta_{i})$ , the event
$x\in E_{A}$ is equivalent to
$(-1)^{n}\sum_{i}w_{i}(x)\cos(n\theta_{i})>2\tau$ .
So $E_{A}$ is the event that the cosine coefficient
$\sum_{i}w_{i}(x)\cos(n\theta_{i})$ of these weights at frequency $n$
exceeds $2\tau$ in absolute value and has the sign that it
has under the target.
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1; HTML line 1373]
Algorithmic outputs rarely pass the test.
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1; HTML line 1376]
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1; HTML line 1377]
The bound on $q$ gives
$q+1\leq d^{(1-\delta)/2}\log d$ , so $n=o(d)$ .
Substituting this bound into the definition of $\tau$ yields
[S4.Ex20; HTML line 1380]
$\tau\lesssim d^{-\delta/2}(\log d)^{3/2}+d^{-\delta}(\log d)^{3}\lesssim d^{-\delta/2}(\log d)^{3}\longrightarrow 0$
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.2; HTML line 1387]
Thus $n<d$ and $\tau\leq 1/2$ for sufficiently large $d$
depending only on $\delta$ , and the Lemma 4.3 and Lemma 4.4 apply.
Let $X$ be the algorithmic output on the random input
$A=O\Lambda O^{\top}$ .
Lemma 4.3 places $O^{\top}X$ in
$\mathcal{K}_{q}$ on an extension preserving the
joint law of $(O,X)$ .
Lemma 4.4 then gives
[S4.Ex21; HTML line 1395]
$|X^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}X|\leq 2\tau\|X\|^{2}$
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.3; HTML line 1402]
with probability at least $0.99$ .
On this event, $S_{A}(X)\leq 0$ .
Consequently,
[S4.Ex22; HTML line 1405]
$\mathbb{E}_{A}[\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}(E_{A})]\leq 0.01.$
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2; HTML line 1414]
Target samples typically pass the test.
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1; HTML line 1417]
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1.1; HTML line 1418]
Let $M=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^{-1})$ denote the target
mean squared norm, and let
$\mu_{n}=\operatorname{tr}(D_{n}\Lambda^{-1})$ denote the target
mean of the cosine statistic.
Write $\beta=|\mu_{n}|/M$ for the normalized target bias.
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2; HTML line 1424]
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.1; HTML line 1425]
Since $n=o(d)$ and
$\sqrt{\kappa}\leq d^{(1-\delta)/2}=o(d)$ ,
we have $d-n\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ for sufficiently large $d$ .
Lemma 4.5 and the the bound on $q$ therefore give
[S4.Ex23; HTML line 1429]
$\beta\geq(1-e^{-4})e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}\geq b\,d^{-\delta/4},\qquad b=(1-e^{-4})e^{-3},$
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.2; HTML line 1436]
where we used
$3n/\sqrt{\kappa}\leq 3+(3\delta/128)\log d$ .
Moreover,
$\varrho_{\kappa}\leq e^{-1/\sqrt{\kappa}}$ and
$d\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ imply
$\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}\leq e^{-2}<1/3$ .
The formula for $M$ in the same lemma yields
$M\geq d/(2\sqrt{\kappa})\geq d^{(1+\delta)/2}/2$ .
Together with the bound on $\tau$ above, these estimates imply
[S4.Ex24; HTML line 1445]
$\frac{\tau}{\beta}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{b}d^{-\delta/4}(\log d)^{3}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad M\beta^{2}\geq\frac{b^{2}}{2}\sqrt{d}\longrightarrow\infty.$
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.3; HTML line 1452]
All estimates are uniform over the stated parameter range.
We may therefore choose $d_{0}(\delta)$ so that the conditions
for the algorithmic bound above hold and, additionally,
$\tau\leq\beta/4$ and $M\beta^{2}\geq 320$ .
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3; HTML line 1457]
[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3.1; HTML line 1458]
Now fix $A$ , draw $Y\sim\pi_{A}$ , and set $Z=S_{A}(Y)$ .
Lemma 4.5 gives mean
$(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}=\beta M$ for the signed cosine quadratic form

$\mathbb{E}Z=(\beta-2\tau)M\geq\frac{\beta M}{2},\qquad\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq\bigl(\sqrt{2M}+2\tau\sqrt{2M}\bigr)^{2}\leq 8M.$

4.4 Limitations of the Analysis
[S4.SS4.p1; HTML line 1504]
[S4.SS4.p1.1; HTML line 1505]
Theorem 4.1 is restricted to
$2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}$ for fixed $\delta\in(0,1)$ .
Although it applies to every $\varepsilon\leq 1/10$ ,
its lower bound does not grow as $\varepsilon$ decreases.
We discuss two limitations of the analysis that affect
extensions to larger condition numbers and higher accuracy.
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1; HTML line 1512]
Limitation on $\kappa$ .
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p1; HTML line 1515]
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p1.1; HTML line 1516]
Our approach does not extend the $\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d)$ lower bound to condition numbers
$\kappa\gtrsim d/\log^{2}d$ .
Our proof controls the output of the algorithm by first applying
Lemma 4.3 to place $O^{\top}X$ in
$\mathcal{K}_{q}$ , and then bounding the cosine statistic
uniformly over this space.
However, the enlarged space $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ has $(q+1)^{2}$ generating vectors.
Since the eigenvalues of $\Lambda$ are distinct,
these vectors span a space of the following dimension.
[S4.Thmtheorem7; HTML line 1526]
Lemma 4.7 (Dimension of the Krylov space).
[S4.Thmtheorem7.p1; HTML line 1528]
[S4.Thmtheorem7.p1.1; HTML line 1529]
For every integer $q\geq 0$ and independent
$g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\allowbreak\sim N(0,I_{d})$ ,
[S4.Ex28; HTML line 1531]
$\dim\mathcal{K}_{q}=\min\{d,(q+1)^{2}\}\qquad\text{almost surely}.$
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p2; HTML line 1540]
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p2.1; HTML line 1541]
Once $(q+1)^{2}\geq d$ with $q=\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d)$ , we have
$\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathbb{R}^{d}$ almost surely.
For $n=2q+1<d$ , the definition of $\theta_{i}$ gives
$\sum_{i}^{d}\cos^{2}(n\theta_{i})=d/2$ , so at least one coordinate
satisfies $|\cos(n\theta_{i})|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}$ .
Taking its coordinate unit vector yields
[S4.Ex29; HTML line 1547]
$\sup_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|u^{\top}D_{n}u|}{\|u\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p2.2; HTML line 1554]
Our separating test in Lemma 4.4 requires this supremum to be at most
$1/2$ , which is incompatible with the displayed bound.
Thus an argument based on a uniform bound over $\mathcal{K}_{q}$
cannot apply once
$(q+1)^{2}\geq d$ .
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px2; HTML line 1561]
Limitation on accuracy.
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p1; HTML line 1564]
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p1.1; HTML line 1565]
Our test compares the ratio $(-1)^{n}x^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}x/\|x\|^{2}$ with a threshold $t$ .
Lemma 4.4 puts every output of the algorithm below $t$ only if $t\geq a$ , where $a$ is the
uniform bound on $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ that the lemma provides. Chebyshev’s inequality with
Lemma 4.5 puts samples from the target above $t$ only if $t<|\mu_{n}|/M$ . Thus
$|\mu_{n}|/M>a$ , whatever the threshold.
At the same time, the Bernstein calculation in the proof of
Fact C.6
requires $a\geq c/\sqrt{d}$ even to certify a failure
probability below one.
Finally, Lemma 4.5 gives
$|\mu_{n}|/M\leq e^{-2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}$ .
Since $n=2q+1$ , we must have
[S4.Ex30; HTML line 1577]
$\frac{c}{\sqrt{d}}\leq a<\frac{|\mu_{n}|}{M}\leq e^{-2(2q+1)/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$
[S4.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p1.2; HTML line 1584]
Taking logarithms yields $q=O(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d)$ ,
independently of $\varepsilon$ .
A different choice of the threshold or of the constants therefore cannot give a bound that grows as
$\varepsilon\to 0$ .
