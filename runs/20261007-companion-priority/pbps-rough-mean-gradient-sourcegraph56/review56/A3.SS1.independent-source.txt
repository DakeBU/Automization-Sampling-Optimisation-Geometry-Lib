C.1 Proof of Lemma B.1 Proof of Lemma B.1 . We first prove ( B.13 ). It suffices to consider f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , the general case following by density and closedness of the gradient. Recall that the conditional density p_{y} of Y_{-} given Y_{+}=y is p_{y}(u)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V((y+u)/2)-\frac{\|y-u\|^{2}}{8\eta}\Bigr\}\,, and define s_{y}(u)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla V((y+u)/2)-\frac{y-u}{4\eta}\,. Differentiating the normalized conditional density gives \nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathrm{Cov}\bigl(f(Y_{-}),s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr)\,. (C.1) The negative log-density of p_{y} has Hessian \frac{1}{4}\,\bigl\{\nabla^{2}V((y+u)/2)+\eta^{-1}I\bigr\}\succeq\frac{\alpha+\eta^{-1}}{4}\,I\,. Hence, the conditional Poincaré inequality implies, for every unit vector a , \displaystyle\Var\bigl(a^{\top}s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr) \displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\alpha+\eta^{-1}}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla_{u}(a^{\top}s_{y})(Y_{-})\|^{2}\,\bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\bigr]\,. The assumptions \alpha I\preceq\nabla^{2}V\preceq\beta I and \beta\eta\leq 1 give \|\nabla_{u}s_{y}(u)\|_{\rm op}\leq\frac{\eta^{-1}-\alpha}{4}\,. Consequently, \Var\bigl(a^{\top}s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr)\leq\frac{(\eta^{-1}-\alpha)^{2}}{4\,(\alpha+\eta^{-1})}\,. Applying conditional Cauchy–Schwarz in ( C.1 ) and taking the supremum over \|a\|=1 yields \|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)\|^{2}\leq\frac{(\eta^{-1}-\alpha)^{2}}{4\,(\alpha+\eta^{-1})}\Var(f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y)\,. Integrating in y and using ( B.9 ), \displaystyle\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha\eta)^{2}}{4\,(1+\alpha\eta)}\,\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,. (C.2) Finally, \|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}-\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2} , which proves ( B.13 ). We next bound the spectrum of \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} . Let f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0} . Since \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} preserves constants, \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f is centered. The Poincaré inequality for the marginal law \pi_{\eta}^{Y} gives \|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\Var_{\pi_{\eta}^{Y}}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f)\leq\Bigl(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\eta\Bigr)\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,. (C.3) Combining ( C.3 ) with ( C.2 ) and rearranging gives \|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|\leq\frac{1-\alpha\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,\|f\|=\rho_{\rm mac}\,\|f\|\,. (C.4) Since \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} is self-adjoint, this proves -\rho_{\rm mac}I\preceq\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\preceq\rho_{\rm mac}I\,. It remains only to obtain the uniform lower bound \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\succeq-\frac{1}{2}\,I . For every g\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y}) , exchangeability and ( B.8 ) give \displaystyle\bigl\langle g,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})g\bigr\rangle \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(g(Y_{+})-g(Y_{-})\bigr)^{2}\bigr]=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl[\bigl(g(X+\sqrt{\eta}Z)-g(X-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\,. (C.5) For fixed X=x , the Gaussian Poincaré inequality implies \displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl[\bigl(g(x+\sqrt{\eta}Z)-g(x-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr)^{2}\bigr] \displaystyle\leq\eta\,\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl\|\nabla g(x+\sqrt{\eta}Z)+\nabla g(x-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr\|^{2}\,. Averaging in X and using that Y_{+} and Y_{-} have the same marginal gives \bigl\langle g,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})g\bigr\rangle\leq 2\eta\,\|\nabla g\|^{2}\,. (C.6) Applying ( C.6 ) with g=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f and using ( B.13 ), we obtain \displaystyle\bigl\langle f,\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})f\bigr\rangle \displaystyle=\bigl\langle\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\bigr\rangle\leq 2\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\bigl\langle f,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})f\bigr\rangle\,. Thus, as an operator inequality, 2\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\preceq I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2} , or equivalently (I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})^{2}(I+2\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\succeq 0\,. (C.7) Since \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} is a self-adjoint contraction, its spectrum lies in [-1,1] . The scalar inequality corresponding to ( C.7 ) is (1-\lambda)^{2}(1+2\lambda)\geq 0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\,, and therefore \operatorname{Spec}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\subseteq[-1/2,1] . Hence -\frac{1}{2}I\,\preceq\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} . Together with ( C.4 ), this proves ( B.14 ). Finally, \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}\succeq(1-\rho_{\rm mac}^{2})\,I=\frac{4\alpha\eta}{(1+\alpha\eta)^{2}}\,I\,, and therefore \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\succeq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha\eta}}{1+\alpha\eta}\,I=\gamma_{\rm gap}I\,. This proves ( B.15 ). ∎ The half-turn analysis below uses the following alternative form of ( C.1 ). Lemma C.1 (Derivative formula for \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} ) . For every f\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y}) and \pi_{\eta}^{Y} -almost every y , \nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\,. (C.8) Proof. First let f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . The representation ( C.1 ) gives \nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),s_{y}(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y)\,. Using the definition of s_{y} , we write s_{y}(u)=\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V((y+u)/2)-\frac{y-u}{4\eta}\Bigr\}-\nabla V((y+u)/2)\,. To rewrite ( C.1 ) in the claimed form, we only need to identify the covariance generated by the vector in braces. Integration by parts in u against the conditional density p_{y} gives \mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[f(Y_{-})\,\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\Bigr\}\ \Bigm|\ Y_{+}=y\Bigr]\,. (C.9) Applying ( C.9 ) to f\equiv 1 , justified by compactly supported cutoffs converging to 1 , gives \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr]=0\,. Combining this with ( C.9 ), we obtain \displaystyle\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y) \displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\Bigl(f(Y_{-}),\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr)-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y) \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[f(Y_{-})\,\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\Bigr\}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y) \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\,. This is ( C.8 ) for smooth f . The extension to H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y}) is a standard approximation argument once we check that the covariance term is continuous in L^{2} . By ( 2.11 ), the conditional law of X given Y_{+}=y has Poincaré constant at most \eta/(1+\alpha\eta)\leq\eta . Hence, for every unit vector a\in\mathbb{R}^{d} , \Var(a^{\top}\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\leq\eta\,\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla^{2}V(X)\,a\|^{2}\mid Y_{+}=y]\leq\beta^{2}\eta\,. Conditional Cauchy–Schwarz and ( B.9 ) therefore imply \bigl\|\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+})\bigr\|_{L^{2}}\leq\beta\sqrt{\eta}\,\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|\,, and the same bound applies to differences. Conditional expectation is an L^{2} contraction, and ( B.13 ) controls the left side. Thus every term in ( C.8 ) is continuous under H^{1} approximation. Density of C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y}) completes the proof. ∎
