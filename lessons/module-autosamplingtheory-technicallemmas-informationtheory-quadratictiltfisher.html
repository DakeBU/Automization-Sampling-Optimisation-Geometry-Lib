    The quadratic fiber score has exact relative Fisher energy

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.InformationTheory.QuadraticTiltFisher.information_quadratic_representative · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let ν be the common measure used in the two quadratic normalizers and let μ be any probability law on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. For η≠0 and centers y,y′, the μ-relative Fisher energy of this explicit normalized quadratic log-ratio representative is exactly η⁻²‖y−y′‖². In the source specialization, ν is the common Gibbs base and μ is its left restricted-Gaussian fiber.

    \[\mathcal I_{\mu}(r_{y,y'})=\int\!\|\nabla r_{y,y'}(x)\|^2\,d\mu(x)=\eta^{-2}\|y-y'\|^2.\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Insert the exact score

      The preceding ASTIS theorem differentiates the displayed quadratic representative genuinely and gives a vector independent of x.

    2. 2Expose the constant integrand

      Substituting the score in the definition of relative Fisher information leaves a spatially constant squared norm.

    3. 3Use probability mass one

      Because μ has total mass one, integrating this nonnegative constant does not introduce a mass factor.

    4. 4Pull the scalar out of the norm

      The norm of a real scalar multiple is its absolute value times the norm. Squaring removes the sign and gives the exact η⁻² coefficient.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • The common normalization measure ν and the probability integration law μ are separate parameters. In the source, μ is the left tilt of ν.
    • The integration law μ is a probability measure; this is what turns the integral of a constant into the same constant.
    • The state space is a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and η is nonzero. The paper's fiber variance satisfies η>0.
    • The argument evaluates the displayed smooth log-ratio representative. It does not differentiate Mathlib's canonical measurable llr representative.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Insert the exact score

    The preceding ASTIS theorem differentiates the displayed quadratic representative genuinely and gives a vector independent of x.

    \[\nabla r_{y,y'}(x)=\eta^{-1}(y-y').\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    TiltedLogRatio.gradient_quadratic_representative

    2. Expose the constant integrand

    Substituting the score in the definition of relative Fisher information leaves a spatially constant squared norm.

    \[\mathcal I_{\mu}(r_{y,y'})=\int\!\|\eta^{-1}(y-y')\|^2\,d\mu(x).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    RelativeFisher.information; RelativeFisher.densityEnergy

    3. Use probability mass one

    Because μ has total mass one, integrating this nonnegative constant does not introduce a mass factor.

    \[\int c\,d\mu=c\,\mu(\mathbb R^d)=c.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    IsProbabilityMeasure μ; MeasureTheory.integral_const

    4. Pull the scalar out of the norm

    The norm of a real scalar multiple is its absolute value times the norm. Squaring removes the sign and gives the exact η⁻² coefficient.

    \[\|\eta^{-1}(y-y')\|^2=|\eta^{-1}|^2\|y-y'\|^2=\eta^{-2}\|y-y'\|^2.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    norm_smul; Real.norm_eq_abs; mul_pow

    Lean statement · information_quadratic_representative

    The function supplied to RelativeFisher.information contains both ν-normalizers explicitly, while the Fisher integral is taken under the separate probability law μ. The normalizers disappear only through the previously proved genuine gradient theorem.

    theorem information_quadratic_representative
    (nu mu : Measure (State (ι := ι))) [IsProbabilityMeasure mu]
    {eta : ℝ} (heta : eta ≠ 0) (y y' : State (ι := ι)) :
    RelativeFisher.information mu (fun _ => 1)
        (fun t =>
          -(‖t - y‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta)) -
            Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (-(‖z - y‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) ∂nu) -
          (-(‖t - y'‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) +
            Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (-(‖z - y'‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) ∂nu)) =
      eta⁻¹ ^ 2 * ‖y - y'‖ ^ 2

    Scope and omitted-condition boundaries
    • This theorem does not identify the Fisher information of an arbitrary canonical llr representative.
    • It does not prove a log-Sobolev inequality, Talagrand inequality, Wasserstein contraction, Markov-kernel contraction, or any SPHMC main theorem.
    • The left law is assumed to be a probability measure and is kept distinct from the common normalization base. Construction and normalization of the actual restricted-Gaussian fiber remain separate adapters.
