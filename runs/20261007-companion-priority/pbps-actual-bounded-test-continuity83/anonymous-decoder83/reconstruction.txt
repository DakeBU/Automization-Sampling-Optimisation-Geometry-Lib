Let E be any finite-dimensional real inner-product space, equipped with its normed additive commutative group structure and its Borel measurable structure. Dimension zero is allowed. Let V:E -> R, let alpha,beta be nonnegative real numbers, and let eta be real. Assume exactly the following six premises: (1) alpha>0; (2) alpha<=beta; (3) V is twice continuously Frechet differentiable; (4) for every x,v in E, alpha*||v||^2 <= (D(DV)(x)[v])[v] <= beta*||v||^2; (5) eta>0; and (6) beta*eta<=1. Here gradients use the real inner product.

The following definitions fix the statement completely. Put Omega=R^N with its countable product measurable structure and P=the countable product of rate-one exponential measures on R. Thus the coordinate variables under P have the product exponential law. For every sample omega in Omega and k in N define e_omega(k)=max(omega(k),0), viewed as a nonnegative real. This definition applies to every real sequence, including zero and negative coordinates.

For y,r in E define c(y,r)=y-eta*grad V(r). For real s and z=(x,v) in E x E define
Phi(y,r,s,(x,v))=(c(y,r)+cos(s)*(x-c(y,r))+sqrt(eta)*sin(s)*v,
                 -sin(s)/sqrt(eta)*(x-c(y,r))+cos(s)*v).
Let d_r(x)=grad V(x)-grad V(r). Define the state update
S(r,(x,v))=(x,v-(2*<v,d_r(x)>/||d_r(x)||^2)*d_r(x)),
and the rate
rate(r,(x,v))=sqrt(eta)*max(0,<v,d_r(x)>).
All these expressions are total Lean expressions; real division by zero is zero, so S(r,(x,v))=(x,v) when d_r(x)=0. Since eta>0, sqrt(eta)>0 in the flow formula.

For t>=0 define the cumulative rate
Lambda(y,r,z,t)=integral from 0 to t of rate(r,Phi(y,r,s,z)) ds.
For threshold e>=0 define tau(y,r,z,e) in [0,infinity] to be the infimum of the nonnegative times u such that Lambda(y,r,z,u)-e>=0. The infimum of an empty hit set is infinity. This is the literal hittingAfter construction in the statement; a waiting time is not stipulated to be strictly positive. In particular zero thresholds and infima at zero are covered by this definition.

A record is either a finite pair (a,z) with a>=0 and z in E x E, or an absorbing stopped symbol. For fixed y,r and a sequence e:N->[0,infinity), define R_0=(0,z0). To update R_k using e(k), keep the stopped symbol stopped. If R_k=(a,z) and tau(y,r,z,e(k))=infinity, put R_(k+1)=stopped. Otherwise put w=tau(y,r,z,e(k)) and
R_(k+1)=(a+w,S(r,Phi(y,r,w,z))).
Formally the finite waiting time is untopD 0(tau), evaluated in the finite branch; untopD 0 has default value zero at infinity. Define T_n to be the accumulated time of R_n when finite and infinity when R_n is stopped. These are precisely the recursively defined record and eventTime of the statement. R_n and T_n depend on y,r,z0,e. The update from n to n+1 uses coordinate n.

There exists a single total function
Z:E -> E -> (E x E) -> [0,infinity) -> Omega -> (E x E)
with all of the following properties simultaneously.

1. Joint measurability. The map (y,r,z0,t,omega) -> Z(y,r,z0,t,omega) is measurable on the displayed product of Borel spaces and the countable product sequence space.

2. Exact trajectory formula on every finite record interval, for every sequence. For every y,r in E, every z0 in E x E, every omega in Omega, every t>=0, every n in N, and every finite record a=(a_time,a_state), if T_n<=t<T_(n+1) and R_n=a for e=e_omega, then
Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)=Phi(y,r,max(t-a_time,0),a_state).
The subtraction is subtraction in the nonnegative reals, hence truncates at zero. Interval endpoints are left closed and right open; all comparisons use extended nonnegative event times, with finite t embedded in that space.

3. Fallback value on every uncovered time, for every sequence. For every y,r,z0,omega and t>=0, if there exists no n with T_n<=t<T_(n+1), then Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)=z0. This clause applies without discarding exceptional input sequences and includes any uncovered time resulting from the literal record construction.

4. Almost-sure path coverage and initialization. For each fixed y,r in E and z0 in E x E, for P-almost every omega, simultaneously for all t>=0 there exist n in N and a finite record a=(a_time,a_state) such that
T_n<=t<T_(n+1), R_n=a, a_time<=t,
max(t-a_time,0)<tau(y,r,a_state,e_omega(n)),
and Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)=Phi(y,r,max(t-a_time,0),a_state).
The residual waiting-time comparison is in the extended nonnegative reals. On that same almost-sure set, Z(y,r,z0,0,omega)=z0. The exceptional null set may depend on y,r,z0; the statement does not put the quantifiers over those parameters inside one common almost-sure event.

5. Probability comparison and continuity at zero. For every fixed y,r,z0 and every t>=0, the event
A_t={omega: Z(y,r,z0,t,omega) != Phi(y,r,t,z0)}
is measurable and
P.real(A_t)<=1-exp(-Lambda(y,r,z0,t)).
Moreover, for every real delta>0, every event
B_(t,delta)={omega: delta<=||Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)-z0||}
is measurable for every t>=0, and P.real(B_(t,delta)) tends to zero as t tends to zero within the nonnegative reals. The norm is the product norm on E x E, and the event uses the non-strict inequality delta<=norm. The limit uses the ordinary nonpunctured neighborhood filter at zero on the nonnegative reals. P.real denotes the real measure value of the event.

6. Bounded continuous test functions. For every y,r in E and z0 in E x E, every continuous real-valued f on E x E, and every real M>=0 satisfying |f(z)|<=M for all z in E x E, the following hold. For every t>=0 the random variable omega -> f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) is measurable and P-integrable, and
|integral f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) dP(omega)-f(Phi(y,r,t,z0))|
  <=2*M*(1-exp(-Lambda(y,r,z0,t))).
As t tends to zero within the nonnegative reals, the integral of f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) under P tends to f(z0). Integrals under P are Bochner integrals. Both f and every admissible bound M are universally quantified after the same single witness Z; the witness is not chosen anew for a test function or bound.

The numerical factors in the displayed formulas and estimates are exactly 1 and 2 as written; there are no additional existential constants, positive lower bounds on dimension or waiting times, or assumed trajectories or selections. All y,r,z0 and all nonnegative times are unrestricted within their displayed domains. The sequencewise clauses and almost-sure clauses have the distinct quantifier scopes recorded above. There is no unresolved semantic ambiguity in the supplied statement and approved definition context.
