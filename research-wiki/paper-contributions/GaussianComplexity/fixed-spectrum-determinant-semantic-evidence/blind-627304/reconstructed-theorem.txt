For every natural number d with d ≥ 2, every real number κ with κ ≥ 2, and every real d by d orthogonal matrix O, make the following definitions. Orthogonality means OOᵀ = OᵀO = I_d; O ranges over the full real orthogonal group, allowing either determinant sign. Index coordinates by the integers i = 0, …, d − 1. For each such i, define θ_i = (i + 1/2)π/d and λ_i = (κ + 1)/2 + ((κ − 1)/2) cos(θ_i). The occurrences of i and d in these formulas are their real casts; 1/2 and every division are real operations. The restriction d ≥ 2 makes the denominator d nonzero.

Let D be the actual d by d diagonal matrix with diagonal entry λ_i in coordinate i and zero off the diagonal, and define the real matrix A = ODOᵀ. Then the genuine real determinant satisfies det(A) = ∏_{i=0}^{d−1} λ_i = ∏_{i=0}^{d−1} [(κ + 1)/2 + ((κ − 1)/2) cos((i + 1/2)π/d)]. Each displayed product is the ordinary full finite product, containing exactly one factor for each of the d coordinates. The angles, the diagonal values, D, and A are defined outputs of the universally chosen inputs d, κ, and O; they are not additional independent inputs or assumed determinant or eigenvalue certificates. All definitions and the equality use the same d, κ, and O.
