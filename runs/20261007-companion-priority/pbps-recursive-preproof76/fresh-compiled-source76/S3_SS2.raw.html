3.2 The ideal conditional half-turn process

Fix Y=yY=y. Recall from (2.8) that the position XX to be sampled has conditional law

πηX|Y=y​(d​x)∝e−Vy​(x)​d​x,Vy​(x)≔V⁡(x)+‖x−y‖22​η.\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-V_{y}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\hskip 20.00003ptV_{y}(x)\coloneqq V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\,.

We construct the half-turn process so that its induced transition preserves this law. The construction starts from vanilla BPS for the conditional target, absorbs the quadratic coupling potential into the deterministic motion, and then introduces an independent conditional reference point to localize the remaining bounce rate. We begin by introducing a Gaussian momentum p∼𝒩⁡(0,I)p\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I). The phase space law to be preserved is

νη,y​(d​x​d​p)≔πηX|Y=y​(d​x)​𝒩​(0,I)​(d​p)∝exp⁡{−Vy​(x)−‖p‖22}​d​x​d​p.\nu_{\eta,y}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p)\coloneqq\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)(\mathrm{d}p)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V_{y}(x)-\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p\,. (3.1)

Recall from (2.4) that RhR_{h} denotes specular reflection across the hyperplane orthogonal to hh.

A natural first attempt is to apply vanilla BPS directly to this conditional distribution. Its infinitesimal generator acts on a smooth test function ff as

(𝖫yvanilla​f)​(x,p)=η​p⊤​∇xf​(x,p)+η[p⊤∇Vy(x)]+{f(x,R∇Vy​(x)p)−f(x,p)}.\begin{split}(\mathsf{L}_{y}^{\rm vanilla}f)(x,p)&=\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)\\ &\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}\nabla V_{y}(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{\nabla V_{y}(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split} (3.2)

The common factor η\sqrt{\eta} only fixes the unit of time; it does not change the invariant law. We choose this unit so that the harmonic motion introduced below has period 2​π2\pi. The first term gives straight-line motion, while the second reflects the momentum against the full conditional gradient. This process is exact, but it can be expensive: even when V=0V=0, the quadratic coupling potential ∥⋅−y∥2/(2η)\|{\cdot}-y\|^{2}/(2\eta) produces a stationary mean bounce rate of order d\sqrt{d}.

To reduce the number of bounce events, we put the quadratic part of the conditional gradient into the deterministic dynamics. This gives the generator

(𝖫yharm​f)​(x,p)=η​p⊤​∇xf​(x,p)−(x−y)⊤η​∇pf​(x,p)+η[p⊤∇V(x)]+{f(x,R∇V​(x)p)−f(x,p)}.\begin{split}(\mathsf{L}_{y}^{\rm harm}f)(x,p)&=\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)-\frac{(x-y)^{\top}}{\sqrt{\eta}}\nabla_{p}f(x,p)\\ &\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}\nabla V(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{\nabla V(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split} (3.3)

The first line is the Hamiltonian flow associated with the quadratic coupling potential: between bounces, it rotates (x−y)/η(x-y)/\sqrt{\eta} and pp. This use of a quadratic reference flow, with the remaining force handled by bounce events, is analogous to the Boomerang sampler [4]. The direct harmonic generator (3.3) still bounces against the full gradient ∇V\nabla V, so its bounce rate can remain large.

To further reduce the bounce rate and the resulting computational cost, we introduce a conditional reference point and use the smoothness of VV to control the local gradient difference. Specifically, draw X~∼πηX|Y=y\widetilde{X}\sim\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y} independently and write the exact decomposition

∇V​(x)=∇V​(X~)+{∇V​(x)−∇V​(X~)}.\nabla V(x)=\nabla V(\widetilde{X})+\{\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{X})\}\,.

Conditionally on X~=x~\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{x}, we incorporate the fixed reference term into the deterministic dynamics and retain only the gradient difference in the BPS jump term. Define the equilibrium position of this deterministic harmonic flow and the residual gradient by

cy,x~≔y−η∇V(x~),hx~(x)≔∇V(x)−∇V(x~).c_{y,\widetilde{x}}\coloneqq y-\eta\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\,,\hskip 20.00003pth_{\widetilde{x}}(x)\coloneqq\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\,. (3.4)

The resulting generator is

𝖫y,x~​f​(x,p)≔η​p⊤​∇xf​(x,p)−(x−cy,x~)⊤η​∇pf​(x,p)+η​[p⊤​hx~​(x)]+​{f⁡(x,Rhx~​(x)​p)−f⁡(x,p)}.\begin{split}\mathsf{L}_{y,\widetilde{x}}f(x,p)&\coloneqq\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)-\frac{(x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})^{\top}}{\sqrt{\eta}}\nabla_{p}f(x,p)\\ &\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split} (3.5)

The harmonic and residual terms sum to the full conditional force η∇Vy(x)\sqrt{\eta}\,\nabla V_{y}(x). Thus x~\widetilde{x} changes only how this force is divided between deterministic motion and bounces. By the β\beta-smoothness of VV,

‖hx~​(x)‖=‖∇V​(x)−∇V​(x~)‖≤β​‖x−x~‖.\|h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)\|=\|\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\|\leq\beta\,\|x-\widetilde{x}\|\,.

Drawing the reference point from the same localized conditional law keeps the residual gradient small and reduces the expected number of bounces per run of the half-turn process to order β​η​d\beta\eta\sqrt{d}; see Proposition 3.3.

Having specified the dynamics, it remains to choose the integration horizon. For fixed (y,x~)(y,\widetilde{x}), the deterministic part of (3.5) is harmonic motion about its equilibrium position cy,x~c_{y,\widetilde{x}}. In the absence of bounces, its position at time tt satisfies

xt−cy,x~=(x0−cy,x~)​cos⁡t+η​p0​sin⁡t.x_{t}-c_{y,\widetilde{x}}=(x_{0}-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})\cos t+\sqrt{\eta}\,p_{0}\sin t\,. (3.6)

As discussed earlier, we choose the integration horizon t=πt=\pi, corresponding to one half-period. This is the first non-zero harmonic time at which the position xπx_{\pi} no longer depends on the initial momentum p0p_{0}. Without bounces, the resulting map is

(xπ,pπ)=(2​cy,x~−x0,−p0).(x_{\pi},p_{\pi})=(2c_{y,\widetilde{x}}-x_{0},-p_{0})\,. (3.7)

Thus, the position is reflected through the equilibrium position cy,x~c_{y,\widetilde{x}} and retains its dependence on x0x_{0}. Bounce events correct for the residual force along the trajectory, while the fixed horizon t=πt=\pi retains the memory-preserving structure of the half-turn process.

The construction is now complete. The following algorithm gives its pathwise prescription: it draws the reference point and momentum, evolves the process with generator (3.5) for time π\pi, and returns only the final position.

Proposition 3.1 (Well-posedness and stationarity of the conditional phase space process).

For every fixed y,x~∈ℝdy,\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and initial phase space point (x0,p0)(x_{0},p_{0}), the event-driven dynamics in Algorithm 1, with the reference point fixed at x~\widetilde{x}, defines a unique non-explosive, time-homogeneous Markov process. The phase space measure νη,y\nu_{\eta,y} is stationary for this process. In particular, the returned position xπx_{\pi} is well-defined almost surely.

The well-posedness and stationarity assertions are proved in Section A.1.
