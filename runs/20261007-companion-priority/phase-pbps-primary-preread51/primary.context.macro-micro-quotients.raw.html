ℌ𝖯≔ran⁡(𝖯)={f∈ℌ:f⁡(x,y)=g⁡(y)​ for some ​g∈L2​(πηY)},ℌ⟂≔ker⁡(𝖯)=ran⁡(𝖯⟂)={f∈ℌ:𝔼⁡[f⁡(X,Y)∣Y]=0}.\begin{split}\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:f(x,y)=g(y)\text{ for some }g\in L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})\}\,,\\ \mathfrak{H}_{\perp}&\coloneqq\ker(\mathsf{P})=\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P}^{\perp})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:\mathbb{E}[f(X,Y)\mid Y]=0\}\,.\end{split} (B.2)

Here 𝖯⟂≔I−𝖯\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\coloneqq I-\mathsf{P}, so ℌ=ℌ𝖯⊕ℌ⟂\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp} and f=𝖯​f+𝖯⟂​ff=\mathsf{P}f+\mathsf{P}^{\perp}f. In the common terminology of hypocoercivity [14, 21], ran⁡(𝖯)\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P}) is the macroscopic subspace and its orthogonal complement is the microscopic subspace.
