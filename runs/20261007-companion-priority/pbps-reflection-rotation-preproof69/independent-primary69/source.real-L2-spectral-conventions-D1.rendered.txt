D.1 Hilbert space and Markov operator conventions
Definition D.1 (Hilbert space notation).
Let  [A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m1: \zeta]  be a probability measure. The real Hilbert space
[A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m2: L^{2}(\zeta)]  consists of equivalence classes of measurable functions
[A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m3: f]  such that  [A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m4: \int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<\infty] . Its inner product and
norm are
[A4.E1.m1: \langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\int fg\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|f\|_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}^{1/2}\,.]
(D.1)
We write
[A4.E2.m1: L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)\coloneqq\Bigl\{f\in L^{2}(\zeta):\int f\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=0\Bigr\}\,.]
(D.2)
When  [A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m5: \zeta=\pi_{\eta}] , we abbreviate the inner product and norm by
[A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m6: \langle f,g\rangle]  and  [A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m7: \|f\|] .
Two functions equal  [A4.SS1.p1.m1: \zeta] -almost everywhere represent the same
vector. A closed linear subspace  [A4.SS1.p1.m2: \mathcal{M}\subseteq L^{2}(\zeta)]
has orthogonal complement
[A4.SS1.p1.m3: \mathcal{M}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{g:\langle f,g\rangle=0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{M}\}] .
Its orthogonal projection is the unique bounded operator
[A4.SS1.p1.m4: \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}]  for which  [A4.SS1.p1.m5: \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}]  and
[A4.SS1.p1.m6: f-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}] .
All operators in the convergence analysis are bounded linear operators
on the indicated Hilbert spaces unless explicitly stated otherwise.
The adjoint  [A4.SS1.p2.m1: \mathsf{L}^{*}]  is characterized by
[A4.SS1.p2.m2: \langle\mathsf{L}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathsf{L}^{*}g\rangle] .
An operator is self-adjoint if  [A4.SS1.p2.m3: \mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}^{*}] , a contraction
if  [A4.SS1.p2.m4: \|\mathsf{L}f\|\leq\|f\|] , and unitary if it is onto and preserves
norms. A self-adjoint involution, meaning  [A4.SS1.p2.m5: \mathsf{L}^{2}=I] , is
unitary. For self-adjoint operators  [A4.SS1.p2.m6: \mathsf{L}_{1},\mathsf{L}_{2}] ,
[A4.E3.m1: \mathsf{L}_{1}\preceq\mathsf{L}_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\langle f,(\mathsf{L}_{2}-\mathsf{L}_{1})f\rangle\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }f]
(D.3)
is the Loewner order. If  [A4.SS1.p2.m7: \mathsf{L}]  is self-adjoint, the
spectral theorem defines  [A4.SS1.p2.m8: \psi(\mathsf{L})]  for bounded Borel functions
[A4.SS1.p2.m9: \psi] ; in particular  [A4.SS1.p2.m10: \mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathsf{L})]  is the orthogonal
projection onto the spectral set  [A4.SS1.p2.m11: E] , and a non-negative operator has
a unique non-negative square root.
Definition D.2 (Markov operators and density evolution).
Let  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m1: \mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})]  be a Markov kernel preserving  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m2: \zeta] . Its
Markov operator on observables is
[A4.E4.m1: (\mathsf{K}f)(z)\coloneqq\int f(z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})\,.]
(D.4)
It is a contraction on  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m3: L^{2}(\zeta)] . Its adjoint
[A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m4: \mathsf{K}^{*}]  governs density evolution: if a law has density  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m5: h]  with
respect to  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m6: \zeta] , then its density after one step is  [A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m7: \mathsf{K}^{*}h] .
Constants are fixed by both operators.
If  [A4.SS1.p3.m1: P\ll\zeta]  has density  [A4.SS1.p3.m2: h] , then
[A4.E5.m1: \chi^{2}(P\|\zeta)=\|h-1\|_{\zeta}^{2}\,.]
(D.5)
Consequently, an operator norm bound for  [A4.SS1.p3.m3: \mathsf{K}]  on
[A4.SS1.p3.m4: L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)]  gives the same bound for its adjoint
[A4.SS1.p3.m5: \mathsf{K}^{*}] , and hence gives  [A4.SS1.p3.m6: \chi^{2}]  contraction.
