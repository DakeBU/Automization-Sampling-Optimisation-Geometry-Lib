4.2 Properties of the Hard Family This subsection states the four lemmas used in the proof of Theorem 4.1 .
Lemma 4.3 places the output of any $q$ -query algorithm in a block Krylov space generated by independent
Gaussian vectors,
Lemma 4.4 bounds a quadratic form with cosine weights
uniformly over this space,
Lemma 4.5 evaluates the same quadratic form under the target, and
Lemma 4.6 verifies that the hard family is admissible.
All proofs are deferred to Appendix C . Krylov representation of the output. We first control the information revealed by adaptive queries via a standard Krylov argument.
The deferred-rotation argument of [ 22 , Lemma 9.2] uses the rotational symmetry
of the undisclosed eigenbasis to represent the algorithm’s
output in a block Krylov space generated by
independent Gaussian vectors. For vectors $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})$ , define $\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda^{j}g_{a}:1\leq a\leq q+1,\ 0\leq j\leq q\}.$ Lemma 4.3 ( Krylov representation). Fix an integer $q\geq 0$ with $2q+1<d$ and let $X$ be the output. There exist independent vectors $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\sim N(0,I_{d})$ such that $O^{\top}X\in\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})\qquad\text{almost surely}.$ The additional starting vector accounts for the
output, which may lie outside the span of the query points
and oracle replies.
Equivalently, the lemma gives a representation $O^{\top}X=\sum_{a=1}^{q+1}p_{a}(\Lambda)g_{a}$ ,
where each $p_{a}$ has degree at most $q$ .
The polynomial coefficients may depend on the entire
extended experiment.
We therefore control the output through an estimate that
holds simultaneously for every vector in $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ .
The proof of Lemma 4.3 is deferred to
Appendix C.1 . A uniform bound on the Krylov space. We next control a high-frequency cosine statistic uniformly over
the space in Lemma 4.3 .
For a query budget $q$ , set $n=2q+1,\qquad D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\cos(n\theta_{1}),\ldots,\cos(n\theta_{d})\bigr).$ The choice of $n$ places this statistic above all frequencies
arising from products of two degree- $q$ cosine polynomials. Lemma 4.4 ( Uniform bound on the Krylov space). There is an absolute constant $C>0$ such that the following holds.
Let $q\geq 0$ satisfy $2q+1<d$ , let $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}$ be independent $N(0,I_{d})$ vectors, and let $\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})$ .
Define $\tau_{d,q}=C\left[(q+1)\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+\frac{(q+1)(q+1+\log d)\log d}{d}\right].$ If $\tau_{d,q}\leq 1/2$ , then, with probability at least $0.99$ , $|u^{\top}D_{n}u|\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}.$ The proof is deferred to
Appendix C.2 .
Since $\lambda_{i}$ is a nonconstant affine function of $\cos\theta_{i}$ , the space $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ is equivalently spanned
by the vectors $\bigl((g_{a})_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\bigr)_{i=1}^{d}$ ,
with $a\leq q+1$ and $j\leq q$ .
Consequently, the estimate is independent of $\kappa$ . On the event in Lemma 4.4 ,
the output representation in Lemma 4.3 therefore gives $\bigl|X^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}X\bigr|\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|X\|^{2}.$ Target moments. We now evaluate the same cosine statistic under the target Gaussian. Lemma 4.5 ( Moments under the target). Let $\kappa\geq 2$ , $U\sim N(0,\Lambda^{-1})$ , and $\varrho_{\kappa}=\tfrac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}$ .
For an integer $1\leq n<d$ , put $D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}(\cos(n\theta_{i}))$ , $M=\mathbb{E}\|U\|^{2}$ , and $\mu_{n}=\mathbb{E}[U^{\top}D_{n}U]$ .
Then $\displaystyle M$ $\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\frac{1-\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}}{1+\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}},\quad\mbox{and }(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}=\frac{d}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\frac{\varrho_{\kappa}^{n}-\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d-n}}{1+\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}}.$ Moreover, both $\operatorname{Var}(\|U\|^{2})$ and $\operatorname{Var}(U^{\top}D_{n}U)$ are at most $2M\leq 2d/\sqrt{\kappa}$ . If $d-n\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ , then $(1-e^{-4})e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}\leq\frac{|\mu_{n}|}{M}\leq e^{-2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$ The proof is deferred to
Appendix C.3 .
For an exact sample $Y\mid O\sim\pi_{A}$ , the vector $U=O^{\top}Y$ has law $N(0,\Lambda^{-1})$ , with $\|Y\|^{2}=\|U\|^{2}$ and $Y^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}Y=U^{\top}D_{n}U$ .
