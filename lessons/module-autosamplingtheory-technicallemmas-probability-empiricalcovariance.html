    The empirical second moment is an average of rank-one operators

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.empiricalSecondMoment · def · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    For a finite sample x_i in a real inner-product space, the empirical second-moment operator is the inverse sample size times the sum of y ↦ ⟪x_i,y⟫x_i. The empty-sample convention is zero; concentration consumers use a nonempty index type.

    \[\widehat\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}x_i\otimes x_i\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Turn each vector into a rank-one operator

      The Mathlib rank-one operator sends y to the inner product with x_i multiplied by x_i.

    2. 2Average the finite family

      Sum the continuous linear maps and scale by the inverse real cardinality.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • E is a real inner-product space and I is a finite type.
    • The sample is an arbitrary function I→E.
    • No probability law, independence, centering, or boundedness is assumed by the definition.

    Construction and meaning

    1. Turn each vector into a rank-one operator

    The Mathlib rank-one operator sends y to the inner product with x_i multiplied by x_i.

    \[(x_i\otimes x_i)y=\langle x_i,y\rangle x_i\]

    Sum the continuous linear maps and scale by the inverse real cardinality.

    \[\widehat\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_i x_i\otimes x_i\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Continuous linear maps carry finite sums and real scalar multiplication.

      A self outer product has operator norm equal to the squared vector norm

      AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.norm_rankOne_self_eq_sq · theorem · Teaching coverage

      Read this first

      Mathematical statement

      For every vector x in a real inner-product space, the continuous rank-one operator y ↦ ⟪x,y⟫x has operator norm exactly ‖x‖².

      \[\|x\otimes x\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|x\|^2\]

      Proof roadmap

      1. 1Use the rank-one norm identity

        Mathlib computes the norm of u⊗v as ‖u‖‖v‖.

      2. 2Rewrite the product as a square

        The identical norm factors are exactly ‖x‖².

      Full assumptions and notation

      All objects and hypotheses

      • E is a real inner-product space.
      • x is arbitrary; finite dimensionality and completeness are not assumed.

      Mathematical proof

      1. Use the rank-one norm identity

      Mathlib computes the norm of u⊗v as ‖u‖‖v‖.

      \[\|x\otimes x\|=\|x\|\,\|x\|\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      Simplify InnerProductSpace.rankOne with its norm theorem.

      2. Rewrite the product as a square

      The identical norm factors are exactly ‖x‖².

      \[\|x\|\,\|x\|=\|x\|^2\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      simp [pow_two] closes the equality.

        Samplinglib mathematical lesson

        Empirical covariance is reduced to bounded symmetric centered summands

        AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.empiricalSecondMoment_preconcentration · theorem · Teaching coverage

        Read this first

        Mathematical statement

        For a nonempty finite sample with ‖x_i‖≤B, a symmetric population operator Σ with ‖Σ‖≤B², and B≥0, the empirical error is the average of x_i⊗x_i−Σ; every summand is symmetric and has operator norm at most 2B².

        \[\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_i(x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma),\qquad (x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma)^*=x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma,\qquad \|x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\|\le2B^2\]

        Proof roadmap

        1. 1Expand the centered finite sum

          Distribute the finite sum over subtraction. Nonemptiness makes the sample cardinality nonzero, so inverse scaling cancels the repeated population term.

        2. 2Preserve symmetry under centering

          Each x_i⊗x_i is symmetric, and the difference of symmetric operators is symmetric.

        3. 3Bound each centered summand

          The triangle inequality, the exact rank-one norm, ‖x_i‖≤B, and ‖Σ‖≤B² yield the standard 2B² bound.

        Full assumptions and notation

        All objects and hypotheses

        • E is a real inner-product space and I is a nonempty finite type.
        • B is nonnegative and every sample vector has norm at most B.
        • The supplied population continuous linear operator is symmetric and has norm at most B².
        • No independence, expectation, variance proxy, tail theorem, or dimension bound is assumed.

        Mathematical proof

        1. Expand the centered finite sum

        Distribute the finite sum over subtraction. Nonemptiness makes the sample cardinality nonzero, so inverse scaling cancels the repeated population term.

        \[|I|^{-1}\sum_i(A_i-\Sigma)=|I|^{-1}\sum_iA_i-\Sigma\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use Finset.sum_sub_distrib, Finset.sum_const, and inv_smul_smul₀.

        2. Preserve symmetry under centering

        Each x_i⊗x_i is symmetric, and the difference of symmetric operators is symmetric.

        \[(x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma)^*=x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Combine isSymmetric_rankOne_self with LinearMap.IsSymmetric.sub.

        3. Bound each centered summand

        The triangle inequality, the exact rank-one norm, ‖x_i‖≤B, and ‖Σ‖≤B² yield the standard 2B² bound.

        \[\|x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\|\le\|x_i\|^2+\|\Sigma\|\le2B^2\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use norm_sub_le, norm_rankOne_self_eq_sq, monotonicity of squaring on nonnegative reals, and ring normalization.

        theorem empiricalSecondMoment_preconcentration
    [Nonempty κ]
    (population : E →L[ℝ] E) (sample : κ → E) (B : ℝ)
    (hB : 0 ≤ B)
    (hpopulationSymmetric : LinearMap.IsSymmetric (population : E →ₗ[ℝ] E))
    (hpopulationNorm : ‖population‖ ≤ B ^ 2)
    (hsampleNorm : ∀ i, ‖sample i‖ ≤ B) :
    (empiricalSecondMoment sample - population =
      (Fintype.card κ : ℝ)⁻¹ •
        ∑ i, (InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i) - population)) ∧
    (∀ i, LinearMap.IsSymmetric
      ((InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i) - population : E →L[ℝ] E) :
        E →ₗ[ℝ] E)) ∧
    (∀ i,
      ‖InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i) - population‖ ≤
        2 * B ^ 2) := by
  have hcard : (Fintype.card κ : ℝ) ≠ 0 := by
    exact_mod_cast Fintype.card_ne_zero
  constructor
  · rw [Finset.sum_sub_distrib]
    unfold empiricalSecondMoment
    rw [smul_sub]
    congr 1
    rw [Finset.sum_const, ← Nat.cast_smul_eq_nsmul ℝ]
    exact (inv_smul_smul₀ hcard population).symm
  constructor
  · intro i
    exact (InnerProductSpace.isSymmetric_rankOne_self (sample i)).sub
      hpopulationSymmetric
  · intro i
    have hsample_sq : ‖sample i‖ ^ 2 ≤ B ^ 2 :=
      (sq_le_sq₀ (norm_nonneg (sample i)) hB).2 (hsampleNorm i)
    calc
      ‖InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i) - population‖
          ≤ ‖InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i)‖ + ‖population‖ :=
        norm_sub_le _ _
      _ = ‖sample i‖ ^ 2 + ‖population‖ := by
        rw [norm_rankOne_self_eq_sq]
      _ ≤ B ^ 2 + B ^ 2 := add_le_add hsample_sq hpopulationNorm
      _ = 2 * B ^ 2 := by ring
