    Ordinary-Wasserstein control gives an SPHMC phase-state moment

    AutoSamplingTheory.ExampleCases.SmoothedPicardHMC.PhaseStateMoment.phase_state_second_moment_of_wasserstein · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    The incoming phase energy is integrable and at most 2(Mx+Mp)+4r².

    \[\mathbb E_\nu[\|X-x_\star\|^2+\|P\|^2]\le2(M_X+M_P)+4r^2.\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Position is a one-Lipschitz observable

      For the max product metric, projecting to position and subtracting xstar cannot increase distance.

    2. 2Momentum is a one-Lipschitz observable

      The second product projection is one-Lipschitz for the same metric.

    3. 3Transfer both moments

      Apply the shared theorem independently to position and momentum, preserving both integrability conclusions.

    4. 4Add only after integrability

      Bochner integral additivity is now legal; adding the bounds gives the combined phase energy.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • The reference phase law has integrable position and momentum squares, bounded respectively by Mx and Mp.
    • The incoming law is within ordinary squared W2 radius r² of the reference law.
    • The state space is finite-dimensional real inner-product Borel; completeness is not needed by this theorem.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Position is a one-Lipschitz observable

    For the max product metric, projecting to position and subtracting xstar cannot increase distance.

    \[g_X(x,p)=x-x_\star,\quad\operatorname{Lip}(g_X)\le1.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    LipschitzWith.of_dist_le_mul and Prod.dist_eq.

    2. Momentum is a one-Lipschitz observable

    The second product projection is one-Lipschitz for the same metric.

    \[g_P(x,p)=p,\quad\operatorname{Lip}(g_P)\le1.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    LipschitzWith.prod_snd.

    3. Transfer both moments

    Apply the shared theorem independently to position and momentum, preserving both integrability conclusions.

    \[I_X\le2M_X+2r^2,\qquad I_P\le2M_P+2r^2.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Two calls to of_wassersteinDistance_sq_le.

    4. Add only after integrability

    Bochner integral additivity is now legal; adding the bounds gives the combined phase energy.

    \[I_X+I_P\le2(M_X+M_P)+4r^2.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Integrable.add, integral_add, and linear arithmetic.

    Instantiate the shared observable theorem with the two phase projections and add the resulting integrable moments.

    Braces mark parameters Lean can infer; square brackets request structures such as a measurable space or probability measure. Named hypotheses are mathematical premises, not facts established by this declaration.     theorem phase_state_second_moment_of_wasserstein
    (nu reference : Measure (E × E)) (xstar : E) {r Mx Mp : ℝ}
    (hX : Integrable (fun z : E × E => ‖z.1 - xstar‖ ^ 2) reference)
    (hP : Integrable (fun z : E × E => ‖z.2‖ ^ 2) reference)
    (hXbound : (∫ z : E × E, ‖z.1 - xstar‖ ^ 2 ∂reference) ≤ Mx)
    (hPbound : (∫ z : E × E, ‖z.2‖ ^ 2 ∂reference) ≤ Mp)
    (hW : WassersteinSpace.wassersteinDistance nu reference ^ 2 ≤
      ENNReal.ofReal (r ^ 2)) :
    Integrable (fun z : E × E => ‖z.1 - xstar‖ ^ 2 + ‖z.2‖ ^ 2) nu ∧
      (∫ z : E × E, ‖z.1 - xstar‖ ^ 2 + ‖z.2‖ ^ 2 ∂nu) ≤
        2 * (Mx + Mp) + 4 * r ^ 2 := by
  have hXlip : LipschitzWith 1 (fun z : E × E => z.1 - xstar) := by
    refine LipschitzWith.of_dist_le_mul ?_
    intro a b
    rw [NNReal.coe_one, one_mul, Prod.dist_eq]
    simpa only [dist_eq_norm, sub_sub_sub_cancel_right] using
      (le_max_left (dist a.1 b.1) (dist a.2 b.2))
  have hPlip : LipschitzWith 1 (fun z : E × E => z.2) :=
    LipschitzWith.prod_snd
  have hXt := WassersteinLipschitzMoment.of_wassersteinDistance_sq_le hXlip hX hW
  have hPt := WassersteinLipschitzMoment.of_wassersteinDistance_sq_le hPlip hP hW
  refine ⟨hXt.1.add hPt.1, ?_⟩
  rw [integral_add hXt.1 hPt.1]
  norm_num at hXt hPt
  linarith

end
