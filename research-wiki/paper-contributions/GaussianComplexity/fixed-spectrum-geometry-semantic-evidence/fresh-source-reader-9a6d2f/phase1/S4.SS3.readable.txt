[[S4.SS3]]

4.3 Proof of Theorem 4.1

[[S4.SS3.p1]]

[[S4.SS3.p1.1]] We compare the probabilities of a single test event under algorithmic outputs and target samples.

[[S4.SS3.2]]

Proof of Theorem 4.1.

[[S4.SS3.p2]]

[[S4.SS3.p2.1]] Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ , $2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta}$ , and $0<\varepsilon\leq 1/10$ . Suppose that an algorithm uses at most $q\leq(\delta/256)\sqrt{\kappa}\log d$ queries and satisfies $\operatorname{TV}(\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}},\pi_{A})\leq\varepsilon$ for every admissible $A$ . Lemma 4.6 makes the hard family admissible.

[[S4.SS3.p3]]

[[S4.SS3.p3.1]] Set $n=2q+1$ and $\tau=\tau_{d,q}$ . Define statistic $S_{A}$ and the associated test event $E_{A}$ as

[[S4.EGx2]]

$\displaystyle S_{A}(x)$ $\displaystyle:=(-1)^{n}x^{\top}T_{n}\!\left(\frac{2A-(\kappa+1)I_{d}}{\kappa-1}\right)x-2\tau\|x\|^{2},$

$\displaystyle E_{A}$ $\displaystyle:=\{x:S_{A}(x)>0\},$

[[S4.SS3.p3.2]] where $T_{n}$ is the degree- $n$ Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.

[[S4.SS3.p4]]

[[S4.SS3.p4.1]] Geometrically, for $x\neq 0$ , the weights $w_{i}(x)=|\langle x,Oe_{i}\rangle|^{2}/\|x\|^{2}$ describe the fractions of its energy $\|x\|^{2}$ carried by the eigenvectors $Oe_{i}$ of $A$ . Using $T_{n}(\cos\theta_{i})=\cos(n\theta_{i})$ , the event $x\in E_{A}$ is equivalent to $(-1)^{n}\sum_{i}w_{i}(x)\cos(n\theta_{i})>2\tau$ . So $E_{A}$ is the event that the cosine coefficient $\sum_{i}w_{i}(x)\cos(n\theta_{i})$ of these weights at frequency $n$ exceeds $2\tau$ in absolute value and has the sign that it has under the target.

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1]]

Algorithmic outputs rarely pass the test.

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1]]

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1]] The bound on $q$ gives $q+1\leq d^{(1-\delta)/2}\log d$ , so $n=o(d)$ . Substituting this bound into the definition of $\tau$ yields

[[S4.Ex20]]

$\tau\lesssim d^{-\delta/2}(\log d)^{3/2}+d^{-\delta}(\log d)^{3}\lesssim d^{-\delta/2}(\log d)^{3}\longrightarrow 0$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.2]] Thus $n<d$ and $\tau\leq 1/2$ for sufficiently large $d$ depending only on $\delta$ , and the Lemma 4.3 and Lemma 4.4 apply. Let $X$ be the algorithmic output on the random input $A=O\Lambda O^{\top}$ . Lemma 4.3 places $O^{\top}X$ in $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ on an extension preserving the joint law of $(O,X)$ . Lemma 4.4 then gives

[[S4.Ex21]]

$|X^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}X|\leq 2\tau\|X\|^{2}$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.3]] with probability at least $0.99$ . On this event, $S_{A}(X)\leq 0$ . Consequently,

[[S4.Ex22]]

$\mathbb{E}_{A}[\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}(E_{A})]\leq 0.01.$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2]]

Target samples typically pass the test.

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1]]

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1.1]] Let $M=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^{-1})$ denote the target mean squared norm, and let $\mu_{n}=\operatorname{tr}(D_{n}\Lambda^{-1})$ denote the target mean of the cosine statistic. Write $\beta=|\mu_{n}|/M$ for the normalized target bias.

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2]]

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.1]] Since $n=o(d)$ and $\sqrt{\kappa}\leq d^{(1-\delta)/2}=o(d)$ , we have $d-n\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ for sufficiently large $d$ . Lemma 4.5 and the the bound on $q$ therefore give

[[S4.Ex23]]

$\beta\geq(1-e^{-4})e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}\geq b\,d^{-\delta/4},\qquad b=(1-e^{-4})e^{-3},$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.2]] where we used $3n/\sqrt{\kappa}\leq 3+(3\delta/128)\log d$ . Moreover, $\varrho_{\kappa}\leq e^{-1/\sqrt{\kappa}}$ and $d\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ imply $\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}\leq e^{-2}<1/3$ . The formula for $M$ in the same lemma yields $M\geq d/(2\sqrt{\kappa})\geq d^{(1+\delta)/2}/2$ . Together with the bound on $\tau$ above, these estimates imply

[[S4.Ex24]]

$\frac{\tau}{\beta}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{b}d^{-\delta/4}(\log d)^{3}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad M\beta^{2}\geq\frac{b^{2}}{2}\sqrt{d}\longrightarrow\infty.$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.3]] All estimates are uniform over the stated parameter range. We may therefore choose $d_{0}(\delta)$ so that the conditions for the algorithmic bound above hold and, additionally, $\tau\leq\beta/4$ and $M\beta^{2}\geq 320$ .

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3]]

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3.1]] Now fix $A$ , draw $Y\sim\pi_{A}$ , and set $Z=S_{A}(Y)$ . Lemma 4.5 gives mean $(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}=\beta M$ for the signed cosine quadratic form and mean $M$ for $\|Y\|^{2}$ . Both variances are at most $2M$ . Hence, using the $L^{2}$ triangle inequality for centered variables,

[[S4.Ex25]]

$\mathbb{E}Z=(\beta-2\tau)M\geq\frac{\beta M}{2},\qquad\operatorname{Var}(Z)\leq\bigl(\sqrt{2M}+2\tau\sqrt{2M}\bigr)^{2}\leq 8M.$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3.2]] Chebyshev’s inequality gives, for every $A$ in the hard family,

[[S4.Ex26]]

$\pi_{A}(E_{A})=\mathbb{P}(Z>0)\geq 1-\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}{(\mathbb{E}Z)^{2}}\geq 1-\frac{32}{M\beta^{2}}\geq 0.9.$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px3]]

Contradiction.

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p1]]

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p1.1]] Finally, the per-input TV guarantee implies $\pi_{A}(E_{A})-\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}(E_{A})\leq\varepsilon$ . Averaging over the random input and using the two probability bounds gives

[[S4.Ex27]]

$0.89\leq\mathbb{E}_{A}\!\left[\pi_{A}(E_{A})-\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}(E_{A})\right]\leq\varepsilon\leq 0.1,$

[[S4.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p1.2]] a contradiction. ∎
