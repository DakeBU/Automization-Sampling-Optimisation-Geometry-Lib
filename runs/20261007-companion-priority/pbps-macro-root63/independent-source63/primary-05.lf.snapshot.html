D.1 Hilbert space and Markov operator conventions

Definition D.1 (Hilbert space notation).

Let ζ\zeta be a probability measure. The real Hilbert space L2​(ζ)L^{2}(\zeta) consists of equivalence classes of measurable functions ff such that ∫f2​𝑑ζ<∞\int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<\infty. Its inner product and norm are

⟨f,g⟩L2​(ζ)≔∫f​g​𝑑ζ,‖f‖L2​(ζ)≔⟨f,f⟩L2​(ζ)1/2.\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\int fg\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|f\|_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}^{1/2}\,. (D.1)

We write

L02​(ζ)≔{f∈L2​(ζ):∫f​𝑑ζ=0}.L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)\coloneqq\Bigl\{f\in L^{2}(\zeta):\int f\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=0\Bigr\}\,. (D.2)

When ζ=πη\zeta=\pi_{\eta}, we abbreviate the inner product and norm by ⟨f,g⟩\langle f,g\rangle and ‖f‖\|f\|.

Two functions equal ζ\zeta-almost everywhere represent the same vector. A closed linear subspace ℳ⊆L2​(ζ)\mathcal{M}\subseteq L^{2}(\zeta) has orthogonal complement ℳ⟂≔{g:⟨f,g⟩=0​ for all ​f∈ℳ}\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{g:\langle f,g\rangle=0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{M}\}. Its orthogonal projection is the unique bounded operator 𝖯ℳ\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}} for which 𝖯ℳ​f∈ℳ\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M} and f−𝖯ℳ​f∈ℳ⟂f-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}.

All operators in the convergence analysis are bounded linear operators on the indicated Hilbert spaces unless explicitly stated otherwise. The adjoint 𝖫∗\mathsf{L}^{*} is characterized by ⟨𝖫​f,g⟩=⟨f,𝖫∗​g⟩\langle\mathsf{L}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathsf{L}^{*}g\rangle. An operator is self-adjoint if 𝖫=𝖫∗\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}^{*}, a contraction if ‖𝖫​f‖≤‖f‖\|\mathsf{L}f\|\leq\|f\|, and unitary if it is onto and preserves norms. A self-adjoint involution, meaning 𝖫2=I\mathsf{L}^{2}=I, is unitary. For self-adjoint operators 𝖫1,𝖫2\mathsf{L}_{1},\mathsf{L}_{2},

𝖫1⪯𝖫2⟺⟨f,(𝖫2−𝖫1)f⟩≥0for every f\mathsf{L}_{1}\preceq\mathsf{L}_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\langle f,(\mathsf{L}_{2}-\mathsf{L}_{1})f\rangle\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }f (D.3)

is the Loewner order. If 𝖫\mathsf{L} is self-adjoint, the spectral theorem defines ψ⁡(𝖫)\psi(\mathsf{L}) for bounded Borel functions ψ\psi; in particular 𝟏E​(𝖫)\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathsf{L}) is the orthogonal projection onto the spectral set EE, and a non-negative operator has a unique non-negative square root.

Definition D.2 (Markov operators and density evolution).

Let 𝒦⁡(z,d​z′)\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}) be a Markov kernel preserving ζ\zeta. Its Markov operator on observables is

(𝖪​f)​(z)≔∫f⁡(z′)​𝒦​(z,d​z′).(\mathsf{K}f)(z)\coloneqq\int f(z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})\,. (D.4)

It is a contraction on L2​(ζ)L^{2}(\zeta). Its adjoint 𝖪∗\mathsf{K}^{*} governs density evolution: if a law has density hh with respect to ζ\zeta, then its density after one step is 𝖪∗​h\mathsf{K}^{*}h. Constants are fixed by both operators.

If P≪ζP\ll\zeta has density hh, then

χ2(P∥ζ)=∥h−1∥ζ2.\chi^{2}(P\|\zeta)=\|h-1\|_{\zeta}^{2}\,. (D.5)

Consequently, an operator norm bound for 𝖪\mathsf{K} on L02​(ζ)L_{0}^{2}(\zeta) gives the same bound for its adjoint 𝖪∗\mathsf{K}^{*}, and hence gives χ2\chi^{2} contraction.
