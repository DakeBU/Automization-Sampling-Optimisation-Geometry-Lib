    The empirical second moment is an average of rank-one operators

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.empiricalSecondMoment · def · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    For a finite sample x_i in a real inner-product space, the empirical second-moment operator is the inverse sample size times the sum of y ↦ ⟪x_i,y⟫x_i. The empty-sample convention is zero; concentration consumers use a nonempty index type.

    \[\widehat\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}x_i\otimes x_i\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Turn each vector into a rank-one operator

      The Mathlib rank-one operator sends y to the inner product with x_i multiplied by x_i.

    2. 2Average the finite family

      Sum the continuous linear maps and scale by the inverse real cardinality.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • E is a real inner-product space and I is a finite type.
    • The sample is an arbitrary function I→E.
    • No probability law, independence, centering, or boundedness is assumed by the definition.

    Construction and meaning

    The Mathlib rank-one operator sends y to the inner product with x_i multiplied by x_i.

    \[(x_i\otimes x_i)y=\langle x_i,y\rangle x_i\]

    Use InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample i) (sample i).

    Sum the continuous linear maps and scale by the inverse real cardinality.

    \[\widehat\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_i x_i\otimes x_i\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Continuous linear maps carry finite sums and real scalar multiplication.

        Samplinglib mathematical lesson

        Empirical covariance is reduced to bounded symmetric centered summands

        AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.empiricalSecondMoment_preconcentration · theorem · Teaching coverage

        Read this first

        Mathematical statement

        For a nonempty finite sample with ‖x_i‖≤B, a symmetric population operator Σ with ‖Σ‖≤B², and B≥0, the empirical error is the average of x_i⊗x_i−Σ; every summand is symmetric and has operator norm at most 2B².

        \[\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma=|I|^{-1}\sum_i(x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma),\qquad (x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma)^*=x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma,\qquad \|x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\|\le2B^2\]

        Proof roadmap

        1. 1Expand the centered finite sum

          Distribute the finite sum over subtraction. Nonemptiness makes the sample cardinality nonzero, so inverse scaling cancels the repeated population term.

        2. 2Preserve symmetry under centering

          Each x_i⊗x_i is symmetric, and the difference of symmetric operators is symmetric.

        3. 3Bound each centered summand

          The triangle inequality, the exact rank-one norm, ‖x_i‖≤B, and ‖Σ‖≤B² yield the standard 2B² bound.

        Full assumptions and notation

        All objects and hypotheses

        • E is a real inner-product space and I is a nonempty finite type.
        • B is nonnegative and every sample vector has norm at most B.
        • The supplied population continuous linear operator is symmetric and has norm at most B².
        • No independence, expectation, variance proxy, tail theorem, or dimension bound is assumed.

        Mathematical proof

        1. Expand the centered finite sum

        Distribute the finite sum over subtraction. Nonemptiness makes the sample cardinality nonzero, so inverse scaling cancels the repeated population term.

        \[|I|^{-1}\sum_i(A_i-\Sigma)=|I|^{-1}\sum_iA_i-\Sigma\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use Finset.sum_sub_distrib, Finset.sum_const, and inv_smul_smul₀.

        2. Preserve symmetry under centering

        Each x_i⊗x_i is symmetric, and the difference of symmetric operators is symmetric.

        \[(x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma)^*=x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Combine isSymmetric_rankOne_self with LinearMap.IsSymmetric.sub.

        3. Bound each centered summand

        The triangle inequality, the exact rank-one norm, ‖x_i‖≤B, and ‖Σ‖≤B² yield the standard 2B² bound.

        \[\|x_i\otimes x_i-\Sigma\|\le\|x_i\|^2+\|\Sigma\|\le2B^2\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use norm_sub_le, norm_rankOne_self_eq_sq, monotonicity of squaring on nonnegative reals, and ring normalization.

        Samplinglib mathematical lesson

        A unit-vector covariance has an exact centered operator second moment

        AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.EmpiricalCovariance.unitRankOneCovariance_momentPackage · theorem · Teaching coverage

        Read this first

        Mathematical statement

        Let X be a unit-vector-valued map such that X⊗X is Bochner integrable, and let C be the Bochner expectation of X⊗X. Then C is self-adjoint, ‖C‖≤1, E[(X⊗X-C)²]=C-C², and ‖C-C²‖≤2‖C‖.

        \[C=\mathbb E[X\otimes X],\quad C^*=C,\quad \|C\|\le1,\quad \mathbb E[(X\otimes X-C)^2]=C-C^2,\quad \|C-C^2\|\le2\|C\|\]

        Proof roadmap

        1. 1Integrate rank-one symmetry

          Every self outer product is symmetric. Evaluation and the real inner product commute with the Bochner integral, so the expectation C is self-adjoint.

        2. 2Bound the population operator

          The Bochner integral norm is at most the integral of the pointwise operator norm. Unit norm makes ‖X⊗X‖=1, and a probability measure gives total mass one.

        3. 3Expand the centered square

          A unit-vector outer product is an idempotent projection. Expanding the noncommutative square and commuting left and right multiplication with integration turns both cross terms into C².

        4. 4Control the variance operator

          Submultiplicativity gives ‖C²‖≤‖C‖². Since ‖C‖≤1, the triangle inequality yields ‖C-C²‖≤2‖C‖.

        Full assumptions and notation

        All objects and hypotheses

        • E is a complete real inner-product space and μ is a probability measure.
        • X has norm exactly one at every point; no almost-everywhere weakening is hidden.
        • The rank-one operator X⊗X is Bochner integrable and its integral is the supplied population operator C.
        • No independent sample family, dimension, matrix exponential, or concentration theorem is assumed.

        Mathematical proof

        1. Integrate rank-one symmetry

        Every self outer product is symmetric. Evaluation and the real inner product commute with the Bochner integral, so the expectation C is self-adjoint.

        \[(X\otimes X)^*=X\otimes X\Longrightarrow C^*=C\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use isSymmetric_rankOne_self, ContinuousLinearMap.integral_apply, integral_inner, and isSelfAdjoint_iff_isSymmetric.

        2. Bound the population operator

        The Bochner integral norm is at most the integral of the pointwise operator norm. Unit norm makes ‖X⊗X‖=1, and a probability measure gives total mass one.

        \[\|C\|\le\mathbb E\|X\otimes X\|=1\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Apply norm_integral_le_of_norm_le_const and norm_rankOne_self_eq_sq.

        3. Expand the centered square

        A unit-vector outer product is an idempotent projection. Expanding the noncommutative square and commuting left and right multiplication with integration turns both cross terms into C².

        \[(X\otimes X-C)^2=X\otimes X-(X\otimes X)C-C(X\otimes X)+C^2\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use isStarProjection_rankOne_self, noncomm_ring, integral_sub/add, and ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_comm.

        4. Control the variance operator

        Submultiplicativity gives ‖C²‖≤‖C‖². Since ‖C‖≤1, the triangle inequality yields ‖C-C²‖≤2‖C‖.

        \[\|C-C^2\|\le\|C\|+\|C\|^2\le2\|C\|\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use norm_sub_le, norm_pow_le', hNorm, and real arithmetic.

            _ = _ := by rw [hinner]

          (hRankOneIntegrable.sub hright |>.sub hleft) hconst
        calc
          (∫ omega,
              InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) -
                InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) * population -

                  InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) * population -
                  population * InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) ∂mu) +
              (∫ _ : Omega, population ^ (2 : ℕ) ∂mu) := by
            simpa only [Pi.add_apply, Pi.sub_apply] using hadd
          _ = _ := by rw [hthree]

            (∫ omega,
              InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) * population ∂mu) =
              (∫ omega,
                InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) ∂mu) *
                population := by

            ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_comm

            (∫ omega,
              population * InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) ∂mu) =
              population *
                (∫ omega,
                  InnerProductSpace.rankOne ℝ (sample omega) (sample omega) ∂mu) := by
          simpa [A, mul_apply_eq_comp] using
            ContinuousLinearMap.integral_comp_comm
              (ContinuousLinearMap.mul ℝ A population) hRankOneIntegrable
        rw [hPopulation, hrightInt, hleftInt]
        simp [hPopulation]
        noncomm_ring
  have hVariance :
      ‖population - population ^ (2 : ℕ)‖ ≤ 2 * ‖population‖ := by
    calc
      ‖population - population ^ (2 : ℕ)‖ ≤
          ‖population‖ + ‖population ^ (2 : ℕ)‖ := norm_sub_le _ _
      _ ≤ ‖population‖ + ‖population‖ ^ (2 : ℕ) := by
        gcongr
        exact norm_pow_le' _ (by norm_num)
      _ ≤ 2 * ‖population‖ := by
        nlinarith [norm_nonneg population]
  exact ⟨hSelfAdjoint, hNorm, hSecondMoment, hVariance⟩
