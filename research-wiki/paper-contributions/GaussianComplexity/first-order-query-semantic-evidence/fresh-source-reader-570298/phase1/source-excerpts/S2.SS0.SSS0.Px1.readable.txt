Targets and oracle. We restrict the target class to centered Gaussians.
This restriction removes the hardness of locating the target and isolates the difficulty of sampling with an unknown covariance.
For an integer d\geq 1 and a parameter \kappa\geq 1 , without loss of generality, we normalize the spectral bounds to 1 and \kappa by rescaling and consider
the precision class \mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa}:=\left\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:A=A^{\top},\quad I_{d}\preceq A\preceq\kappa I_{d}\right\}. An input A\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa} specifies the quadratic
potential f_{A}(x):=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax and the target distribution \pi_{A}:=N(0,A^{-1}) .
At a point x\in\mathbb{R}^{d} , the exact first-order oracle returns \operatorname{FO}_{A}(x):=\bigl(f_{A}(x),\nabla f_{A}(x)\bigr)=\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax,Ax\right). (1) On this class, exact first-order access is equivalent to exact
matrix–vector access.
Indeed, a first-order response contains Ax , while a
matrix–vector response determines both the gradient Ax and the function value \frac{1}{2}\langle x,Ax\rangle .
Thus the exact value-and-gradient, gradient-only, and
matrix–vector query complexities coincide.
