[[S4.SS2]]

4.2 Properties of the Hard Family

[[S4.SS2.p1]]

[[S4.SS2.p1.1]] This subsection states the four lemmas used in the proof of Theorem 4.1. Lemma 4.3 places the output of any $q$ -query algorithm in a block Krylov space generated by independent Gaussian vectors, Lemma 4.4 bounds a quadratic form with cosine weights uniformly over this space, Lemma 4.5 evaluates the same quadratic form under the target, and Lemma 4.6 verifies that the hard family is admissible. All proofs are deferred to Appendix C.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1]]

Krylov representation of the output.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p1]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p1.1]] We first control the information revealed by adaptive queries via a standard Krylov argument. The deferred-rotation argument of [22, Lemma 9.2] uses the rotational symmetry of the undisclosed eigenbasis to represent the algorithm’s output in a block Krylov space generated by independent Gaussian vectors.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p2]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p2.1]] For vectors $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})$ , define

[[S4.Ex10]]

$\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda^{j}g_{a}:1\leq a\leq q+1,\ 0\leq j\leq q\}.$

[[S4.Thmtheorem3]]

Lemma 4.3 (Krylov representation).

[[S4.Thmtheorem3.p1]]

[[S4.Thmtheorem3.p1.1]] Fix an integer $q\geq 0$ with $2q+1<d$ and let $X$ be the output. There exist independent vectors $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\sim N(0,I_{d})$ such that

[[S4.Ex11]]

$O^{\top}X\in\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})\qquad\text{almost surely}.$

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p3]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px1.p3.1]] The additional starting vector accounts for the output, which may lie outside the span of the query points and oracle replies. Equivalently, the lemma gives a representation $O^{\top}X=\sum_{a=1}^{q+1}p_{a}(\Lambda)g_{a}$ , where each $p_{a}$ has degree at most $q$ . The polynomial coefficients may depend on the entire extended experiment. We therefore control the output through an estimate that holds simultaneously for every vector in $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ . The proof of Lemma 4.3 is deferred to Appendix C.1.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2]]

A uniform bound on the Krylov space.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p1]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p1.1]] We next control a high-frequency cosine statistic uniformly over the space in Lemma 4.3. For a query budget $q$ , set

[[S4.Ex12]]

$n=2q+1,\qquad D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\cos(n\theta_{1}),\ldots,\cos(n\theta_{d})\bigr).$

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p1.2]] The choice of $n$ places this statistic above all frequencies arising from products of two degree- $q$ cosine polynomials.

[[S4.Thmtheorem4]]

Lemma 4.4 (Uniform bound on the Krylov space).

[[S4.Thmtheorem4.p1]]

[[S4.Thmtheorem4.p1.1]] There is an absolute constant $C>0$ such that the following holds. Let $q\geq 0$ satisfy $2q+1<d$ , let $g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}$ be independent $N(0,I_{d})$ vectors, and let $\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})$ . Define

[[S4.Ex13]]

$\tau_{d,q}=C\left[(q+1)\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+\frac{(q+1)(q+1+\log d)\log d}{d}\right].$

[[S4.Thmtheorem4.p1.2]] If $\tau_{d,q}\leq 1/2$ , then, with probability at least $0.99$ ,

[[S4.Ex14]]

$|u^{\top}D_{n}u|\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}.$

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p2]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p2.1]] The proof is deferred to Appendix C.2. Since $\lambda_{i}$ is a nonconstant affine function of $\cos\theta_{i}$ , the space $\mathcal{K}_{q}$ is equivalently spanned by the vectors $\bigl((g_{a})_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\bigr)_{i=1}^{d}$ , with $a\leq q+1$ and $j\leq q$ . Consequently, the estimate is independent of $\kappa$ .

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p3]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px2.p3.1]] On the event in Lemma 4.4, the output representation in Lemma 4.3 therefore gives

[[S4.Ex15]]

$\bigl|X^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}X\bigr|\leq 2\tau_{d,q}\|X\|^{2}.$

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px3]]

Target moments.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px3.p1]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px3.p1.1]] We now evaluate the same cosine statistic under the target Gaussian.

[[S4.Thmtheorem5]]

Lemma 4.5 (Moments under the target).

[[S4.Thmtheorem5.p1]]

[[S4.Thmtheorem5.p1.1]] Let $\kappa\geq 2$ , $U\sim N(0,\Lambda^{-1})$ , and $\varrho_{\kappa}=\tfrac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}$ . For an integer $1\leq n<d$ , put $D_{n}=\operatorname{diag}(\cos(n\theta_{i}))$ , $M=\mathbb{E}\|U\|^{2}$ , and $\mu_{n}=\mathbb{E}[U^{\top}D_{n}U]$ . Then

[[S4.EGx1]]

$\displaystyle M$ $\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\frac{1-\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}}{1+\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}},\quad\mbox{and }(-1)^{n}\mu_{n}=\frac{d}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\frac{\varrho_{\kappa}^{n}-\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d-n}}{1+\varrho_{\kappa}^{2d}}.$

[[S4.Thmtheorem5.p1.2]] Moreover, both $\operatorname{Var}(\|U\|^{2})$ and $\operatorname{Var}(U^{\top}D_{n}U)$ are at most $2M\leq 2d/\sqrt{\kappa}$ .

[[S4.Thmtheorem5.p2]]

[[S4.Thmtheorem5.p2.1]] If $d-n\geq\sqrt{\kappa}$ , then

[[S4.Ex17]]

$(1-e^{-4})e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}\leq\frac{|\mu_{n}|}{M}\leq e^{-2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px3.p2]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px3.p2.1]] The proof is deferred to Appendix C.3. For an exact sample $Y\mid O\sim\pi_{A}$ , the vector $U=O^{\top}Y$ has law $N(0,\Lambda^{-1})$ , with $\|Y\|^{2}=\|U\|^{2}$ and $Y^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}Y=U^{\top}D_{n}U$ . The lemma therefore gives the means and variances of $\|Y\|^{2}$ and $Y^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}Y$ under the target.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px4]]

Admissibility and oracle equivalence.

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px4.p1]]

[[S4.SS2.SSS0.Px4.p1.1]] We finally verify that the hard family belongs to the prescribed class. The proof is deferred to Appendix C.4.

[[S4.Thmtheorem6]]

Lemma 4.6 (Admissibility).

[[S4.Thmtheorem6.p1]]

[[S4.Thmtheorem6.p1.1]] For every $O\in\mathrm{O}(d)$ , the matrix $A=O\Lambda O^{\top}$ satisfies $I_{d}\preceq A\preceq\kappa I_{d}$ .
