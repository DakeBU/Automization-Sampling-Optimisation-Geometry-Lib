[A3.Thmtheorem5]
Fact C.5 (Cosine basis and moment identities). The matrix $B$ satisfies $\operatorname{range}(B)=\mathcal{K}_{q},\qquad\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]=I_{r^{2}},\qquad\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]=0.$ Moreover, $\|t_{i}\|^{2}\leq 2r,\qquad\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=dI_{r},\qquad\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=0.$

[A3.SS2.2]
Proof. $\boxed{\operatorname{range}(B)=\mathcal{K}_{q}}$ . Since $\lambda_{i}=(\kappa+1)/2+(\kappa-1)\cos\theta_{i}/2$ , the identity $\cos(j\theta)=T_{j}(\cos\theta)$ expresses $\cos(j\theta_{i})$ as a degree- $j$ polynomial in $\lambda_{i}$ with nonzero leading coefficient. For each $a$ , the change of basis between $\bigl((g_{a})_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\bigr)_{i=1}^{d}$ and $\Lambda^{j}g_{a}$ , $0\leq j\leq q$ , is therefore triangular and invertible. The nonzero column normalizations in $B$ do not change its range, proving the first identity. $\boxed{\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]=I_{r^{2}}}$ . Recall that $\theta_{i}=(i-\tfrac{1}{2})\pi/d$ . For every integer $k$ with $0<|k|<2d$ , $\displaystyle 2\sin\!\left(\frac{k\pi}{2d}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos(k\theta_{i})$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[\sin\!\left(\frac{ki\pi}{d}\right)-\sin\!\left(\frac{k(i-1)\pi}{d}\right)\right]$ $\displaystyle=\sin(k\pi)-\sin(0)=0.$ Since $\sin(k\pi/(2d))\neq 0$ , this gives $\sum_{i}\cos(k\theta_{i})=0$ . Index the coordinates of $t_{i}$ by $0,\ldots,q$ . The entries involving the constant coordinate satisfy $\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{0}^{2}=d,\qquad\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{0}(t_{i})_{j}=\sqrt{2}\sum_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})=0\quad(1\leq j\leq q).$ For $1\leq j,k\leq q$ , the product-to-sum identity gives $\displaystyle\sum_{i}(t_{i})_{j}(t_{i})_{k}$ $\displaystyle=2\sum_{i}\cos(j\theta_{i})\cos(k\theta_{i})$ $\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\cos((j-k)\theta_{i})+\sum_{i}\cos((j+k)\theta_{i})$ $\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d,&j=k,\\ 0,&j\neq k.\end{cases}$ Indeed, $0<j+k\leq 2q<2d$ , while $j-k=0$ exactly when $j=k$ ; otherwise $0<|j-k|<2d$ . By symmetry, these calculations cover all matrix entries, and hence $\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=dI_{r}$ . Since $\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]=I_{r}$ , the row definition of $B$ now gives $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}B]$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\left(I_{r}\otimes\sum_{i}t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\right)=I_{r^{2}}.$ $\boxed{\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]=0}$ . Since $n=2q+1$ , for $0\leq j,k\leq q$ we have $1\leq n-j-k\leq n\pm j\pm k\leq n+j+k\leq 4q+1<2d.$ Thus the cosine-sum identity above gives $\displaystyle\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\cos(j\theta_{i})\cos(k\theta_{i})$ $\displaystyle=\quad$ $\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}\bigl[\cos((n+j+k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n+j-k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n-j+k)\theta_{i})+\cos((n-j-k)\theta_{i})\bigr]$ $\displaystyle=\quad$ $\displaystyle 0.$ Each entry of $\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}$ is a constant multiple of such a sum, so $\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}=0$ . Consequently, $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{\top}D_{n}B]$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\top}]\otimes t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\left(I_{r}\otimes\sum_{i}\cos(n\theta_{i})t_{i}t_{i}^{\top}\right)=0.$ Finally, $\|t_{i}\|^{2}=1+2\sum_{j=1}^{q}\cos^{2}(j\theta_{i})\leq 1+2q\leq 2r.$ ∎
