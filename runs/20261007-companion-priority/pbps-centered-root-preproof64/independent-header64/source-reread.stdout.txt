2.2 Proximal augmentation and the restricted Gaussian oracle

Our algorithm operates on the augmented distribution underlying the
proximal sampler of Lee, Shen, and Tian [17]. For a
scale `\eta\in(0,1/\beta]`, consider the joint law

`\pi_{\eta}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,.`

(2.6)

The original proximal sampler algorithm performs Gibbs sampling on `\pi_{\eta}`.
Namely, under `\pi_{\eta}` one may generate the joint state as

`X\sim\mu\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\ \text{independently}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptY=X+\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,.`

(2.7)

Its `X`-marginal is `\pi_{\eta}^{X}=\mu`. Write `\pi_{\eta}^{Y}` for
the `Y`-marginal, so that
`\pi_{\eta}^{Y}(\mathrm{d}y)\propto e^{-U_{\eta}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y`; by
(2.7), `\pi_{\eta}^{Y}=\mu*\mathcal{N}(0,\eta I)`
is a Gaussian smoothing of the target.
We use superscripts for marginal and conditional laws; in particular,

`\pi_{\eta}^{Y\mid X=x}=\mathcal{N}(x,\eta I)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.`

(2.8)

Gibbs sampling alternates exact draws from the two conditional laws in
(2.8).
The scale `\eta` controls the strength of the quadratic coupling
potential.
Decreasing `\eta` makes `X` and `Y` more tightly coupled and the
conditional law of `X` given `Y` more localized and better
conditioned, which reduces the cost of conditional sampling. The
resulting transitions are also more local, however, and generally require
more Gibbs sampling iterations. Thus `\eta` balances the cost of each
iteration against the number of iterations.
The convergence and query
complexity are studied further in
[6, 9, 15].

The next proposition collects the basic geometric properties of the
augmented distribution used throughout the paper: the curvature and
concentration of the conditional law, the curvature of the
`Y`-marginal, and a reflection symmetry of the joint law.

Proposition 2.1 (Geometry of the joint law).

The joint law (2.6) has the following properties.

(i) 

For every `y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`, the conditional law
`\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}` has potential (that is, negative
log-density up to an additive constant)

`V_{y}(x)\coloneqq V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\,.`

(2.9)

This conditional potential satisfies

`(\alpha+\eta^{-1})I\preceq\nabla^{2}V_{y}(x)\preceq(\beta+\eta^{-1})I\,,`

(2.10)

so its condition number is at most
`(1+\beta\eta)/(1+\alpha\eta)\leq 2`. Consequently, for every
smooth `f`,

`\Var_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(f)\leq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,,`

(2.11)

and

`\frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}I\preceq\mathrm{Cov}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(X)\preceq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}I\,.`

(2.12)

(ii) 

The `Y`-marginal potential satisfies

`\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha\eta}I\preceq\nabla^{2}U_{\eta}(y)\preceq\frac{\beta}{1+\beta\eta}I\,.`

(2.13)

(iii) 

The state-space reflection

`\mathsf{U}:(x,y)\mapsto(x,2x-y)`

(2.14)

preserves `\pi_{\eta}` and is an involution.

The Hessian
bound in part (i) follows immediately from (1.1); the
Poincaré and covariance bounds then follow from the standard
log-concavity facts (D.6) and
Lemma D.3 in Appendix D, applied with
`m=\alpha+\eta^{-1}` and `L=\beta+\eta^{-1}`. The
marginal curvature bound in part (ii) is derived in
Appendix D, at (D.10). The
reflection statement follows directly from (2.6).

We use the standard proximal map convention of
Moreau [24]:

`\operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)\coloneqq\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigl\{V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,.`

(2.15)

The minimizer is unique under (1.1). The objective
minimized here is exactly the conditional potential
(2.9), so `\operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)` is the
mode of `\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}`; the conditional draw is in this
sense a stochastic analogue of a proximal step, whence the
terminology arises. This exact minimizer is used below only in the analysis; the implementable algorithm never evaluates it and
does not assume access to a proximal oracle.
We use the following
conditional sampling primitive, introduced in [17].

Definition 2.2 (Restricted Gaussian oracle).

For `\eta>0`, a restricted Gaussian oracle (RGO) takes input
`y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}` and, using fresh randomness, returns a sample with law
`\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}`.

B.1 Operator framework and micro–macro decomposition

Our target distribution is the joint law `\pi_{\eta}` defined in
(2.6), and we set `\mathfrak{H}\coloneqq L^{2}(\pi_{\eta})`. Recall from
(3.11) that the ideal Proximal BPS has
transition kernel

`\mathcal{K}\bigl((x,y),\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\bigr)=\delta_{2x-y}(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime})\,\bigl[\rho\,\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})+(1-\rho)\,\mathcal{H}_{y^{\prime}}(x,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})\bigr]\,,`

where `\mathcal{H}_{y^{\prime}}` is the conditional half-turn kernel from
Proposition 3.2. Since the algorithm is a discrete-time
chain, its evolution is described by the induced one-step Markov operator
on observables,

`(\mathsf{K}f)(x,y)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}\bigl((x,y),\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\bigr)\,.`

Thus `\mathcal{K}` acts on probability laws, whereas `\mathsf{K}` is its
induced action on observables. The first term in brackets
in `\mathcal{K}` is the conditional resampling branch. After the
auxiliary coordinate is updated to `y^{\prime}`,
this branch replaces the `x`-coordinate by an independent draw from
`\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y^{\prime}}`. The associated conditional expectation operator
is

`(\mathsf{P}f)(x,y)\coloneqq\int f(x^{\prime},y)\,\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[f(X,Y)\mid Y=y]\,.`

(B.1)

In particular, `\mathsf{P}f` depends only on the coordinate `y`. The range
and kernel of `\mathsf{P}` are

`\begin{split}\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:f(x,y)=g(y)\text{ for some }g\in L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})\}\,,\\
\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}&\coloneqq\ker(\mathsf{P})=\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P}^{\perp})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:\mathbb{E}[f(X,Y)\mid Y]=0\}\,.\end{split}`

(B.2)

Here `\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\coloneqq I-\mathsf{P}`, so
`\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}` and
`f=\mathsf{P}f+\mathsf{P}^{\perp}f`. In the common terminology of
hypocoercivity [14, 21], `\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})` is the
macroscopic subspace and its orthogonal complement is the
microscopic subspace.
Thus functions in `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}` depend only on the coordinate `y`,
whereas functions in `\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}` have zero mean under
`\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}` for `\pi_{\eta}^{Y}`-almost every `y`.

Relative to the decomposition
`\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`, we write an operator
`\mathsf{T}` on `\mathfrak{H}` in block form as

`\mathsf{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\perp}\\
\mathsf{T}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{T}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\begin{aligned} \mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}\,,&\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\perp}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\,,\\
\mathsf{T}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}\,,&\mathsf{T}_{\perp\perp}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\,.\end{aligned}`

(B.3)

The first subscript specifies the target subspace and the second the source
subspace; thus `\mathsf{T}_{ab}:\mathfrak{H}_{b}\to\mathfrak{H}_{a}` for
`a,b\in\{\mathsf{P},\perp\}`.

We first apply this block notation to the reflection map `\mathsf{U}` from
(2.14). We use the same symbol for its pullback on
observables:

`(\mathsf{U}f)(x,y)\coloneqq f(\mathsf{U}(x,y))=f(x,2x-y)\,.`

(B.4)

This pullback is self-adjoint and unitary, and hence has block matrix

`\mathsf{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\\
\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,.`

Here `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{P}\mathsf{U}\mathsf{P}` maps `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}` to itself, whereas
`\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{U}\mathsf{P}` maps `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}` to `\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`.
Since
`\mathsf{U}` is self-adjoint and unitary, `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}` is a self-adjoint
contraction on `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}`. Writing `\mathsf{U}^{2}=I` in blocks gives

`\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\,.`

(B.5)

We next express the joint half-turn operator `\mathsf{H}` in the same block
decomposition. It is defined in (A.9) and is a
self-adjoint Markov contraction by
Proposition A.2. Since `\mathsf{H}` acts only on the
`x`-coordinate, while every
function in `\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})` depends only on `y`, we have
`\mathsf{H}\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{P}`. Self-adjointness also gives
`\mathsf{P}\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{P}`. Set
`\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{H}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}:\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`.
The identity `\mathsf{P}\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{P}` makes `\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}` invariant under
`\mathsf{H}`, so `\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}` is its restriction to `\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`. It is
therefore self-adjoint, and
`\|\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}g\|=\|\mathsf{H}g\|\leq\|g\|` for
`g\in\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`. Consequently,

`\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{P}+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt-I\preceq\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\preceq I\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\,.`

(B.6)

Relative to `\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`, this decomposition
has the block representation

`\mathsf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}}&0\\
0&\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,.`

The Markov operator `\mathsf{K}` induced by the transition kernel `\mathcal{K}`
therefore factors as

`\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{U}\bigl[\rho\mathsf{P}+(1-\rho)\mathsf{H}\bigr]=\mathsf{U}\bigl[\mathsf{P}+(1-\rho)\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\bigr]\,.`

(B.7)

Accordingly, its block representation is

`\mathsf{K}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\\
\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}}&0\\
0&(1-\rho)\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,.`

The diagonal factor in the block representation of `\mathsf{K}` contracts
`\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}` but leaves `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}` unchanged. For an observable
`f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}`, contraction therefore relies on the
`\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`-component `\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f` created by reflection. The next
subsection estimates this component and the subsequent action of
`\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}`.

B.2 Estimates for the reflection and half-turn operators

We begin with the reflection operator `\mathsf{U}`. Let
`f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}`, so that
`f(x,y)=f(y)`. Although `f` initially depends only on the auxiliary
coordinate `y`, reflection gives `(\mathsf{U}f)(x,y)=f(2x-y)`, which generally depends on both coordinates. Its macroscopic and microscopic
components are `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f` and `\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f`, respectively. The following
probabilistic representation identifies the squared norm of the microscopic
component created by reflection with an expected conditional variance, thereby
enabling quantitative estimate by the conditional Poincaré estimates used in
Appendix C.

Concretely, under `\pi_{\eta}`, write

`Y_{+}\coloneqq X+\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,,\hskip 20.00003ptY_{-}\coloneqq X-\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\,,\hskip 20.00003ptX\sim\mu\,.`

(B.8)

The pair `(Y_{+},Y_{-})` is exchangeable. We henceforth identify
`\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}` with `L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})` and write `f(y)` for a
macroscopic observable. Under this identification,

`(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f)(y)=\mathbb{E}[f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Var(f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+})\,.`

(B.9)

To quantify the microscopic component `\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f` created when reflection
acts on `f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}`, define

`\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})^{1/2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\,.`

(B.10)

By (B.5),
`\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}`, and hence

`\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|\,,\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\,.`

(B.11)

Thus a lower bound for `\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}` quantifies how much of a macroscopic
observable reflection transfers into the microscopic subspace.

Lemma B.1 (Uniform macroscopic coercivity).

Assume `\beta\eta\leq 1`, and set

`\rho_{\rm mac}\coloneqq\frac{1-\alpha\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma_{\rm gap}\coloneqq\sqrt{1-\rho_{\rm mac}^{2}}=\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha\eta}}{1+\alpha\eta}\,.`

(B.12)

Note that `\gamma_{\rm gap}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\alpha\eta}`.
Then the following hold.

(1) For every `f\in L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})`,
`\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})` and

`4\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}-\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,.`

(B.13)

(2) Consider the mean-zero macroscopic subspace `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\pi_{\eta})`.
Then, it holds that

`\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\|\leq\rho_{\rm mac}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\succeq-\frac{1}{2}\,I\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.`

(B.14)

Consequently,

`\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}=(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})^{1/2}\succeq\gamma_{\rm gap}I\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.`

(B.15)

We prove Lemma B.1 in
Appendix C.1. The identity
(B.9) expresses `\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|` in terms of the
conditional variance of `f(Y_{-})` given `Y_{+}`. Differentiating the
corresponding conditional expectation shows that this variance controls how
quickly `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f` changes with `y`. The Poincaré inequality for
`\pi_{\eta}^{Y}` then bounds the norm of a centered `f` in terms of
`\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|`, giving the stated estimate.

Since `\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}` is boundedly invertible on `\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}`,
(B.11) gives the polar decomposition

`\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}\,:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}}\,.`

(B.16)

Here `\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}` measures the size of the microscopic component created by
reflection, while the isometry `\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}` records its direction in
`\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}`.
The next proposition says that the half-turn acts nearly as `-I` on the
corresponding microscopic directions `\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}f`.

Proposition B.2 (Global half-turn bound).

There exist universal constants `c,C>0` such that, if
`\beta\eta\leq c`, then

`\bigl\|(I+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp})\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}v\bigr\|^{2}\leq C\log(1/\gamma_{\rm gap})\,\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}v\|^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptv\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\,.`

(B.17)

We prove Proposition B.2 in
Sections C.2 and C.3.

Together, Lemmas B.1 and B.2
provide the estimates used in the modified `L^{2}` argument below.

C.1 Proof of
Lemma B.1

Proof of Lemma B.1.

We first prove
(B.13). It suffices to consider
`f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})`, the general case following by density and
closedness of the gradient.

Recall that the conditional
density `p_{y}` of `Y_{-}` given `Y_{+}=y` is

`p_{y}(u)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V((y+u)/2)-\frac{\|y-u\|^{2}}{8\eta}\Bigr\}\,,`

and define

`s_{y}(u)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla V((y+u)/2)-\frac{y-u}{4\eta}\,.`

Differentiating the normalized conditional density gives

`\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathrm{Cov}\bigl(f(Y_{-}),s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr)\,.`

(C.1)

The negative log-density of `p_{y}` has Hessian

`\frac{1}{4}\,\bigl\{\nabla^{2}V((y+u)/2)+\eta^{-1}I\bigr\}\succeq\frac{\alpha+\eta^{-1}}{4}\,I\,.`

Hence, the conditional Poincaré inequality implies, for every unit
vector `a`,

`\displaystyle\Var\bigl(a^{\top}s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr)`
`\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\alpha+\eta^{-1}}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla_{u}(a^{\top}s_{y})(Y_{-})\|^{2}\,\bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\bigr]\,.`

The assumptions
`\alpha I\preceq\nabla^{2}V\preceq\beta I` and
`\beta\eta\leq 1` give

`\|\nabla_{u}s_{y}(u)\|_{\rm op}\leq\frac{\eta^{-1}-\alpha}{4}\,.`

Consequently,

`\Var\bigl(a^{\top}s_{y}(Y_{-})\bigm|Y_{+}=y\bigr)\leq\frac{(\eta^{-1}-\alpha)^{2}}{4\,(\alpha+\eta^{-1})}\,.`

Applying conditional Cauchy–Schwarz in
(C.1) and taking the supremum over
`\|a\|=1` yields

`\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)\|^{2}\leq\frac{(\eta^{-1}-\alpha)^{2}}{4\,(\alpha+\eta^{-1})}\Var(f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y)\,.`

Integrating in `y` and using (B.9),

`\displaystyle\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha\eta)^{2}}{4\,(1+\alpha\eta)}\,\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,.`

(C.2)

Finally,
`\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}-\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}`,
which proves (B.13).

We next bound the spectrum of `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}`. Let `f\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}`.
Since `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}` preserves constants, `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f` is centered.
The Poincaré inequality for the marginal law `\pi_{\eta}^{Y}` gives

`\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}=\Var_{\pi_{\eta}^{Y}}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f)\leq\Bigl(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\eta\Bigr)\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\,.`

(C.3)

Combining (C.3) with
(C.2) and rearranging gives

`\|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|\leq\frac{1-\alpha\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,\|f\|=\rho_{\rm mac}\,\|f\|\,.`

(C.4)

Since `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}` is self-adjoint, this proves

`-\rho_{\rm mac}I\preceq\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\preceq\rho_{\rm mac}I\,.`

It remains only to obtain the uniform lower bound
`\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\succeq-\frac{1}{2}\,I`.
For every `g\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})`, exchangeability and
(B.8) give

`\displaystyle\bigl\langle g,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})g\bigr\rangle`
`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(g(Y_{+})-g(Y_{-})\bigr)^{2}\bigr]=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl[\bigl(g(X+\sqrt{\eta}Z)-g(X-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr)^{2}\bigr]\,.`

(C.5)

For fixed `X=x`, the Gaussian Poincaré inequality implies

`\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl[\bigl(g(x+\sqrt{\eta}Z)-g(x-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr)^{2}\bigr]`
`\displaystyle\leq\eta\,\mathbb{E}_{Z}\bigl\|\nabla g(x+\sqrt{\eta}Z)+\nabla g(x-\sqrt{\eta}Z)\bigr\|^{2}\,.`

Averaging in `X` and using that `Y_{+}` and `Y_{-}` have the same
marginal gives

`\bigl\langle g,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})g\bigr\rangle\leq 2\eta\,\|\nabla g\|^{2}\,.`

(C.6)

Applying (C.6) with `g=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f` and using
(B.13), we obtain

`\displaystyle\bigl\langle f,\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})f\bigr\rangle`
`\displaystyle=\bigl\langle\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\bigr\rangle\leq 2\eta\,\|\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\bigl\langle f,(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2})f\bigr\rangle\,.`

Thus, as an operator inequality, `2\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\preceq I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}`,
or equivalently

`(I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})^{2}(I+2\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\succeq 0\,.`

(C.7)

Since `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}` is a self-adjoint contraction, its spectrum lies in
`[-1,1]`. The scalar inequality corresponding to
(C.7) is

`(1-\lambda)^{2}(1+2\lambda)\geq 0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\,,`

and therefore `\operatorname{Spec}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}})\subseteq[-1/2,1]`. Hence
`-\frac{1}{2}I\,\preceq\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}`.
Together with (C.4), this proves
(B.14).

Finally,

`\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}\succeq(1-\rho_{\rm mac}^{2})\,I=\frac{4\alpha\eta}{(1+\alpha\eta)^{2}}\,I\,,`

and therefore

`\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\succeq\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha\eta}}{1+\alpha\eta}\,I=\gamma_{\rm gap}I\,.`

This proves (B.15).
∎

The half-turn analysis below uses the following alternative form of
(C.1).

Lemma C.1 (Derivative formula for `\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}`).

For every `f\in H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})` and `\pi_{\eta}^{Y}`-almost every `y`,

`\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\,.`

(C.8)

Proof.

First let `f\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})`. The representation
(C.1) gives

`\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)=\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),s_{y}(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y)\,.`

Using the definition of `s_{y}`, we write

`s_{y}(u)=\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V((y+u)/2)-\frac{y-u}{4\eta}\Bigr\}-\nabla V((y+u)/2)\,.`

To rewrite (C.1) in the claimed form, we
only need to identify the covariance generated by the vector in braces.
Integration by parts in `u` against the conditional density `p_{y}` gives

`\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[f(Y_{-})\,\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\Bigr\}\ \Bigm|\ Y_{+}=y\Bigr]\,.`

(C.9)

Applying (C.9) to `f\equiv 1`, justified by compactly
supported cutoffs converging to `1`, gives

`\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr]=0\,.`

Combining this with (C.9), we obtain

`\displaystyle\nabla\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f(y)`
`\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\Bigl(f(Y_{-}),\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr)-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)`

`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[f(Y_{-})\,\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\nabla V(X)-\frac{y-Y_{-}}{4\eta}\Bigr\}\,\Bigm|\,Y_{+}=y\Bigr]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)`

`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(Y_{-})\mid Y_{+}=y]-\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\,.`

This is (C.8) for smooth `f`.

The extension to `H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})` is a standard approximation
argument once we check that the covariance term is continuous in
`L^{2}`. By (2.11), the conditional
law of `X` given `Y_{+}=y` has Poincaré constant at most
`\eta/(1+\alpha\eta)\leq\eta`. Hence, for every unit vector
`a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}`,

`\Var(a^{\top}\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+}=y)\leq\eta\,\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla^{2}V(X)\,a\|^{2}\mid Y_{+}=y]\leq\beta^{2}\eta\,.`

Conditional Cauchy–Schwarz and
(B.9) therefore imply

`\bigl\|\mathrm{Cov}(f(Y_{-}),\nabla V(X)\mid Y_{+})\bigr\|_{L^{2}}\leq\beta\sqrt{\eta}\,\|\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f\|\,,`

and the same bound applies to differences. Conditional expectation is
an `L^{2}` contraction, and (B.13) controls the
left side. Thus every term in (C.8) is
continuous under `H^{1}` approximation. Density of
`C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})` in `H^{1}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})` completes the proof.
∎

D.1 Hilbert space and Markov operator conventions

Definition D.1 (Hilbert space notation).

Let `\zeta` be a probability measure. The real Hilbert space
`L^{2}(\zeta)` consists of equivalence classes of measurable functions
`f` such that `\int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<\infty`. Its inner product and
norm are

`\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\int fg\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|f\|_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}^{1/2}\,.`

(D.1)

We write

`L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)\coloneqq\Bigl\{f\in L^{2}(\zeta):\int f\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=0\Bigr\}\,.`

(D.2)

When `\zeta=\pi_{\eta}`, we abbreviate the inner product and norm by
`\langle f,g\rangle` and `\|f\|`.

Two functions equal `\zeta`-almost everywhere represent the same
vector. A closed linear subspace `\mathcal{M}\subseteq L^{2}(\zeta)`
has orthogonal complement
`\mathcal{M}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{g:\langle f,g\rangle=0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{M}\}`.
Its orthogonal projection is the unique bounded operator
`\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}` for which `\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}` and
`f-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}`.

All operators in the convergence analysis are bounded linear operators
on the indicated Hilbert spaces unless explicitly stated otherwise.
The adjoint `\mathsf{L}^{*}` is characterized by
`\langle\mathsf{L}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathsf{L}^{*}g\rangle`.
An operator is self-adjoint if `\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}^{*}`, a contraction
if `\|\mathsf{L}f\|\leq\|f\|`, and unitary if it is onto and preserves
norms. A self-adjoint involution, meaning `\mathsf{L}^{2}=I`, is
unitary. For self-adjoint operators `\mathsf{L}_{1},\mathsf{L}_{2}`,

`\mathsf{L}_{1}\preceq\mathsf{L}_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\langle f,(\mathsf{L}_{2}-\mathsf{L}_{1})f\rangle\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }f`

(D.3)

is the Loewner order. If `\mathsf{L}` is self-adjoint, the
spectral theorem defines `\psi(\mathsf{L})` for bounded Borel functions
`\psi`; in particular `\mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathsf{L})` is the orthogonal
projection onto the spectral set `E`, and a non-negative operator has
a unique non-negative square root.

Definition D.2 (Markov operators and density evolution).

Let `\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})` be a Markov kernel preserving `\zeta`. Its
Markov operator on observables is

`(\mathsf{K}f)(z)\coloneqq\int f(z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})\,.`

(D.4)

It is a contraction on `L^{2}(\zeta)`. Its adjoint
`\mathsf{K}^{*}` governs density evolution: if a law has density `h` with
respect to `\zeta`, then its density after one step is `\mathsf{K}^{*}h`.
Constants are fixed by both operators.

If `P\ll\zeta` has density `h`, then

`\chi^{2}(P\|\zeta)=\|h-1\|_{\zeta}^{2}\,.`

(D.5)

Consequently, an operator norm bound for `\mathsf{K}` on
`L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)` gives the same bound for its adjoint
`\mathsf{K}^{*}`, and hence gives `\chi^{2}` contraction.
