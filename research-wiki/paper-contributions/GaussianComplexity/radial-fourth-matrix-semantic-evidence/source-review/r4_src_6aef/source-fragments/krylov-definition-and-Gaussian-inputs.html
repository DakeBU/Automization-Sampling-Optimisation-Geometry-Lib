Krylov representation of the output.

We first control the information revealed by adaptive queries via a standard Krylov argument. The deferred-rotation argument of [22, Lemma 9.2] uses the rotational symmetry of the undisclosed eigenbasis to represent the algorithm’s output in a block Krylov space generated by independent Gaussian vectors.

For vectors g1,…,gq+1∈ℝdg_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and Λ=diag⁡(λ1,…,λd)\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}), define

𝒦q(g1,…,gq+1)=span{Λjga:1≤a≤q+1, 0≤j≤q}.\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda^{j}g_{a}:1\leq a\leq q+1,\ 0\leq j\leq q\}.
Lemma 4.3 (Krylov representation).

Fix an integer q≥0q\geq 0 with 2​q+1<d2q+1<d and let XX be the output. There exist independent vectors g1,…,gq+1∼N⁡(0,Id)g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\sim N(0,I_{d}) such that

O⊤​X∈𝒦q​(g1,…,gq+1)almost surely.O^{\top}X\in\mathcal{K}_{q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1})\qquad\text{almost surely}.

The additional starting vector accounts for the output, which may lie outside the span of the query points and oracle replies. Equivalently, the lemma gives a representation O⊤​X=∑a=1q+1pa​(Λ)​gaO^{\top}X=\sum_{a=1}^{q+1}p_{a}(\Lambda)g_{a}, where each pap_{a} has degree at most qq. The polynomial coefficients may depend on the entire extended experiment. We therefore control the output through an estimate that holds simultaneously for every vector in 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q}.
