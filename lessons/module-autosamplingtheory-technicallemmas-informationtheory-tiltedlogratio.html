    Samplinglib mathematical lesson

    Canonical llr equals the explicit normalized tilt ratio a.e.

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.InformationTheory.TiltedLogRatio.llr_tilted_tilted_ae · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    For a σ-finite base μ and two integrable exponential tilts, Mathlib's canonical log-likelihood ratio of the f-tilt against the g-tilt agrees almost everywhere under the left tilted law with f−log Z_f−g+log Z_g.

    \[\operatorname{llr}(\mu_f,\mu_g)(x)=f(x)-\log Z_f-g(x)+\log Z_g\quad\mu_f\text{-a.e.}\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Expand the left tilt

      Mathlib's left-tilt identity contributes f−log Z_f and leaves a base-to-right-tilt llr.

    2. 2Expand the right tilt

      The right-tilt identity contributes −g+log Z_g, while the self log ratio is zero.

    3. 3Transfer the null set

      The equality is first assembled μ-a.e. and then transported to μ_f because every tilted measure is absolutely continuous with respect to its base.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • The common base μ is σ-finite.
    • f is measurable and both exp f and exp g are μ-integrable.
    • The equality is under the left tilt μ_f, which is the law used by relative Fisher information.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Expand the left tilt

    Mathlib's left-tilt identity contributes f−log Z_f and leaves a base-to-right-tilt llr.

    \[\operatorname{llr}(\mu_f,\mu_g)=f-\log Z_f+\operatorname{llr}(\mu,\mu_g).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    MeasureTheory.llr_tilted_left

    2. Expand the right tilt

    The right-tilt identity contributes −g+log Z_g, while the self log ratio is zero.

    \[\operatorname{llr}(\mu,\mu_g)=-g+\log Z_g.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    MeasureTheory.llr_tilted_right; MeasureTheory.llr_self

    3. Transfer the null set

    The equality is first assembled μ-a.e. and then transported to μ_f because every tilted measure is absolutely continuous with respect to its base.

    \[\mu_f\ll\mu.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    MeasureTheory.tilted_absolutelyContinuous

      Samplinglib mathematical lesson

      The restricted-Gaussian relative score is constant

      AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.InformationTheory.TiltedLogRatio.gradient_quadratic_representative · theorem · Teaching coverage

      Read this first

      Mathematical statement

      For η≠0, the explicit normalized log-ratio representative of the quadratic fiber centered at y against the one centered at y′ has the constant gradient η⁻¹(y−y′). The paper consumer specializes to η>0.

      \[\nabla_x\!\left[-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\eta}-\log Z_y+\frac{\|x-y'\|^2}{2\eta}+\log Z_{y'}\right]=\eta^{-1}(y-y').\]

      Proof roadmap

      1. 1Differentiate the two quadratics

        The genuine Fréchet gradients are −η⁻¹(x−y) and −η⁻¹(x−y′).

      2. 2Discard only spatial constants

        The two log normalizers may depend on μ, η and the centers, but not on x, so their derivatives vanish.

      3. 3Cancel x

        Subtracting the two quadratic scores cancels x and leaves the center displacement with the source sign.

      Full assumptions and notation

      All objects and hypotheses

      • E is a complete real inner-product space.
      • η is nonzero; SPHMC and the cited restricted-Gaussian theorem use η>0.
      • The differentiated function is displayed explicitly and is not Mathlib's arbitrary canonical measurable llr representative.

      Mathematical proof

      1. Differentiate the two quadratics

      The genuine Fréchet gradients are −η⁻¹(x−y) and −η⁻¹(x−y′).

      \[\nabla q_{\eta,y}(x)=-\eta^{-1}(x-y).\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      HasFDerivAt.norm_sq; InnerProductSpace.toDual

      2. Discard only spatial constants

      The two log normalizers may depend on μ, η and the centers, but not on x, so their derivatives vanish.

      \[\nabla_x\log Z_y=\nabla_x\log Z_{y'}=0.\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      HasFDerivAt.sub_const; HasFDerivAt.add_const

      3. Cancel x

      Subtracting the two quadratic scores cancels x and leaves the center displacement with the source sign.

      \[-\eta^{-1}(x-y)+\eta^{-1}(x-y')=\eta^{-1}(y-y').\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      module; map_sub

      theorem gradient_quadratic_representative
    (mu : Measure E) {eta : ℝ} (heta : eta ≠ 0) (y y' x : E) :
    gradient
        (fun t =>
          -(‖t - y‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta)) -
            Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (-(‖z - y‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) ∂mu) -
          (-(‖t - y'‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) +
            Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (-(‖z - y'‖ ^ 2 / (2 * eta))) ∂mu)) x =
      eta⁻¹ • (y - y') := by
  have hy := hasGradientAt_quadraticExponent heta y x
  have hy' := hasGradientAt_quadraticExponent heta y' x
  apply HasGradientAt.gradient
  rw [hasGradientAt_iff_hasFDerivAt]
  change HasFDerivAt
    (fun t => quadraticExponent eta y t -
      Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (quadraticExponent eta y z) ∂mu) -
      quadraticExponent eta y' t +
      Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (quadraticExponent eta y' z) ∂mu))
    (InnerProductSpace.toDual ℝ E (eta⁻¹ • (y - y'))) x
  let c := Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (quadraticExponent eta y z) ∂mu)
  let c' := Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (quadraticExponent eta y' z) ∂mu)
  have hderiv : HasFDerivAt
      (fun t => quadraticExponent eta y t - c - quadraticExponent eta y' t + c')
      ((InnerProductSpace.toDual ℝ E (-eta⁻¹ • (x - y))) -
        InnerProductSpace.toDual ℝ E (-eta⁻¹ • (x - y'))) x :=
    ((hy.hasFDerivAt.sub_const c).sub hy'.hasFDerivAt).add_const c'
  have hvec :
      (-eta⁻¹ • (x - y)) - (-eta⁻¹ • (x - y')) =
        eta⁻¹ • (y - y') := by
    module
  rw [← hvec, map_sub]
  simpa only [c, c'] using hderiv
