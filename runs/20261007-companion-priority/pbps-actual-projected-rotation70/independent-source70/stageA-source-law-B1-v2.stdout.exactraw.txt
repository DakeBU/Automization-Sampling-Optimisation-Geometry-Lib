2.2 Proximal augmentation and the restricted Gaussian oracle

Our algorithm operates on the augmented distribution underlying the
proximal sampler of Lee, Shen, and Tian [17]. For a
scale 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m1 RAW[95819,96193) \eta\in(0,1/\beta]
, consider the joint law

MATH S2.E6.m1 RAW[96437,97977) \pi_{\eta}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

(2.6)

The original proximal sampler algorithm performs Gibbs sampling on 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m2 RAW[98317,98543) \pi_{\eta}
.
Namely, under 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m3 RAW[98559,98785) \pi_{\eta}
 one may generate the joint state as

MATH S2.E7.m1 RAW[99041,100061) X\sim\mu\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\ \text{independently}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptY=X+\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,.

(2.7)

Its 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m4 RAW[100338,100521) X
-marginal is 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m5 RAW[100534,100826) \pi_{\eta}^{X}=\mu
. Write 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m6 RAW[100834,101084) \pi_{\eta}^{Y}
 for
the 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m7 RAW[101093,101276) Y
-marginal, so that

MATH S2.SS2.p1.m8 RAW[101295,102178) \pi_{\eta}^{Y}(\mathrm{d}y)\propto e^{-U_{\eta}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y
; by
(2.7), 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m9 RAW[102472,103097) \pi_{\eta}^{Y}=\mu*\mathcal{N}(0,\eta I)

is a Gaussian smoothing of the target.
We use superscripts for marginal and conditional laws; in particular,

MATH S2.E8.m1 RAW[103426,105403) \pi_{\eta}^{Y\mid X=x}=\mathcal{N}(x,\eta I)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

(2.8)

Gibbs sampling alternates exact draws from the two conditional laws in
(2.8).
The scale 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m10 RAW[106046,106237) \eta
 controls the strength of the quadratic coupling
potential.
Decreasing 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m11 RAW[106308,106499) \eta
 makes 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m12 RAW[106506,106690) X
 and 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m13 RAW[106695,106879) Y
 more tightly coupled and the
conditional law of 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m14 RAW[106928,107112) X
 given 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m15 RAW[107119,107303) Y
 more localized and better
conditioned, which reduces the cost of conditional sampling. The
resulting transitions are also more local, however, and generally require
more Gibbs sampling iterations. Thus 
MATH S2.SS2.p1.m16 RAW[107506,107697) \eta
 balances the cost of each
iteration against the number of iterations.
The convergence and query
complexity are studied further in
[6, 9, 15].

The next proposition collects the basic geometric properties of the
augmented distribution used throughout the paper: the curvature and
concentration of the conditional law, the curvature of the

MATH S2.SS2.p2.m1 RAW[108317,108500) Y
-marginal, and a reflection symmetry of the joint law.

Proposition 2.1 (Geometry of the joint law).

The joint law (2.6) has the following properties.

(i) 

For every 
MATH S2.I1.i1.p1.m1 RAW[109816,110095) y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the conditional law

MATH S2.I1.i1.p1.m2 RAW[110184,110532) \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}
 has potential (that is, negative
log-density up to an additive constant)

MATH S2.E9.m1 RAW[110892,111747) V_{y}(x)\coloneqq V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\,.

(2.9)

This conditional potential satisfies

MATH S2.E10.m1 RAW[112346,113402) (\alpha+\eta^{-1})I\preceq\nabla^{2}V_{y}(x)\preceq(\beta+\eta^{-1})I\,,

(2.10)

so its condition number is at most

MATH S2.I1.i1.p1.m3 RAW[113780,114425) (1+\beta\eta)/(1+\alpha\eta)\leq 2
. Consequently, for every
smooth 
MATH S2.I1.i1.p1.m4 RAW[114525,114710) f
,

MATH S2.E11.m1 RAW[114999,116253) \Var_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(f)\leq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,,

(2.11)

and

MATH S2.E12.m1 RAW[116820,117950) \frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}I\preceq\mathrm{Cov}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(X)\preceq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}I\,.

(2.12)

(ii) 

The 
MATH S2.I1.i2.p1.m1 RAW[118462,118647) Y
-marginal potential satisfies

MATH S2.E13.m1 RAW[118964,120005) \frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha\eta}I\preceq\nabla^{2}U_{\eta}(y)\preceq\frac{\beta}{1+\beta\eta}I\,.

(2.13)

(iii) 

The state-space reflection

MATH S2.E14.m1 RAW[120761,121392) \mathsf{U}:(x,y)\mapsto(x,2x-y)

(2.14)

preserves 
MATH S2.I1.i3.p1.m1 RAW[121745,121973) \pi_{\eta}
 and is an involution.

The Hessian
bound in part (i) follows immediately from (1.1); the
Poincaré and covariance bounds then follow from the standard
log-concavity facts (D.6) and
Lemma D.3 in Appendix D, applied with

MATH S2.SS2.p3.m1 RAW[123430,123763) m=\alpha+\eta^{-1}
 and 
MATH S2.SS2.p3.m2 RAW[123768,124099) L=\beta+\eta^{-1}
. The
marginal curvature bound in part (ii) is derived in
Appendix D, at (D.10). The
reflection statement follows directly from (2.6).

We use the standard proximal map convention of
Moreau [24]:

MATH S2.E15.m1 RAW[125509,126843) \operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)\coloneqq\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigl\{V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,.

(2.15)

The minimizer is unique under (1.1). The objective
minimized here is exactly the conditional potential
(2.9), so 
MATH S2.SS2.p4.m1 RAW[127790,128223) \operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)
 is the
mode of 
MATH S2.SS2.p4.m2 RAW[128239,128585) \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}
; the conditional draw is in this
sense a stochastic analogue of a proximal step, whence the
terminology arises. This exact minimizer is used below only in the analysis; the implementable algorithm never evaluates it and
does not assume access to a proximal oracle.
We use the following
conditional sampling primitive, introduced in [17].

Definition 2.2 (Restricted Gaussian oracle).

For 
MATH S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m1 RAW[129538,129782) \eta>0
, a restricted Gaussian oracle (RGO) takes input

MATH S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m2 RAW[129831,130116) y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
 and, using fresh randomness, returns a sample with law

MATH S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m3 RAW[130172,130526) \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}
.

B.1 Operator framework and micro–macro decomposition

Our target distribution is the joint law 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m1 RAW[708477,708703) \pi_{\eta}
 defined in
(2.6), and we set 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m2 RAW[709015,709487) \mathfrak{H}\coloneqq L^{2}(\pi_{\eta})
. Recall from
(3.11) that the ideal Proximal BPS has
transition kernel

MATH A2.Ex1.m1 RAW[710047,712680) \mathcal{K}\bigl((x,y),\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\bigr)=\delta_{2x-y}(\mathrm{d}y^{\prime})\,\bigl[\rho\,\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})+(1-\rho)\,\mathcal{H}_{y^{\prime}}(x,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})\bigr]\,,

where 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m3 RAW[712803,713115) \mathcal{H}_{y^{\prime}}
 is the conditional half-turn kernel from
Proposition 3.2. Since the algorithm is a discrete-time
chain, its evolution is described by the induced one-step Markov operator
on observables,

MATH A2.Ex2.m1 RAW[713919,715639) (\mathsf{K}f)(x,y)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}\bigl((x,y),\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\bigr)\,.

Thus 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m4 RAW[715761,716000) \mathcal{K}
 acts on probability laws, whereas 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m5 RAW[716035,716239) \mathsf{K}
 is its
induced action on observables. The first term in brackets
in 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m6 RAW[716308,716547) \mathcal{K}
 is the conditional resampling branch. After the
auxiliary coordinate is updated to 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m7 RAW[716631,716857) y^{\prime}
,
this branch replaces the 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m8 RAW[716884,717067) x
-coordinate by an independent draw from

MATH A2.SS1.p1.m9 RAW[717107,717496) \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y^{\prime}}
. The associated conditional expectation operator
is

MATH A2.E1.m1 RAW[717768,719465) (\mathsf{P}f)(x,y)\coloneqq\int f(x^{\prime},y)\,\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[f(X,Y)\mid Y=y]\,.

(B.1)

In particular, 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m10 RAW[719753,720021) \mathsf{P}f
 depends only on the coordinate 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m11 RAW[720053,720237) y
. The range
and kernel of 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m12 RAW[720263,720468) \mathsf{P}
 are

MATH A2.E2.m1 RAW[720692,723787) \begin{split}\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:f(x,y)=g(y)\text{ for some }g\in L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{Y})\}\,,\\
\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}&\coloneqq\ker(\mathsf{P})=\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P}^{\perp})=\{f\in\mathfrak{H}:\mathbb{E}[f(X,Y)\mid Y]=0\}\,.\end{split}

(B.2)

Here 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m13 RAW[724065,724428) \mathsf{P}^{\perp}\coloneqq I-\mathsf{P}
, so

MATH A2.SS1.p1.m14 RAW[724433,724868) \mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
 and

MATH A2.SS1.p1.m15 RAW[724873,725340) f=\mathsf{P}f+\mathsf{P}^{\perp}f
. In the common terminology of
hypocoercivity [14, 21], 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m16 RAW[725546,725895) \operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})
 is the
macroscopic subspace and its orthogonal complement is the
microscopic subspace.
Thus functions in 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m17 RAW[726123,726383) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
 depend only on the coordinate 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m18 RAW[726414,726598) y
,
whereas functions in 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m19 RAW[726621,726870) \mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
 have zero mean under

MATH A2.SS1.p1.m20 RAW[726892,727239) \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}
 for 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m21 RAW[727244,727495) \pi_{\eta}^{Y}
-almost every 
MATH A2.SS1.p1.m22 RAW[727509,727693) y
.

Relative to the decomposition

MATH A2.SS1.p2.m1 RAW[727808,728242) \mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
, we write an operator

MATH A2.SS1.p2.m2 RAW[728265,728469) \mathsf{T}
 on 
MATH A2.SS1.p2.m3 RAW[728473,728680) \mathfrak{H}
 in block form as

MATH A2.E3.m1 RAW[728917,732205) \mathsf{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\perp}\\
\mathsf{T}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{T}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\begin{aligned} \mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}\,,&\mathsf{T}_{\mathsf{P}\perp}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\,,\\
\mathsf{T}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}\,,&\mathsf{T}_{\perp\perp}&\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\,.\end{aligned}

(B.3)

The first subscript specifies the target subspace and the second the source
subspace; thus 
MATH A2.SS1.p2.m4 RAW[732569,733108) \mathsf{T}_{ab}:\mathfrak{H}_{b}\to\mathfrak{H}_{a}
 for

MATH A2.SS1.p2.m5 RAW[733113,733583) a,b\in\{\mathsf{P},\perp\}
.

We first apply this block notation to the reflection map 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m1 RAW[733725,733929) \mathsf{U}
 from
(2.14). We use the same symbol for its pullback on
observables:

MATH A2.E4.m1 RAW[734566,735679) (\mathsf{U}f)(x,y)\coloneqq f(\mathsf{U}(x,y))=f(x,2x-y)\,.

(B.4)

This pullback is self-adjoint and unitary, and hence has block matrix

MATH A2.Ex3.m1 RAW[736242,737315) \mathsf{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\\
\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}\end{pmatrix}\,.

Here 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m2 RAW[737437,737823) \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{P}\mathsf{U}\mathsf{P}
 maps 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m3 RAW[737829,738088) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
 to itself, whereas

MATH A2.SS1.p3.m4 RAW[738108,738627) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{U}\mathsf{P}
 maps 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m5 RAW[738633,738892) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
 to 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m6 RAW[738896,739144) \mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
.
Since

MATH A2.SS1.p3.m7 RAW[739152,739356) \mathsf{U}
 is self-adjoint and unitary, 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m8 RAW[739386,739666) \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}

contraction on 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m9 RAW[739700,739959) \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P}}
. Writing 
MATH A2.SS1.p3.m10 RAW[739969,740242) \mathsf{U}^{2}=I
 in blocks gives

MATH A2.E5.m1 RAW[740478,742053) \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}=I-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\,.

(B.5)

We next express the joint half-turn operator 
MATH A2.SS1.p4.m1 RAW[742416,742620) \mathsf{H}
 in the same block
decomposition. It is defined in (A.9) and is a
self-adjoint Markov contraction by
Proposition A.2. Since 
MATH A2.SS1.p4.m2 RAW[743607,743811) \mathsf{H}
 acts only on the

MATH A2.SS1.p4.m3 RAW[743829,744012) x
-coordinate, while every
function in 
MATH A2.SS1.p4.m4 RAW[744049,744397) \operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{P})

MATH A2.SS1.p4.m5 RAW[744414,744597) y
, we have

MATH A2.SS1.p4.m6 RAW[744607,744893) \mathsf{H}\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{P}
. Self-adjointness also gives

MATH A2.SS1.p4.m7 RAW[744923,745209) \mathsf{P}\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{P}
. Set

MATH A2.SS1.p4.m8 RAW[745215,746034) \mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{H}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}:\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}
.
The identity

, so

MATH A2.E6.m1 RAW[748723,749646) \mathsf{H}=\mathsf{P}+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt-I\preceq\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}\preceq I\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}\,.

Relative to
