1 Introduction

We study the problem of sampling from a centered Gaussian distribution given query access to its unnormalized density. Gaussian sampling is a fundamental algorithmic task with applications in Bayesian inference [7], numerical simulation [11], and randomized approximation algorithms [16].

The target is πA=N⁡(0,A−1)\pi_{A}=N(0,A^{-1}), where the unknown precision matrix AA satisfies Id⪯A⪯κ​IdI_{d}\preceq A\preceq\kappa I_{d}. An algorithm accesses AA through matrix–vector product queries. At each step, it chooses a vector x∈ℝdx\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and observes A​xAx. The goal of the algorithm is to output a random vector whose distribution is within ε\varepsilon of πA\pi_{A} in total variation (TV) distance. We ask how many queries required for sampling from dd-dimensional Gaussian distribution to accuracy ε\varepsilon with the condition number κ\kappa.

Gaussian sampling has a long history, with methods based on polynomial approximations to matrix square roots and inverse square roots [13, 12], Krylov subspaces and conjugate gradients [23, 4, 11], and Markov chain Monte Carlo, including Gibbs sampling and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo [25, 14, 2]. In the first-order oracle model considered here, [17] obtained a query bound of O⁡(κ​d​log3⁡(κ​dε))O(\kappa d\log^{3}(\tfrac{\kappa d}{\varepsilon})) for Metropolized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. For Gaussian targets, [2] improved this to O~​(κ​d1/4​log⁡(1ε))\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\kappa}\,d^{1/4}\log(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon})) using long, randomized integration times, with initialization included in the query count. Analyses of Hit-and-Run also yield an O⁡(κ​d​log⁡(κ​dε))O(\kappa d\log(\tfrac{\kappa d}{\varepsilon})) query bound, since each exact Gaussian line update requires only one matrix-vector query [3, 8]. A sharper worst-case upper bound is provided by the polynomial sampler of [10], which requires O⁡(min⁡{d,κ​log⁡(dε)})O(\min\{d,\sqrt{\kappa}\log(\tfrac{d}{\varepsilon})\}) queries for total variation error ε\varepsilon. At a sufficiently small constant total variation error, they proved a lower bound of Ω⁡(min⁡{d,κ})\Omega(\min\{d,\sqrt{\kappa}\}) against arbitrary randomized adaptive algorithms. They further obtained Ωδ​(κ​log⁡d)\Omega_{\delta}(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d) when κ≤d1/5−δ\kappa\leq d^{1/5-\delta}, for any fixed δ∈(0,1/5)\delta\in(0,1/5), matching their upper bound at constant accuracy in this regime. They conjectured that Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d) queries are necessary whenever κ​log⁡d≤d\sqrt{\kappa}\log d\leq d. A recent preprint [22] gives an Ω⁡(log⁡d)\Omega(\log d) lower bound for κ=2\kappa=2 and ε=110\varepsilon=\frac{1}{10}, using instances with a fixed spectrum and a random eigenbasis. None of these lower bounds grows as ε→0\varepsilon\to 0, and for κ≥d1/5\kappa\geq d^{1/5} they are below the upper bound by a factor of order log⁡(d/ε)\log(d/\varepsilon).

However, both regimes arise in applications. Large condition numbers arise naturally in spatial statistics and Gaussian process regression [1, 6, 18]. On a bounded domain, the covariance matrices of a fixed Matérn model satisfy κ=Ω⁡(d)\kappa=\Omega(d) as the number dd of sampling locations increases [26]. Sparse precision matrices arising from SPDE-based Gaussian Markov random field approximations of Mat’ern fields can likewise become ill-conditioned under mesh refinement [19, 24]. For example, a regular-grid discretization of the Matérn SPDE with ν=1\nu=1 on a fixed two-dimensional periodic domain satisfies κ=Θ⁡(d2)\kappa=\Theta(d^{2}) under uniform refinement.

High sampling accuracy matters for estimating a rare failure probability pp [20], since TV error ε\varepsilon allows relative bias up to ε/p\varepsilon/p. High accuracy matters in Gibbs samplers with Gaussian conditionals [7, 21], where uncorrected sampling errors can bias the chain [15]. From a common initial law, a uniform per-update TV error of ε\varepsilon accumulates to at most N​εN\varepsilon over NN updates. In neither regime do the known bounds determine the query complexity. This leads to the following question.

Can we establish sharp lower bounds for high-accuracy sampling from ill-conditioned Gaussians, uniformly over all dimensions?

1.1 Our results

Our first result is the following lower bound, which holds for every κ≥4\kappa\geq 4 and increases as ε\varepsilon decreases to zero.

Theorem 1.1 (Informal; see Theorem 3.1).

For every integer d≥1d\geq 1, κ≥4\kappa\geq 4, and 0<ε≤1/160<\varepsilon\leq 1/16, any Gaussian sampler requires Ω⁡(min⁡{d,κ​log⁡(1+dκ​ε)})\Omega\big(\min\big\{d,\,\sqrt{\kappa}\log\big(1+\frac{d}{\sqrt{\kappa}\,\varepsilon}\big)\big\}\big) queries.

Our lower bound matches the known upper bound of O⁡(min⁡{d,κ​log⁡(dε)})O\!\big(\min\big\{d,\sqrt{\kappa}\log(\tfrac{d}{\varepsilon})\big\}\big) up to constant factors whenever κ≤d2−δ\kappa\leq d^{2-\delta} for any fixed δ∈(0,2)\delta\in(0,2). At constant accuracy, the two bounds were previously known to match only for κ≤d1/5−δ\kappa\leq d^{1/5-\delta}. This confirms the conjecture of [10] that Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d) queries are necessary at constant accuracy whenever κ​log⁡d≤d\sqrt{\kappa}\log d\leq d. It also matches the upper bound for every κ≥4\kappa\geq 4 whenever ε≤d−c\varepsilon\leq d^{-c} for any fixed c>0c>0. For κ≥d2\kappa\geq d^{2}, it yields the optimal Θ⁡(d)\Theta(d) query complexity.

Remark 1.3 (Connection to strongly log-concave sampling).

Our lower bounds also apply to sampling from π∝e−V\pi\propto e^{-V} with Id⪯∇2V⪯κ​IdI_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}V\preceq\kappa I_{d} using exact value-and-gradient queries, even when the minimizer is known. For κ=2\kappa=2 and TV accuracy 1/101/10, [22] establishes an Oη​(dη)O_{\eta}(d^{\eta}) upper bound for every fixed η>0\eta>0, leaving a gap from the Ω⁡(log⁡d)\Omega(\log d) lower bound, whereas Gaussian sampling has complexity Θ⁡(log⁡d)\Theta(\log d) in this regime. For general condition numbers and high accuracy, [9] obtain TV error ε\varepsilon using O~​(κ​d1/4​polylog⁡(1/ε))\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\kappa}\,d^{1/4}\operatorname{polylog}(1/\varepsilon)) expected gradient queries with a warm start. Thus, our results provide an improved lower bounds for general strongly log-concave sampling with almost tight lower bounds for Gaussian subclass.
