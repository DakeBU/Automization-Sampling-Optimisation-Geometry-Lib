    Evaluate canonical KL with an explicit normalized tilt ratio

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.InformationTheory.TiltedKL.finite_klDiv_and_toReal_eq_integral_normalizedLogRatio · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let mu be a nonzero sigma-finite measure and let f and g define integrable exponential tilts. If their displayed normalized log-density ratio is integrable under the left tilt, then the canonical KL divergence from the left tilt to the right tilt is finite, and its real value is exactly the left-tilt integral of that displayed ratio.

    \[\mathrm{KL}(\mu_f\|\mu_g)<\infty,\qquad \mathrm{KL}(\mu_f\|\mu_g)=\int\!\left(f-\log Z_f-g+\log Z_g\right)\,d\mu_f.\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Normalize the two tilts and obtain absolute continuity

      Integrability of both exponential weights makes each tilt a probability measure. The left tilt is absolutely continuous with respect to the common base, and the base is absolutely continuous with respect to the right tilt; composing gives the KL direction.

    2. 2Transfer integrability only at the value level

      The previously compiled representative theorem identifies Mathlib's canonical llr with the explicit normalized ratio almost everywhere under the left tilt. Therefore the assumed integrability of the explicit ratio implies integrability of the canonical llr.

    3. 3Certify finite canonical KL

      Mathlib's finite-KL criterion requires precisely absolute continuity in the KL direction and integrability of its canonical llr. Both have now been proved.

    4. 4Replace the canonical llr integral by the smooth representative

      Because both tilted measures have mass one, Mathlib's equal-mass KL formula has no extra mass correction. Almost-everywhere equality then changes the integrand without changing the integral.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • The common base measure is sigma-finite and nonzero.
    • The left exponent f is measurable, and exp(f) and exp(g) are integrable under the base measure, so both normalized tilts are probability measures.
    • The explicit normalized ratio f-log Z_f-g+log Z_g is integrable under the left tilt. This real analytic obligation is not inferred merely from normalization.
    • No differentiability or gradient statement is used.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Normalize the two tilts and obtain absolute continuity

    Integrability of both exponential weights makes each tilt a probability measure. The left tilt is absolutely continuous with respect to the common base, and the base is absolutely continuous with respect to the right tilt; composing gives the KL direction.

    \[\mu_f\ll\mu\ll\mu_g\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu_f\ll\mu_g,\qquad \mu_f(X)=\mu_g(X)=1.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use isProbabilityMeasure_tilted, tilted_absolutelyContinuous, absolutelyContinuous_tilted and trans.

    2. Transfer integrability only at the value level

    The previously compiled representative theorem identifies Mathlib's canonical llr with the explicit normalized ratio almost everywhere under the left tilt. Therefore the assumed integrability of the explicit ratio implies integrability of the canonical llr.

    \[\operatorname{llr}(\mu_f,\mu_g)=f-\log Z_f-g+\log Z_g\quad\mu_f\text{-a.e.}\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Apply Integrable.congr to TiltedLogRatio.llr_tilted_tilted_ae in the reverse direction.

    3. Certify finite canonical KL

    Mathlib's finite-KL criterion requires precisely absolute continuity in the KL direction and integrability of its canonical llr. Both have now been proved.

    \[\mu_f\ll\mu_g\ \wedge\ \operatorname{llr}(\mu_f,\mu_g)\in L^1(\mu_f)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{KL}(\mu_f\|\mu_g)\ne\infty.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Invoke InformationTheory.klDiv_ne_top.

    4. Replace the canonical llr integral by the smooth representative

    Because both tilted measures have mass one, Mathlib's equal-mass KL formula has no extra mass correction. Almost-everywhere equality then changes the integrand without changing the integral.

    \[\mathrm{KL}(\mu_f\|\mu_g)=\int\operatorname{llr}(\mu_f,\mu_g)\,d\mu_f=\int(f-\log Z_f-g+\log Z_g)\,d\mu_f.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use InformationTheory.toReal_klDiv_of_measure_eq and integral_congr_ae.

    theorem finite_klDiv_and_toReal_eq_integral_normalizedLogRatio
    (mu : Measure X) [SigmaFinite mu] [NeZero mu] (f g : X → ℝ)
    (hf : Measurable f)
    (hfi : Integrable (fun x => Real.exp (f x)) mu)
    (hgi : Integrable (fun x => Real.exp (g x)) mu)
    (hrep : Integrable
      (fun x =>
        f x - Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (f z) ∂mu) - g x +
          Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (g z) ∂mu))
      (mu.tilted f)) :
    InformationTheory.klDiv (mu.tilted f) (mu.tilted g) ≠ ⊤ ∧
      (InformationTheory.klDiv (mu.tilted f) (mu.tilted g)).toReal =
        ∫ x,
          (f x - Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (f z) ∂mu) - g x +
            Real.log (∫ z, Real.exp (g z) ∂mu)) ∂(mu.tilted f) := by
  let _ : IsProbabilityMeasure (mu.tilted f) := isProbabilityMeasure_tilted hfi
  let _ : IsProbabilityMeasure (mu.tilted g) := isProbabilityMeasure_tilted hgi
  have hae := TiltedLogRatio.llr_tilted_tilted_ae mu f g hf hfi hgi
  have hac : mu.tilted f ≪ mu.tilted g :=
    (tilted_absolutelyContinuous mu f).trans (absolutelyContinuous_tilted hgi)
  have hllr : Integrable (MeasureTheory.llr (mu.tilted f) (mu.tilted g))
      (mu.tilted f) :=
    hrep.congr hae.symm
  constructor
  · exact InformationTheory.klDiv_ne_top hac hllr
  · rw [InformationTheory.toReal_klDiv_of_measure_eq hac (by simp)]
    exact integral_congr_ae hae

end

    Scope and omitted-condition boundaries
    • The explicit ratio's integrability under the left fiber is an input, not a consequence of exponential-weight integrability alone.
    • Almost-everywhere equality is used for values, integrability and integrals only; it is not used to transport classical gradients.
    • The theorem does not prove the restricted-fiber score formula, relative Fisher energy, log-Sobolev inequality, Talagrand inequality, W2 contraction or SPHMC main theorem.
