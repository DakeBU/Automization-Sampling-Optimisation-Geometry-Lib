2 Preliminaries We write $\|\cdot\|$ for the Euclidean norm and $I_{d}$ for the $d\times d$ identity matrix.
For symmetric matrices $A$ and $B$ , the notation $A\preceq B$ means that $B-A$ is positive semidefinite. Let $O(n):=\{O\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:O^{\top}O=I_{n}\}$ denote the real orthogonal group, equipped with Haar probability
measure.
It has two components, $\{\det O=+1\}$ , $\{\det O=-1\}$ , and each have Haar probability $1/2$ .
For $b\in\{-1,+1\}$ , we write $\mu_{n,b}$ for Haar probability
measure conditioned on $\det O=b$ . Targets and oracle. We restrict the target class to centered Gaussians.
This restriction removes the hardness of locating the target and isolates the difficulty of sampling with an unknown covariance.
For an integer $d\geq 1$ and a parameter $\kappa\geq 1$ , without loss of generality, we normalize the spectral bounds to $1$ and $\kappa$ by rescaling and consider
the precision class $\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa}:=\left\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:A=A^{\top},\quad I_{d}\preceq A\preceq\kappa I_{d}\right\}.$ An input $A\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa}$ specifies the quadratic
potential $f_{A}(x):=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax$ and the target distribution $\pi_{A}:=N(0,A^{-1})$ .
At a point $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , the exact first-order oracle returns $\operatorname{FO}_{A}(x):=\bigl(f_{A}(x),\nabla f_{A}(x)\bigr)=\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax,Ax\right).$ (1) On this class, exact first-order access is equivalent to exact
matrix–vector access.
Indeed, a first-order response contains $Ax$ , while a
matrix–vector response determines both the gradient $Ax$ and the function value $\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Ax\rangle$ .
Thus the exact value-and-gradient, gradient-only, and
matrix–vector query complexities coincide. Randomized algorithms. A randomized algorithm chooses its queries and stopping decisions
as Borel functions of its internal randomness and previous oracle
responses, where the randomness is independent of the input.
Its output may be any $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ -valued Borel function of
the same information.
The algorithm has fixed query budget $T$ if it makes
at most $T$ queries for every admissible input and every
realization of its internal randomness. Accuracy measures. For probability measures $P$ and $Q$ on a common measurable space,
their total variation distance is $\operatorname{TV}(P,Q):=\sup_{B}|P(B)-Q(B)|=\frac{1}{2}\int|p-q|\,d\lambda,$ where the supremum ranges over measurable sets, and $p$ and $q$ are the densities of $P$ and $Q$ with respect to any common
dominating measure $\lambda$ .
In particular, $|\mathbb{E}_{P}h-\mathbb{E}_{Q}h|\leq\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)$ for every measurable function $h$ taking values in $[0,1]$ . Sampling accuracy and query complexity. For a fixed input $A$ , let $\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}$ denote
the law of the algorithm’s output, with all internal
randomness included.
For $0<\varepsilon<1$ , define $Q_{G}(d,\kappa,\varepsilon):=\inf\left\{T\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\,\mathrm{Alg}\text{ with fixed query budget }T\\[2.0pt]
\text{such that }\displaystyle\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa}}\operatorname{TV}\bigl(\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}},\pi_{A}\bigr)\leq\varepsilon\end{array}\right\}.$ (2) The accuracy requirement is uniform over inputs and concerns
the output law after averaging over the algorithm’s seed.
Throughout the paper, query budgets are deterministic caps
on the number of queries in each run, rather than bounds
on the expected number of queries.

B.2.2 Single-block Separation We distinguish $P_{+1}$ and $P_{-1}$ using a test of the form $x\mapsto e^{-\lambda\|x\|^{2}}$ .
After Gaussian integration and determinant cancellation, its
expectation difference reduces to a spherical integral.
The following identities evaluate that integral. Choosing $\lambda$ so that these identities apply gives the
following quantitative separation. Proposition B.7 ( Single-block separation). For $n\geq 3$ and $\kappa\geq 4$ , $\operatorname{TV}(P_{+1},P_{-1})\geq\frac{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{n}}{2+\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-5n/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$ (19) Proof. For $\lambda\geq 0$ , let $h_{\lambda}(x):=e^{-\lambda\|x\|^{2}}$ and define $H(\lambda):=\mathbb{E}\left[\det(O)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\lambda}(x)p_{O}(x)\,dx\right].$ Since the two determinant components have Haar probability $1/2$ , $H(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{+1}}h_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}_{P_{-1}}h_{\lambda}\right).$ As $0<h_{\lambda}\leq 1$ , it follows that $\operatorname{TV}(P_{+1},P_{-1})\geq 2|H(\lambda)|.$ (20) We compute $H(\lambda)$ and then choose $\lambda>0$ . For every $Q\in O(n)$ , conjugation preserves Haar measure and
the determinant sign, while $A_{QOQ^{\top}}=QA_{O}Q^{\top}$ and $p_{QOQ^{\top}}(Qx)=p_{O}(x)$ .
Consequently, for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , $\mathbb{E}[\det(O)p_{O}(x)]=\mathbb{E}[\det(O)p_{O}(\|x\|e_{1})].$ At $\|x\|e_{1}$ , the Gaussian exponent depends on $O$ only
through $U$ , since $e_{1}^{\top}A_{O}e_{1}=(\kappa+1-(\kappa-1)U)/2$ .
Moreover, $I_{n}\preceq A_{O}\preceq\kappa I_{n}$ gives the
integrable bound $h_{\lambda}(x)p_{O}(\|x\|e_{1})\leq\left(\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-\|x\|^{2}/2}.$ Using $|\det(O)|=1$ , Fubini’s theorem, and Gaussian integration,
we obtain $\displaystyle H(\lambda)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\det(O)\sqrt{\det A_{O}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-[\kappa+1+4\lambda-(\kappa-1)U]\|x\|^{2}/4}\,dx\right]$ $\displaystyle=2^{n/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\det(O)\sqrt{\det A_{O}}}{(\kappa+1+4\lambda-(\kappa-1)U)^{n/2}}\right],$ where $\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-a\|x\|^{2}/4}\,dx=(4\pi/a)^{n/2}$ for $a>0$ .
The denominator is at least $2+4\lambda$ , so
Lemma B.6 applies with $h(u)=(\kappa+1+4\lambda-(\kappa-1)u)^{-n/2}$ and gives $H(\lambda)=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)^{n-1}}{2^{n/2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)U-(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)}{(\kappa+1+4\lambda-(\kappa-1)U)^{n/2}}\right].$ (21) Since $Oe_{1}$ is uniform on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ ,
Lemma A.1 applies to $U$ . We now match the denominator with a positive multiple of $1+r^{2}-2rU$ .
At $\lambda=0$ , this matching gives $r=(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)/(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)$ , but $H(0)=0$ because both target distributions have total mass one.
We therefore decrease $r$ by a factor $1-1/n$ and set $\displaystyle r$ $\displaystyle:=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1},$ (22) $\displaystyle\lambda$ $\displaystyle:=\frac{\kappa-1}{8r}(1+r^{2})-\frac{\kappa+1}{4}=\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}+(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)^{2}}{8n(n-1)}.$ Then $0<r<1$ , $\lambda>0$ , and $\displaystyle\kappa+1+4\lambda-(\kappa-1)U$ $\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa-1}{2r}(1+r^{2}-2rU)$ (23) $\displaystyle=\frac{n(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}{2(n-1)}(1+r^{2}-2rU).$ The two Poisson identities yield $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)U-(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)}{(1+r^{2}-2rU)^{n/2}}\right]$ $\displaystyle=\frac{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)r-(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)}{1-r^{2}}$ $\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{n(1-r^{2})}.$ Substitution into Eq. ( 21 ) gives $\displaystyle H(\lambda)$ $\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(\sqrt{\kappa}-1)^{n-1}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n/2}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{n(1-r^{2})}\right)$ (24) $\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{n}\frac{(1-1/n)^{n/2}}{1-r^{2}}.$ It remains to lower-bound the test gap.
Since $n/2\geq 1$ , convexity of $t\mapsto t^{n/2}$ implies $\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n/2}\geq 1-\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{2}.$ Also, $\displaystyle n(1-r^{2})$ $\displaystyle=\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}+2.$ Together with Eq. ( 20 ) and
Eq. ( 24 ), these estimates give $\displaystyle\operatorname{TV}(P_{+1},P_{-1})$ $\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{n}\frac{(1-1/n)^{n/2}}{1-r^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{n}}{2+\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}}.$ This proves the first claimed bound.
For the second, $\kappa\geq 4$ implies $\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}\right)$ $\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1/\sqrt{\kappa}}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}(1-\kappa^{-1})}\leq\frac{8}{3\sqrt{\kappa}}\leq\frac{3}{\sqrt{\kappa}}.$ Hence $((\sqrt{\kappa}-1)/(\sqrt{\kappa}+1))^{n}\geq e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}$ .
Using $1+t\leq e^{t}$ , we also have $2+\frac{4n\sqrt{\kappa}}{(\sqrt{\kappa}+1)^{2}}\leq 2+\frac{4n}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\leq 2e^{2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$ Therefore, $\operatorname{TV}(P_{+1},P_{-1})\geq\frac{e^{-3n/\sqrt{\kappa}}}{2e^{2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-5n/\sqrt{\kappa}}.$ ∎
