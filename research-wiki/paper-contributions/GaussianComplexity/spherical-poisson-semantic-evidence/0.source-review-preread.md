## Independent mathematical reconstruction

The target consists of two normalized surface expectations for a uniform point ζ on S^(n-1), with U=ζ₁ and 0<r<1. Set q=1+r²−2rU. On the sphere q=|ζ−r e₁|², hence q≥(1−r)²>0; the powers q^(−n/2) and reciprocals 1/q^(n/2) coincide on this positive base. The expected values are 1/(1−r²) and r/(1−r²), respectively. These are real L1 integrals: the sphere is compact, q is bounded away from zero there, and |U|≤1. They are not merely evaluations of a totalized integral.

The author's route is general Poisson representation (HFT1.21) for continuous closed-ball functions harmonic on the open unit ball, applied separately to 1 and x↦x₁ at a=r e₁. Its kernel is P(a,u)=(1−|a|²)/|u−a|^n with normalized surface measure σ. Divide the two resulting equations by positive 1−r². This route must stay attributed as a general theorem plus two applications. A direct proof of just these identities can be valid as an ASTIS alternative but must not be presented as a formal proof of the general HFT theorem.

The raw A.1 statement is internally inconsistent: it begins n≥2 and later literally says “where n≥3.” Thus n≥3 is the unambiguous intersection. The standard Poisson identities also hold in dimension2, but any Lean theorem at n≥2 should explicitly record the n=2 extension rather than silently erasing the source discrepancy. The raw r range excludes0; if a later theorem includes r=0, that is a valid endpoint extension needing explicit scope. Neither formula extends to r=1 under these hypotheses.

In B.2.2, n≥3 and κ≥4 are the source context. The actual consumer is linearity for numerator (√κ+1)U−(√κ−1), followed by the selected r=(1−1/n)(√κ−1)/(√κ+1). The intermediate value is ((√κ+1)r−(√κ−1))/(1−r²), then −(√κ−1)/(n(1−r²)). This is the selected-numerator spherical expectation immediately before Eq24. Eq24 also uses denominator scaling and the prefactor from Eq21; these identities alone do not establish Eq24, a Gaussian mixture separation result, or the final TV bound.

If implementation uses a potential/flux alternative, independent obligations include smoothness through second order away from the pole, correct n=2 logarithmic versus n>2 power derivative, harmonicity on a genuine open neighborhood of the closed integration ball, a larger parent radius before transferring derivatives to the target sphere, and a normalized flux/divergence calculation. These are implementation obligations anticipated from the mathematics, not claims that the author used that route.
