4.4 Limitations of the Analysis

Theorem 4.1 is restricted to 2≤κ≤d1−δ2\leq\kappa\leq d^{1-\delta} for fixed δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1). Although it applies to every ε≤1/10\varepsilon\leq 1/10, its lower bound does not grow as ε\varepsilon decreases. We discuss two limitations of the analysis that affect extensions to larger condition numbers and higher accuracy.

Limitation on κ\kappa.

Our approach does not extend the Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d) lower bound to condition numbers κ≳d/log2⁡d\kappa\gtrsim d/\log^{2}d. Our proof controls the output of the algorithm by first applying Lemma 4.3 to place O⊤​XO^{\top}X in 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q}, and then bounding the cosine statistic uniformly over this space. However, the enlarged space 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} has (q+1)2(q+1)^{2} generating vectors. Since the eigenvalues of Λ\Lambda are distinct, these vectors span a space of the following dimension.

Lemma 4.7 (Dimension of the Krylov space).

For every integer q≥0q\geq 0 and independent g1,…,gq+1∼N⁡(0,Id)g_{1},\ldots,g_{q+1}\allowbreak\sim N(0,I_{d}),

dim𝒦q=min⁡{d,(q+1)2}almost surely.\dim\mathcal{K}_{q}=\min\{d,(q+1)^{2}\}\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Once (q+1)2≥d(q+1)^{2}\geq d with q=Ω⁡(κ​log⁡d)q=\Omega(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d), we have 𝒦q=ℝd\mathcal{K}_{q}=\mathbb{R}^{d} almost surely. For n=2​q+1<dn=2q+1<d, the definition of θi\theta_{i} gives ∑idcos2⁡(n​θi)=d/2\sum_{i}^{d}\cos^{2}(n\theta_{i})=d/2, so at least one coordinate satisfies |cos⁡(n​θi)|≥1/2|\cos(n\theta_{i})|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}. Taking its coordinate unit vector yields

supu∈𝒦q∖{0}|u⊤​Dn​u|‖u‖2≥12.\sup_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|u^{\top}D_{n}u|}{\|u\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Our separating test in Lemma 4.4 requires this supremum to be at most 1/21/2, which is incompatible with the displayed bound. Thus an argument based on a uniform bound over 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} cannot apply once (q+1)2≥d(q+1)^{2}\geq d.

Limitation on accuracy.

Our test compares the ratio (−1)n​x⊤​O​Dn​O⊤​x/‖x‖2(-1)^{n}x^{\top}OD_{n}O^{\top}x/\|x\|^{2} with a threshold tt. Lemma 4.4 puts every output of the algorithm below tt only if t≥at\geq a, where aa is the uniform bound on 𝒦q\mathcal{K}_{q} that the lemma provides. Chebyshev’s inequality with Lemma 4.5 puts samples from the target above tt only if t<|μn|/Mt<|\mu_{n}|/M. Thus |μn|/M>a|\mu_{n}|/M>a, whatever the threshold. At the same time, the Bernstein calculation in the proof of Fact C.6 requires a≥c/da\geq c/\sqrt{d} even to certify a failure probability below one. Finally, Lemma 4.5 gives |μn|/M≤e−2n/κ|\mu_{n}|/M\leq e^{-2n/\sqrt{\kappa}}. Since n=2​q+1n=2q+1, we must have

cd≤a<|μn|M≤e−2(2q+1)/κ.\frac{c}{\sqrt{d}}\leq a<\frac{|\mu_{n}|}{M}\leq e^{-2(2q+1)/\sqrt{\kappa}}.

Taking logarithms yields q=O⁡(κ​log⁡d)q=O(\sqrt{\kappa}\log d), independently of ε\varepsilon. A different choice of the threshold or of the constants therefore cannot give a bound that grows as ε→0\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 4.8 (Scope of the limitations).

These limitations concern our proof. We do not know whether the same fixed-spectrum family supports stronger lower bounds. Our analysis therefore leaves open whether the same fixed-spectrum family supports stronger lower bounds.
