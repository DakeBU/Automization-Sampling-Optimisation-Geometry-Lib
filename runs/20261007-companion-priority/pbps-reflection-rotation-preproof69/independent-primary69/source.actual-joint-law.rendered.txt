2.2 Proximal augmentation and the restricted Gaussian oracle
Our algorithm operates on the augmented distribution underlying the
proximal sampler of Lee, Shen, and Tian [17]. For a
scale  [S2.SS2.p1.m1: \eta\in(0,1/\beta]] , consider the joint law
[S2.E6.m1: \pi_{\eta}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,.]
(2.6)
The original proximal sampler algorithm performs Gibbs sampling on  [S2.SS2.p1.m2: \pi_{\eta}] .
Namely, under  [S2.SS2.p1.m3: \pi_{\eta}]  one may generate the joint state as
[S2.E7.m1: X\sim\mu\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\ \text{independently}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptY=X+\sqrt{\eta}\,Z\,.]
(2.7)
Its  [S2.SS2.p1.m4: X] -marginal is  [S2.SS2.p1.m5: \pi_{\eta}^{X}=\mu] . Write  [S2.SS2.p1.m6: \pi_{\eta}^{Y}]  for
the  [S2.SS2.p1.m7: Y] -marginal, so that
[S2.SS2.p1.m8: \pi_{\eta}^{Y}(\mathrm{d}y)\propto e^{-U_{\eta}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}y] ; by
(2.7),  [S2.SS2.p1.m9: \pi_{\eta}^{Y}=\mu*\mathcal{N}(0,\eta I)]
is a Gaussian smoothing of the target.
We use superscripts for marginal and conditional laws; in particular,
[S2.E8.m1: \pi_{\eta}^{Y\mid X=x}=\mathcal{N}(x,\eta I)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V(x)-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.]
(2.8)
Gibbs sampling alternates exact draws from the two conditional laws in
(2.8).
The scale  [S2.SS2.p1.m10: \eta]  controls the strength of the quadratic coupling
potential.
Decreasing  [S2.SS2.p1.m11: \eta]  makes  [S2.SS2.p1.m12: X]  and  [S2.SS2.p1.m13: Y]  more tightly coupled and the
conditional law of  [S2.SS2.p1.m14: X]  given  [S2.SS2.p1.m15: Y]  more localized and better
conditioned, which reduces the cost of conditional sampling. The
resulting transitions are also more local, however, and generally require
more Gibbs sampling iterations. Thus  [S2.SS2.p1.m16: \eta]  balances the cost of each
iteration against the number of iterations.
The convergence and query
complexity are studied further in
[6, 9, 15].
The next proposition collects the basic geometric properties of the
augmented distribution used throughout the paper: the curvature and
concentration of the conditional law, the curvature of the
[S2.SS2.p2.m1: Y] -marginal, and a reflection symmetry of the joint law.
Proposition 2.1 (Geometry of the joint law).
The joint law (2.6) has the following properties.
(i)
For every  [S2.I1.i1.p1.m1: y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}] , the conditional law
[S2.I1.i1.p1.m2: \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}]  has potential (that is, negative
log-density up to an additive constant)
[S2.E9.m1: V_{y}(x)\coloneqq V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\,.]
(2.9)
This conditional potential satisfies
[S2.E10.m1: (\alpha+\eta^{-1})I\preceq\nabla^{2}V_{y}(x)\preceq(\beta+\eta^{-1})I\,,]
(2.10)
so its condition number is at most
[S2.I1.i1.p1.m3: (1+\beta\eta)/(1+\alpha\eta)\leq 2] . Consequently, for every
smooth  [S2.I1.i1.p1.m4: f] ,
[S2.E11.m1: \Var_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(f)\leq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,,]
(2.11)
and
[S2.E12.m1: \frac{\eta}{1+\beta\eta}I\preceq\mathrm{Cov}_{\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}}(X)\preceq\frac{\eta}{1+\alpha\eta}I\,.]
(2.12)
(ii)
The  [S2.I1.i2.p1.m1: Y] -marginal potential satisfies
[S2.E13.m1: \frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha\eta}I\preceq\nabla^{2}U_{\eta}(y)\preceq\frac{\beta}{1+\beta\eta}I\,.]
(2.13)
(iii)
The state-space reflection
[S2.E14.m1: \mathsf{U}:(x,y)\mapsto(x,2x-y)]
(2.14)
preserves  [S2.I1.i3.p1.m1: \pi_{\eta}]  and is an involution.
The Hessian
bound in part (i) follows immediately from (1.1); the
Poincaré and covariance bounds then follow from the standard
log-concavity facts (D.6) and
Lemma D.3 in Appendix D, applied with
[S2.SS2.p3.m1: m=\alpha+\eta^{-1}]  and  [S2.SS2.p3.m2: L=\beta+\eta^{-1}] . The
marginal curvature bound in part (ii) is derived in
Appendix D, at (D.10). The
reflection statement follows directly from (2.6).
We use the standard proximal map convention of
Moreau [24]:
[S2.E15.m1: \operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)\coloneqq\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigl\{V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr\}\,.]
(2.15)
The minimizer is unique under (1.1). The objective
minimized here is exactly the conditional potential
(2.9), so  [S2.SS2.p4.m1: \operatorname{prox}_{\eta V}(y)]  is the
mode of  [S2.SS2.p4.m2: \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}] ; the conditional draw is in this
sense a stochastic analogue of a proximal step, whence the
terminology arises. This exact minimizer is used below only in the analysis; the implementable algorithm never evaluates it and
does not assume access to a proximal oracle.
We use the following
conditional sampling primitive, introduced in [17].
Definition 2.2 (Restricted Gaussian oracle).
For  [S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m1: \eta>0] , a restricted Gaussian oracle (RGO) takes input
[S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m2: y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}]  and, using fresh randomness, returns a sample with law
[S2.Thmtheorem2.p1.m3: \pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}] .
