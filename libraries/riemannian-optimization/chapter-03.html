    Explicit geodesics and exponential maps on the unit sphere

    Nicolas Boumal for the geometric route; Samplinglib supplies the ambient Hilbert-space formulas and explicit fourth-order analytic bounds.

    An Introduction to Optimization on Smooth Manifolds, 2023 · sphere geometry, geodesics, and the Riemannian exponential map · faithful paraphrase

    Complete source statement (Samplinglib restatement)

    In a real inner-product space, the standard trigonometric curve gives the signed unit-sphere geodesic. It is smooth, agrees with the ambient exponential formula at nonnegative time, preserves unit norm for tangent increments, and has explicit derivatives and uniform bounds through order four.

    • The ambient space is a real normed additive commutative group with an inner product.
    • Norm preservation assumes a unit base point and an orthogonal tangent increment.
    • The displayed closed derivative bundle assumes nonzero velocity; zero velocity is the constant curve.

    ambient exponential

    \[\operatorname{Exp}_w(z)=\cos(\|z\|)w+\operatorname{sinc}(\|z\|)z\]

    derivative envelope

    \[\|\gamma^{(q)}(t)\|\le2\|z\|^q,\qquad q=1,2,3,4\]

              A fourth-order certificate for sphere geodesics

              AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Geometry.UnitSphere.unitSphereGeodesic_derivatives_and_bounds · theorem · Teaching coverage

              Read this first

              Mathematical statement

              At a unit base point and nonzero velocity, the first four derivatives have explicit formulas and satisfy ‖γ^(q)(t)‖≤2‖z‖^q for q=1,2,3,4.

              \[\|\gamma_{w,z}^{(q)}(t)\|\le2\|z\|^q\quad(q=1,2,3,4)\]

              Proof roadmap

              1. 1Differentiate four times

                Sine and cosine alternate and each differentiation contributes one power of ‖z‖.

              2. 2Bound the two components

                Use the triangle inequality, ‖w‖=1, and |sin|,|cos|≤1.

              3. 3Return one reusable certificate

                The public theorem packages four exact derivative witnesses and four bounds.

              Full assumptions and notation

              All objects and hypotheses

              • E is any real inner-product space.
              • ‖w‖=1.
              • z≠0 selects the displayed closed form.
              • No finite-dimensional or probabilistic hypothesis is used.

              Mathematical proof

              1. Differentiate four times

              Sine and cosine alternate and each differentiation contributes one power of ‖z‖.

              \[\gamma'(t)=-r\sin(tr)w+\cos(tr)z,\quad\gamma''(t)=-r^2\cos(tr)w-r\sin(tr)z,\ldots\]
              Corresponding Lean step

              Private HasDerivAt lemmas prove every displayed derivative.

              2. Bound the two components

              Use the triangle inequality, ‖w‖=1, and |sin|,|cos|≤1.

              \[\|a(t)w+b(t)z\|\le2\|z\|^q\]
              Corresponding Lean step

              Private norm lemmas prove all four uniform envelopes.

              3. Return one reusable certificate

              The public theorem packages four exact derivative witnesses and four bounds.

              \[(\gamma',\gamma'',\gamma^{(3)},\gamma^{(4)})\text{ and }(2r,2r^2,2r^3,2r^4)\]

                      Mathematical statement

                      At a global maximum on the unit sphere, a finite trace of tangent exponential-curve second derivatives is nonpositive.

                      \[\Delta f(w)=\sum_i \frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i))\big|_{t=0}\le0\]

                      Proof roadmap

                      1. 1Preserve feasibility along tangent curves

                        Every scaled tangent direction remains orthogonal to the unit base point, so the exponential curve stays on the sphere.

                      2. 2Restrict the maximum to one dimension

                        The sphere maximum is a local maximum at zero along each exponential curve.

                      3. 3Take the finite trace

                        Every directional double derivative is nonpositive, and their finite sum is the supplied Laplacian.

                      Full assumptions and notation

                      All objects and hypotheses
