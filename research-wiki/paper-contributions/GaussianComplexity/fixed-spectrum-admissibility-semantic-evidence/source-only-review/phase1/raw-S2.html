Targets and oracle.

We restrict the target class to centered Gaussians. This restriction removes the hardness of locating the target and isolates the difficulty of sampling with an unknown covariance. For an integer d≥1d\geq 1 and a parameter κ≥1\kappa\geq 1, without loss of generality, we normalize the spectral bounds to 11 and κ\kappa by rescaling and consider the precision class

𝒜d,κ:={A∈ℝd×d:A=A⊤,Id⪯A⪯κId}.\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa}:=\left\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:A=A^{\top},\quad I_{d}\preceq A\preceq\kappa I_{d}\right\}.

An input A∈𝒜d,κA\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\kappa} specifies the quadratic potential fA​(x):=12​x⊤​A​xf_{A}(x):=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax and the target distribution πA:=N⁡(0,A−1)\pi_{A}:=N(0,A^{-1}). At a point x∈ℝdx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, the exact first-order oracle returns

FOA⁡(x):=(fA​(x),∇fA​(x))=(12​x⊤​A​x,A​x).\operatorname{FO}_{A}(x):=\bigl(f_{A}(x),\nabla f_{A}(x)\bigr)=\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ax,Ax\right). (1)

On this class, exact first-order access is equivalent to exact matrix–vector access. Indeed, a first-order response contains A​xAx, while a matrix–vector response determines both the gradient A​xAx and the function value 12​⟨x,A​x⟩\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Ax\rangle. Thus the exact value-and-gradient, gradient-only, and matrix–vector query complexities coincide.

Randomized algorithms.

A randomized algorithm chooses its queries and stopping decisions as Borel functions of its internal randomness and previous oracle responses, where the randomness is independent of the input. Its output may be any ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}-valued Borel function of the same information. The algorithm has fixed query budget TT if it makes at most TT queries for every admissible input and every realization of its internal randomness.

The accuracy requirement is uniform over inputs and concerns the output law after averaging over the algorithm’s seed. Throughout the paper, query budgets are deterministic caps on the number of queries in each run, rather than bounds on the expected number of queries.
