    Finite power series and fourth-order derivative budgets

    Mathlib supplies the monomial derivative identity; Samplinglib packages finite supports, signed coefficient budgets, and the fourth-order scalar estimate.

    mathlib commit db584cd6d46c92f209a44c0f1c829460d327499d · iteratedDeriv_pow and the finite-sum iterated-derivative interface · faithful paraphrase

    Complete source statement (Samplinglib restatement)

    A finite real power series is smooth, has the expected descending-factorial derivative formula at every order, and admits an absolute coefficient bound on the unit interval; the same elementary estimates control the scalar fourth-order composition expression.

    • The exponent support is finite and coefficients are arbitrary real numbers.
    • The derivative budget is evaluated at a point with absolute value at most one.
    • The fourth-order estimate assumes separate absolute bounds for the first four outer derivatives.

    exact derivative

    \[P^{(r)}(u)=\sum_{j\in S}j^{\underline r}a_j u^{j-r}\]

    unit-interval budget

    \[|P^{(r)}(u)|\le\sum_{j\in S}j^{\underline r}|a_j|\quad(|u|\le1)\]

                AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Analysis.FinitePowerSeries.fourthOrderCompositionExpression_abs_le · theorem · Teaching coverage

                Mathematical statement

                Bounds on the first four outer derivatives control the exact five-term scalar fourth-order chain-rule expression.

                \[|k_4u_1^4+6k_3u_1^2u_2+3k_2u_2^2+4k_2u_1u_3+k_1u_4|\le A_4|u_1|^4+6A_3|u_1|^2|u_2|+3A_2|u_2|^2+4A_2|u_1||u_3|+A_1|u_4|\]

                Proof roadmap

                1. 1Split the five terms

                  The coefficients 1,6,3,4,1 are the exact one-dimensional fourth-order chain-rule coefficients.

                2. 2Bound each term

                  Apply the triangle inequality and then substitute |k_i|≤A_i.

                Full assumptions and notation

                All objects and hypotheses

                • |k_i|≤A_i for i=1,2,3,4.
                • The inner derivative values are arbitrary reals.
                • No sign assumption is used.

                The coefficients 1,6,3,4,1 are the exact one-dimensional fourth-order chain-rule coefficients.

                \[t_1=k_4u_1^4,\ldots,t_5=k_1u_4\]

                The proof names the five summands.
