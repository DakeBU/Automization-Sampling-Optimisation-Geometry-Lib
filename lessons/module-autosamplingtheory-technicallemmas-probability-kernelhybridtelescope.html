    Average a history-dependent one-call kernel error

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.KernelHybridTelescope.abs_real_comp_sub_le_integral_eventBound · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let μ be a probability law on a measurable history space A, let K and L be Markov kernels from A to an output space B, and let ε:A→ℝ be μ-integrable. If, for μ-almost every history x, every measurable output event S satisfies |K(x,S)−L(x,S)|≤ε(x), then every measurable T satisfies |(μK)(T)−(μL)(T)|≤∫ε dμ.

    \[\left|(\mu K)(T)-(\mu L)(T)\right|\le \int_A\varepsilon(x)\,\mu(dx),\qquad \left|K(x,S)-L(x,S)\right|\le\varepsilon(x).\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Write both output probabilities as history integrals

      Fix a measurable output event T. Kernel measurability makes x↦K(x,T) and x↦L(x,T) measurable, and the Markov property bounds both functions between zero and one. They are therefore integrable under the probability law μ. The definition of passing μ through a kernel gives ordinary real integrals.

    2. 2Move subtraction inside the integral

      Because both conditional event-probability functions are integrable, their integral difference equals the integral of their pointwise difference. This is the hidden condition behind the paper-level instruction to average the conditional error.

    3. 3Use the conditional budget under the ideal history law

      The absolute value of an integral is at most the integral of the absolute value. Specializing the almost-everywhere hypothesis to T then bounds the integrand by ε(x), and monotonicity of the integral yields the claimed mean error. The result retains the expected local budget; it does not replace it by a worst-case bound.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • A and B carry measurable spaces; μ is a probability measure and K,L are genuine Markov probability kernels.
    • The conditional event bound holds μ-almost everywhere and uniformly over every measurable event S at each accepted history.
    • ε is a real-valued μ-integrable function. The event bound itself forces ε≥0 on the histories where it holds; no separate nonnegativity premise is needed.
    • All event masses are finite probabilities, so conversion from extended nonnegative values to real values loses no information.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Write both output probabilities as history integrals

    Fix a measurable output event T. Kernel measurability makes x↦K(x,T) and x↦L(x,T) measurable, and the Markov property bounds both functions between zero and one. They are therefore integrable under the probability law μ. The definition of passing μ through a kernel gives ordinary real integrals.

    \[(\mu K)(T)=\int_A K(x,T)\,\mu(dx),\qquad (\mu L)(T)=\int_A L(x,T)\,\mu(dx).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    The local hint proves integrability from Kernel.measurable_coe and measureReal_le_one. The local happly unfolds Measure.bind, uses Measure.bind_apply, and invokes integral_toReal after proving every conditional event has finite mass.

    2. Move subtraction inside the integral

    Because both conditional event-probability functions are integrable, their integral difference equals the integral of their pointwise difference. This is the hidden condition behind the paper-level instruction to average the conditional error.

    \[(\mu K)(T)-(\mu L)(T)=\int_A\bigl(K(x,T)-L(x,T)\bigr)\,\mu(dx).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    integral_sub is applied only after hint K and hint L have supplied both integrability witnesses.

    3. Use the conditional budget under the ideal history law

    The absolute value of an integral is at most the integral of the absolute value. Specializing the almost-everywhere hypothesis to T then bounds the integrand by ε(x), and monotonicity of the integral yields the claimed mean error. The result retains the expected local budget; it does not replace it by a worst-case bound.

    \[\left|\int_A(K(x,T)-L(x,T))\,d\mu\right|\le\int_A|K(x,T)-L(x,T)|\,d\mu\le\int_A\varepsilon(x)\,d\mu.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    abs_integral_le_integral_abs supplies the first inequality. Integrable.abs and integral_mono_ae use hbound specialized to T for the second.

    theorem abs_real_comp_sub_le_integral_eventBound
    {A B : Type*} [MeasurableSpace A] [MeasurableSpace B]
    (μ : Measure A) [IsProbabilityMeasure μ]
    (K L : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel K] [IsMarkovKernel L]
    (ε : A → ℝ) (hε : Integrable ε μ)
    (hbound : ∀ᵐ x ∂μ, ∀ s, MeasurableSet s →
      |(K x).real s - (L x).real s| ≤ ε x) :
    ∀ t, MeasurableSet t →
      |(K ∘ₘ μ).real t - (L ∘ₘ μ).real t| ≤ ∫ x, ε x ∂μ := by
  intro t ht
  have hint (M : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel M] :
      Integrable (fun x => (M x).real t) μ := by
    apply (integrable_const (1 : ℝ)).mono'
      (M.measurable_coe ht).ennreal_toReal.aestronglyMeasurable
    exact Filter.Eventually.of_forall fun x => by
      rw [Real.norm_eq_abs, abs_of_nonneg ENNReal.toReal_nonneg]
      exact (measureReal_le_one : (M x).real t ≤ 1)
  have happly (M : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel M] :
      (M ∘ₘ μ).real t = ∫ x, (M x).real t ∂μ := by
    change (Measure.bind μ M t).toReal = ∫ x, (M x t).toReal ∂μ
    rw [Measure.bind_apply ht M.aemeasurable]
    exact (integral_toReal (M.measurable_coe ht).aemeasurable
      (Filter.Eventually.of_forall fun x => measure_lt_top (M x) t)).symm
  rw [happly K, happly L, ← integral_sub (hint K) (hint L)]
  exact abs_integral_le_integral_abs.trans <|
    integral_mono_ae ((hint K).sub (hint L)).abs hε <|
      hbound.mono fun x hx => hx t ht

      Later adaptive computation cannot amplify one replaced call

      AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.KernelHybridTelescope.abs_real_comp_commonSuffix_sub_le_integral_eventBound · theorem · Teaching coverage

      Read this first

      Mathematical statement

      Under the preceding hypotheses, let S be any Markov kernel from the one-call output space B to a final output space C. Applying the same downstream kernel S after K and after L preserves the mean error bound: every measurable final event T differs by at most ∫ε dμ.

      \[\left|\bigl(\mu(KS)\bigr)(T)-\bigl(\mu(LS)\bigr)(T)\right|\le\int_A\varepsilon\,d\mu.\]

      Proof roadmap

      1. 1Control the laws immediately after the replaced slot

        The first theorem gives an eventwise discrepancy bound between μK and μL equal to the ideal-history mean of the local budget.

      2. 2Apply total-variation data processing to the common suffix

        A common Markov kernel cannot increase an eventwise total-variation bound. Treat every later adaptive action, including its final readout, as S. This is the mathematical reason adjacent hybrids only pay for the slot at which they differ.

      Full assumptions and notation

      All objects and hypotheses

      • The input law, two replacement kernels and conditional error budget satisfy the first theorem.
      • S is the same Markov kernel in both hybrids. In an adaptive proof, the history state must contain enough information that all later calls really are represented by this common suffix.
      • Kernel associativity is used as equality of composed measures; no informal reassociation of conditional distributions is assumed.

      Mathematical proof

      1. Control the laws immediately after the replaced slot

      The first theorem gives an eventwise discrepancy bound between μK and μL equal to the ideal-history mean of the local budget.

      \[\forall U,\quad |(\mu K)(U)-(\mu L)(U)|\le\int_A\varepsilon\,d\mu.\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      abs_real_comp_sub_le_integral_eventBound is called with exactly the same μ,K,L,ε and almost-everywhere conditional estimate.

      2. Apply total-variation data processing to the common suffix

      A common Markov kernel cannot increase an eventwise total-variation bound. Treat every later adaptive action, including its final readout, as S. This is the mathematical reason adjacent hybrids only pay for the slot at which they differ.

      \[\sup_T|((\mu K)S)(T)-((\mu L)S)(T)|\le\sup_U|(\mu K)(U)-(\mu L)(U)|.\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      KernelTotalVariation.abs_real_comp_sub_le supplies factor-one contraction. Measure.comp_assoc rewrites the result into the source-facing composition order.

      Finite adjacent hybrid errors add

      AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.KernelHybridTelescope.abs_sub_zero_le_sum_range_of_adjacent · theorem · Teaching coverage

      Read this first

      Mathematical statement

      For real sequences a and δ and a natural number n, if every adjacent difference |a(i+1)−a(i)| is at most δ(i) for i<n, then the endpoint difference |a(n)−a(0)| is at most the sum of those n budgets.

      \[\bigl|a_n-a_0\bigr|\le\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_i\quad\text{whenever}\quad |a_{i+1}-a_i|\le\delta_i.\]

      Proof roadmap

      1. 1Split the last endpoint difference

        Insert the penultimate value a_n between a_{n+1} and a_0. The real triangle inequality separates the newest replacement error from the accumulated earlier error.

      2. 2Apply the newest budget and the induction hypothesis

        The step assumption bounds the first term by δ_n. The induction hypothesis bounds the second by the sum through n−1. Appending δ_n gives exactly the finite range sum through n.

      Full assumptions and notation

      All objects and hypotheses

      • The hybrid chain has a fixed finite length n; this theorem says nothing about a random or infinite number of calls.
      • Each adjacent discrepancy is a real number with the displayed bound. In the paper consumer, a_i is the probability of one fixed output event under hybrid i.
      • The same event is followed through the whole telescope before taking a total-variation supremum.

      Mathematical proof

      1. Split the last endpoint difference

      Insert the penultimate value a_n between a_{n+1} and a_0. The real triangle inequality separates the newest replacement error from the accumulated earlier error.

      \[|a_{n+1}-a_0|\le|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_n-a_0|.\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      abs_sub_le is the triangle inequality in the required three-point form.

      2. Apply the newest budget and the induction hypothesis

      The step assumption bounds the first term by δ_n. The induction hypothesis bounds the second by the sum through n−1. Appending δ_n gives exactly the finite range sum through n.

      \[|a_{n+1}-a_0|\le\delta_n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_i=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\delta_i.\]
      Corresponding Lean step

      add_le_add combines the two bounds; Finset.sum_range_succ and commutativity identify the resulting expression with the canonical range sum.

      theorem abs_sub_zero_le_sum_range_of_adjacent
    (a δ : ℕ → ℝ) (n : ℕ)
    (hstep : ∀ i < n, |a (i + 1) - a i| ≤ δ i) :
    |a n - a 0| ≤ ∑ i ∈ range n, δ i := by
  induction n with
  | zero => simp
  | succ n ih =>
      calc
        |a (n + 1) - a 0| ≤ |a (n + 1) - a n| + |a n - a 0| := by
          exact abs_sub_le _ _ _
        _ ≤ δ n + ∑ i ∈ range n, δ i :=
          add_le_add (hstep n (Nat.lt_succ_self n))
            (ih fun i hi => hstep i (hi.trans (Nat.lt_succ_self n)))
        _ = ∑ i ∈ range (n + 1), δ i := by
          rw [sum_range_succ, add_comm]
