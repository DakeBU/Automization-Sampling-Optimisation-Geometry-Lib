    A completed Ito process is integrable at a bounded stopping time

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.StochasticProcesses.BoundedStoppedItoMean.itoIntegralProcess_at_boundedStopping_integrable · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let eta be progressive and square-integrable on a positive finite horizon T, and let tau be a nonnegative stopping time with tau at most T pointwise. The completed Ito process I(eta) evaluated at tau is integrable.

    \[I_t=\int_0^t\eta_s\,dB_s,\qquad \tau\le T\Longrightarrow I_\tau\in L^1.\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Stop the integrand

      Form the closed stopped integrand eta times the indicator of s≤tau. It remains progressive and L2 on the same horizon.

    2. 2Use terminal L2 integrability

      The terminal representative of the completed Ito integral of the stopped integrand belongs to L2, hence to L1 on the finite measure space.

    3. 3Identify random evaluation

      The completed bounded random-stopping theorem identifies that terminal representative almost everywhere with the original continuous Ito process evaluated at tau. Integrability transfers across almost-everywhere equality.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • eta is a Samplinglib ProgressiveL2Integrand on a positive NNReal horizon T.
    • B is Brownian with respect to the filtration and measure; this contract implies that the measure is a probability measure.
    • The filtration satisfies Samplinglib's usual conditions.
    • tau is an NNReal-valued Chewi stopping time and tau(omega)≤T for every omega.
    • Lean requests an ambient finite-measure instance to elaborate the completed process; this is logically redundant because the Brownian contract supplies a probability measure.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Stop the integrand

    Form the closed stopped integrand eta times the indicator of s≤tau. It remains progressive and L2 on the same horizon.

    \[\eta^\tau_s=\eta_s\mathbf 1_{s\le\tau}.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use ProgressiveL2Stopping.stop.

    2. Use terminal L2 integrability

    The terminal representative of the completed Ito integral of the stopped integrand belongs to L2, hence to L1 on the finite measure space.

    \[\int_0^T\eta^\tau_s\,dB_s\in L^2\subseteq L^1.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use ItoIntegralProcess.terminalRepresentative_integrable.

    3. Identify random evaluation

    The completed bounded random-stopping theorem identifies that terminal representative almost everywhere with the original continuous Ito process evaluated at tau. Integrability transfers across almost-everywhere equality.

    \[\int_0^T\eta^\tau_s\,dB_s=I_\tau\quad\text{a.s.}\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use RandomStoppingGeneralIto.itoIntegralTerminal_stop_ae and Integrable.congr.

    Scope and omitted-condition boundaries
    • The theorem requires a positive finite construction horizon and a pointwise bounded NNReal stopping time. It does not cover unbounded stopping, local-L2 integrands before localization, vector stochastic integrals, or any Ito formula.
