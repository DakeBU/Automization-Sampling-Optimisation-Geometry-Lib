    Second derivative at a local minimum

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Geometry.SphereSecondOrder.IsLocalMin.deriv_deriv_nonneg_of_continuousAt · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    For a real function continuous at a local minimum, the second derivative represented by deriv (deriv f) is nonnegative.

    \[x\text{ local min of }f,\ f\text{ continuous at }x\Longrightarrow (\operatorname{deriv}\operatorname{deriv}f)(x)\ge0\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Rule out a negative second derivative

      If the second derivative were negative, Mathlib’s strict second-derivative test would also make x a local maximum.

    2. 2Combine the two local inequalities

      A point that is both a local minimum and a local maximum has a neighborhood on which f is constant.

    3. 3Differentiate the local equality twice

      The derivative is locally zero, hence its derivative at x is zero, contradicting strict negativity.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • f is a real-valued function on the real line.
    • x is a local minimum of f.
    • f is continuous at x; Mathlib deriv is total and equals zero at nondifferentiable points.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Rule out a negative second derivative

    If the second derivative were negative, Mathlib’s strict second-derivative test would also make x a local maximum.

    \[f^{\prime\prime}(x)<0\Longrightarrow x\text{ is a local maximum}\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use isLocalMax_of_deriv_deriv_neg, with Fermat’s identity at the local minimum.

    2. Combine the two local inequalities

    A point that is both a local minimum and a local maximum has a neighborhood on which f is constant.

    \[f(x)\le f(y)\le f(x)\Longrightarrow f(y)=f(x)\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Intersect the two neighborhood filters and apply antisymmetry.

    3. Differentiate the local equality twice

    The derivative is locally zero, hence its derivative at x is zero, contradicting strict negativity.

    \[f\equiv f(x)\text{ locally}\Longrightarrow f^\prime\equiv0\text{ locally}\Longrightarrow f^{\prime\prime}(x)=0\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Filter.EventuallyEq.deriv and deriv_eq transport the local equality.

    theorem IsLocalMin.deriv_deriv_nonneg_of_continuousAt
    {f : ℝ → ℝ} {x : ℝ} (hmin : IsLocalMin f x)
    (hc : ContinuousAt f x) :
    0 ≤ deriv (deriv f) x := by
  by_contra hnonneg
  have hneg : deriv (deriv f) x < 0 := lt_of_not_ge hnonneg
  have hmax : IsLocalMax f x :=
    isLocalMax_of_deriv_deriv_neg hneg hmin.deriv_eq_zero hc
  have hconst : f =ᶠ[𝓝 x] fun _ : ℝ => f x := by
    filter_upwards [hmin, hmax] with y hymin hymax
    exact le_antisymm hymax hymin
  have hderiv : deriv f =ᶠ[𝓝 x] fun _ : ℝ => 0 := by
    simpa using hconst.deriv
  have hzero : deriv (deriv f) x = 0 := by
    calc
      deriv (deriv f) x = deriv (fun _ : ℝ => 0) x := hderiv.deriv_eq
      _ = 0 := by simp

        Nonnegative Laplacian at a unit-sphere minimum

        AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Geometry.SphereSecondOrder.unitSphere_isMinOn_laplacian_nonneg · theorem · Teaching coverage

        Read this first

        Mathematical statement

        At a global minimum on the unit sphere, a finite trace of tangent exponential-curve second derivatives is nonnegative.

        \[\Delta f(w)=\sum_i \frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i))\big|_{t=0}\ge0\]

        Proof roadmap

        1. 1Keep every curve on the sphere

          Unit norm and tangency make Exp_w(t z_i) a sphere point for every real t.

        2. 2Restrict the minimum to each curve

          The global sphere minimum becomes a local minimum of every scalar curve at t=0.

        3. 3Sum the directional signs

          The real second-order necessary condition makes every diagonal entry nonnegative; a finite sum preserves the sign.

        Full assumptions and notation

        All objects and hypotheses

        • E is a real inner-product space and the index type is finite.
        • w has unit norm and every direction z_i is tangent at w.
        • f has a global minimum on the unit sphere at w.
        • Each exponential-curve restriction is continuous at zero; diagonal entries equal its Mathlib double derivative and the Laplacian equals their finite sum.

        Mathematical proof

        1. Keep every curve on the sphere

        Unit norm and tangency make Exp_w(t z_i) a sphere point for every real t.

        \[\|\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i)\|=1\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Apply unitSphereExp_norm_eq_one after scaling the tangent direction.

        2. Restrict the minimum to each curve

        The global sphere minimum becomes a local minimum of every scalar curve at t=0.

        \[f(w)\le f(\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i))\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Build an IsLocalMin from the global IsMinOn inequality.

        3. Sum the directional signs

        The real second-order necessary condition makes every diagonal entry nonnegative; a finite sum preserves the sign.

        \[0\le h_i\ \forall i\Longrightarrow0\le\sum_i h_i=\Delta f(w)\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Rewrite each diagonal identity and use Finset.sum_nonneg.

        Nonpositive Laplacian at a unit-sphere maximum

        AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Geometry.SphereSecondOrder.unitSphere_isMaxOn_laplacian_nonpos · theorem · Teaching coverage

        Read this first

        Mathematical statement

        At a global maximum on the unit sphere, a finite trace of tangent exponential-curve second derivatives is nonpositive.

        \[\Delta f(w)=\sum_i \frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i))\big|_{t=0}\le0\]

        Proof roadmap

        1. 1Preserve feasibility along tangent curves

          Every scaled tangent direction remains orthogonal to the unit base point, so the exponential curve stays on the sphere.

        2. 2Restrict the maximum to one dimension

          The sphere maximum is a local maximum at zero along each exponential curve.

        3. 3Take the finite trace

          Every directional double derivative is nonpositive, and their finite sum is the supplied Laplacian.

        Full assumptions and notation

        All objects and hypotheses

        • E is a real inner-product space and the index type is finite.
        • w has unit norm and every direction z_i is tangent at w.
        • f has a global maximum on the unit sphere at w.
        • Each exponential-curve restriction is continuous at zero; diagonal entries equal its Mathlib double derivative and the Laplacian equals their finite sum.

        Mathematical proof

        1. Preserve feasibility along tangent curves

        Every scaled tangent direction remains orthogonal to the unit base point, so the exponential curve stays on the sphere.

        \[\|\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i)\|=1\]
        Corresponding Lean step

        Use the public unit-sphere norm theorem.

        2. Restrict the maximum to one dimension

        The sphere maximum is a local maximum at zero along each exponential curve.

        \[f(\operatorname{Exp}_w(tz_i))\le f(w)\]

        Specialize IsMaxOn to the feasible curve.

        Every directional double derivative is nonpositive, and their finite sum is the supplied Laplacian.

        \[h_i\le0\ \forall i\Longrightarrow\sum_i h_i=\Delta f(w)\le0\]
        Corresponding Lean step
