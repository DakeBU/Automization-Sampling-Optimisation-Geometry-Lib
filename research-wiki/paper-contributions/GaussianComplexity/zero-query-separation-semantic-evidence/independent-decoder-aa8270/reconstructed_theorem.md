For every natural number d with d ≥ 1 and every real number κ with κ ≥ 4, work in the real Euclidean space ℝ^d with coordinates indexed by 0, …, d−1. Let A₁ = diag(κ, 1, …, 1), let ν₀ be the centered Gaussian probability measure with covariance I_d, and let ν₁ be the centered Gaussian probability measure with covariance A₁⁻¹ = diag(1/κ, 1, …, 1). When d = 1, these diagonal matrices consist only of their first entry.

Define the closed slab B = {x ∈ ℝ^d : |x₀| ≤ 1/2}. Then

ν₁(B) − ν₀(B) > 1/8.

The Gaussian parameters above are covariances, so the first coordinate under ν₁ has variance 1/κ. Both measures have mean zero. The constants 1/2 and 1/8 are fixed and do not depend on d or κ.
