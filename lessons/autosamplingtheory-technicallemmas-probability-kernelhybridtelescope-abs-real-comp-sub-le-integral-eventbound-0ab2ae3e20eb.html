    Average a history-dependent one-call kernel error

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.Probability.KernelHybridTelescope.abs_real_comp_sub_le_integral_eventBound · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let μ be a probability law on a measurable history space A, let K and L be Markov kernels from A to an output space B, and let ε:A→ℝ be μ-integrable. If, for μ-almost every history x, every measurable output event S satisfies |K(x,S)−L(x,S)|≤ε(x), then every measurable T satisfies |(μK)(T)−(μL)(T)|≤∫ε dμ.

    \[\left|(\mu K)(T)-(\mu L)(T)\right|\le \int_A\varepsilon(x)\,\mu(dx),\qquad \left|K(x,S)-L(x,S)\right|\le\varepsilon(x).\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Write both output probabilities as history integrals

      Fix a measurable output event T. Kernel measurability makes x↦K(x,T) and x↦L(x,T) measurable, and the Markov property bounds both functions between zero and one. They are therefore integrable under the probability law μ. The definition of passing μ through a kernel gives ordinary real integrals.

    2. 2Move subtraction inside the integral

      Because both conditional event-probability functions are integrable, their integral difference equals the integral of their pointwise difference. This is the hidden condition behind the paper-level instruction to average the conditional error.

    3. 3Use the conditional budget under the ideal history law

      The absolute value of an integral is at most the integral of the absolute value. Specializing the almost-everywhere hypothesis to T then bounds the integrand by ε(x), and monotonicity of the integral yields the claimed mean error. The result retains the expected local budget; it does not replace it by a worst-case bound.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • A and B carry measurable spaces; μ is a probability measure and K,L are genuine Markov probability kernels.
    • The conditional event bound holds μ-almost everywhere and uniformly over every measurable event S at each accepted history.
    • ε is a real-valued μ-integrable function. The event bound itself forces ε≥0 on the histories where it holds; no separate nonnegativity premise is needed.
    • All event masses are finite probabilities, so conversion from extended nonnegative values to real values loses no information.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Write both output probabilities as history integrals

    Fix a measurable output event T. Kernel measurability makes x↦K(x,T) and x↦L(x,T) measurable, and the Markov property bounds both functions between zero and one. They are therefore integrable under the probability law μ. The definition of passing μ through a kernel gives ordinary real integrals.

    \[(\mu K)(T)=\int_A K(x,T)\,\mu(dx),\qquad (\mu L)(T)=\int_A L(x,T)\,\mu(dx).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    The local hint proves integrability from Kernel.measurable_coe and measureReal_le_one. The local happly unfolds Measure.bind, uses Measure.bind_apply, and invokes integral_toReal after proving every conditional event has finite mass.

    2. Move subtraction inside the integral

    Because both conditional event-probability functions are integrable, their integral difference equals the integral of their pointwise difference. This is the hidden condition behind the paper-level instruction to average the conditional error.

    \[(\mu K)(T)-(\mu L)(T)=\int_A\bigl(K(x,T)-L(x,T)\bigr)\,\mu(dx).\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    integral_sub is applied only after hint K and hint L have supplied both integrability witnesses.

    3. Use the conditional budget under the ideal history law

    The absolute value of an integral is at most the integral of the absolute value. Specializing the almost-everywhere hypothesis to T then bounds the integrand by ε(x), and monotonicity of the integral yields the claimed mean error. The result retains the expected local budget; it does not replace it by a worst-case bound.

    \[\left|\int_A(K(x,T)-L(x,T))\,d\mu\right|\le\int_A|K(x,T)-L(x,T)|\,d\mu\le\int_A\varepsilon(x)\,d\mu.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    abs_integral_le_integral_abs supplies the first inequality. Integrable.abs and integral_mono_ae use hbound specialized to T for the second.

    theorem abs_real_comp_sub_le_integral_eventBound
    {A B : Type*} [MeasurableSpace A] [MeasurableSpace B]
    (μ : Measure A) [IsProbabilityMeasure μ]
    (K L : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel K] [IsMarkovKernel L]
    (ε : A → ℝ) (hε : Integrable ε μ)
    (hbound : ∀ᵐ x ∂μ, ∀ s, MeasurableSet s →
      |(K x).real s - (L x).real s| ≤ ε x) :
    ∀ t, MeasurableSet t →
      |(K ∘ₘ μ).real t - (L ∘ₘ μ).real t| ≤ ∫ x, ε x ∂μ := by
  intro t ht
  have hint (M : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel M] :
      Integrable (fun x => (M x).real t) μ := by
    apply (integrable_const (1 : ℝ)).mono'
      (M.measurable_coe ht).ennreal_toReal.aestronglyMeasurable
    exact Filter.Eventually.of_forall fun x => by
      rw [Real.norm_eq_abs, abs_of_nonneg ENNReal.toReal_nonneg]
      exact (measureReal_le_one : (M x).real t ≤ 1)
  have happly (M : Kernel A B) [IsMarkovKernel M] :
      (M ∘ₘ μ).real t = ∫ x, (M x).real t ∂μ := by
    change (Measure.bind μ M t).toReal = ∫ x, (M x t).toReal ∂μ
    rw [Measure.bind_apply ht M.aemeasurable]
    exact (integral_toReal (M.measurable_coe ht).aemeasurable
      (Filter.Eventually.of_forall fun x => measure_lt_top (M x) t)).symm
  rw [happly K, happly L, ← integral_sub (hint K) (hint L)]
  exact abs_integral_le_integral_abs.trans <|
    integral_mono_ae ((hint K).sub (hint L)).abs hε <|
      hbound.mono fun x hx => hx t ht
