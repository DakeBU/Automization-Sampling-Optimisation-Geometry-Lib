Anonymous theorem reconstruction

Fix a finite-dimensional real inner-product space E, with its normed additive group structure and its Borel measurable structure. Dimension zero is permitted. Fix V : E -> R, nonnegative real parameters alpha and beta, and eta : R. The only supplied mathematical hypotheses are:
(1) alpha > 0;
(2) alpha <= beta;
(3) V is twice continuously differentiable;
(4) for every x,v in E,
    alpha * ||v||^2 <= D(DV)(x)[v][v] <= beta * ||v||^2;
(5) eta > 0;
(6) beta * eta <= 1.
Here alpha and beta in real formulas mean their coercions from NNReal to R. D denotes the real Frechet derivative, and grad V is the real inner-product gradient. No measurable path, nonexplosion, normalizer positivity, or probability-kernel premise is supplied.

Define the following objects literally. The clock sample space is Omega = R^N, equipped with the canonical countable product measurable structure and the probability P = product over k in N of Exp(1), where Exp(1) is the rate-one exponential measure on R. For every sample omega, including exceptional samples outside the support of P, let e_k(omega) = max(omega_k,0), regarded as an NNReal.

For y,r in E define c(y,r) = y - eta * grad V(r). For z=(x,v) in E x E and real s, put
    Phi(y,r,s,(x,v)) =
      ( c(y,r) + cos(s)*(x-c(y,r)) + sqrt(eta)*sin(s)*v,
        (-sin(s)/sqrt(eta))*(x-c(y,r)) + cos(s)*v ).
Scalar products with vectors here are real scalar multiplication. Write g_r(x) = grad V(x) - grad V(r). Define
    S(r,(x,v)) = ( x, v - (2*<v,g_r(x)> / ||g_r(x)||^2)*g_r(x) ),
    rate(r,(x,v)) = sqrt(eta) * max(0,<v,g_r(x)>).
These are total literal Lean functions: there is no supplied premise g_r(x) != 0. Division follows the total real-field convention, so the displayed S is defined also at a zero gradient difference.

For u in NNReal, define the cumulative rate
    Lambda(y,r,z,u) = integral from 0 to (u:R) of rate(r,Phi(y,r,s,z)) ds.
For e in NNReal define tau(y,r,z,e) in NNReal union {infinity} as the infimum of nonnegative times u for which Lambda(y,r,z,u)-e belongs to [0,infinity), equivalently Lambda(y,r,z,u) >= e, with infinity when the hit set is empty. This is precisely the supplied hittingAfter-at-zero convention; no alternative first strictly positive time is used.

The finite-record state space is (NNReal x (E x E)) disjoint union {stopped}. Given y,r,e, update a finite record (a,z) as follows. If tau(y,r,z,e)=infinity, return stopped. Otherwise return
    ( a+tau(y,r,z,e), S(r,Phi(y,r,(tau(y,r,z,e):R),z)) ).
The stopped record is absorbing. Finite values of tau in this expression are extracted by untopD 0 after the infinity guard. Initialize R_0(y,r,z0,e)=(0,z0) and recursively use e_k to produce R_{k+1} from R_k. Define T_n as the accumulated time of R_n when R_n is finite, and infinity when R_n is stopped. The stopped record carries no phase state at infinity. A finite-time trajectory may still follow the last finite record when its next time is infinity.

Conclusion: there exists a single total map
    Z : E -> E -> (E x E) -> NNReal -> Omega -> (E x E)
with all of the following properties. All clock records and times below use e=e(omega).

A. Joint measurability and total path convention.
The map (y,r,z0,t,omega) |-> Z(y,r,z0,t,omega) is measurable on the displayed product measurable spaces. For every y,r,z0, every sample omega, every t in NNReal, every n in N and every finite record (a,z), if
    T_n <= t < T_{n+1} and R_n=(a,z),
then
    Z(y,r,z0,t,omega) = Phi(y,r,((t-a):NNReal):R,z).
NNReal subtraction here is truncated at zero; on these time intervals the later almost-sure conclusion also states a<=t. Endpoint conventions are left closed and right open. For every y,r,z0,omega,t, if there is no n with T_n<=t<T_{n+1}, then
    Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)=z0.
Thus the existential witness has a specified value even for exceptional clock samples or times that lie in no such interval.

B. Almost-sure coverage at every nonnegative time and initialization.
For every fixed y,r,z0, for P-almost every omega, simultaneously for every t in NNReal, there exist n in N and a finite record (a,z) such that
    T_n <= t < T_{n+1},
    R_n=(a,z),
    a<=t,
    ((t-a):NNReal) < tau(y,r,z,e_n(omega)),
    Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)=Phi(y,r,((t-a):NNReal):R,z).
On the same almost-sure set,
    Z(y,r,z0,0,omega)=z0.
The exceptional null set may depend on y,r,z0. The time universal quantifier is inside the almost-sure assertion, so one such set works for all times for that fixed triple. This is a coverage and initialization conclusion, not a hypothesis on clock samples. In particular, the statement supplies finite record-index coverage for every finite nonnegative time almost surely.

C. A deviation probability bound and continuity in probability at zero.
For every y,r,z0 and every t in NNReal, the event
    B_t = {omega : Z(y,r,z0,t,omega) != Phi(y,r,(t:R),z0)}
is measurable and its real measure satisfies
    P.real(B_t) <= 1-exp(-Lambda(y,r,z0,t)).
For every real delta>0 the events
    C_{t,delta} = {omega : delta <= ||Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)-z0||}
are measurable for all t in NNReal, and
    lim_{t -> 0 in NNReal} P.real(C_{t,delta}) = 0.
The phase norm is the product norm appearing in Lean. The limit is the ordinary, nonpunctured neighborhood filter at zero within nonnegative times.

D. Bounded continuous test expectations.
For every y,r,z0, every continuous f : E x E -> R, and every real M>=0 satisfying |f(z)|<=M for every phase z, the random variable omega |-> f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) is measurable and Bochner integrable with respect to P for every t in NNReal. Its expectation obeys
    | integral f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) dP(omega)
        - f(Phi(y,r,(t:R),z0)) |
       <= 2*M*(1-exp(-Lambda(y,r,z0,t))).
Also
    lim_{t -> 0 in NNReal} integral f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) dP(omega)
       = f(z0).
Continuity and a global absolute bound are supplied only for the universally quantified test f; no derivative or compact-support premise on f is present. The same single Z works for every f and every admissible M.

E. Normalized phase law and squared expectation continuity.
For y in E let
    q_y = volume tilted by [x |-> -V(x)-||x-y||^2/(2*eta)],
where tilted means normalized exponential tilt, with its stipulated totalized zero convention if the normalizer vanishes. Let
    nu_y = q_y product stdGaussian(E),
where stdGaussian(E) is the identity-covariance standard Gaussian probability on E, and the product makes position and velocity independent. For every y, q_y and nu_y are probability measures. Probability normalization is an output, not a premise; no arbitrary reference density or unnormalized tilt replaces the displayed law. These measures depend on y,V,eta and not on the reference point r.

For every y,r, every continuous f : E x E -> R and every real M>=0 with |f(z)|<=M for all z, define the actual clock expectation, indexed by initial phase,
    A_t(z0) = integral f(Z(y,r,z0,t,omega)) dP(omega).
For every t in NNReal, A_t is measurable as a function of z0,
    z0 |-> (A_t(z0)-f(z0))^2
is Bochner integrable under nu_y, and, for every z0 without an almost-everywhere restriction,
    (A_t(z0)-f(z0))^2 <= 4*M^2.
Finally,
    lim_{t -> 0 in NNReal}
      integral (A_t(z0)-f(z0))^2 dnu_y(z0) = 0.
The inner expectation is under the actual exponential-clock product P; the outer integral is under the literal normalized tilted-position times Gaussian-velocity law. This asserts the displayed squared-integral convergence for bounded continuous tests. It does not quantify over all L2 functions or assert a semigroup law, invariance of nu_y, reversibility, uniqueness, an infinitesimal generator identity, or continuity at every positive time.

Quantifier and regularity summary.
After fixing E,V,alpha,beta,eta and the six analytic premises, the existential Z precedes every parameter choice y,r,z0, all times, all clock samples and every bounded continuous test. Joint measurability, almost-sure finite-index coverage, initialization, integrability, probability normalization, deviation estimates, and both expectation limits are conclusions. No randomness in y,r,z0 is assumed. All nonnegative times include zero; natural indices include zero. Totality applies on all of Omega, while the strongest time coverage is almost sure for each fixed initial/parameter triple. No dimension-dependent constant, extra derivative bound, externally supplied path, additional initial-law assumption, or hidden moment assumption occurs in the displayed proposition. The numeric constants in the displayed quantitative outputs are exactly 2 and 4, and the event-probability factor is exactly 1-exp(-Lambda).
