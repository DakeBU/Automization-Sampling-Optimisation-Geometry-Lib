    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    For real sequences a and δ and a natural number n, if every adjacent difference |a(i+1)−a(i)| is at most δ(i) for i<n, then the endpoint difference |a(n)−a(0)| is at most the sum of those n budgets.

    \[\bigl|a_n-a_0\bigr|\le\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_i\quad\text{whenever}\quad |a_{i+1}-a_i|\le\delta_i.\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Split the last endpoint difference

      Insert the penultimate value a_n between a_{n+1} and a_0. The real triangle inequality separates the newest replacement error from the accumulated earlier error.

    2. 2Apply the newest budget and the induction hypothesis

      The step assumption bounds the first term by δ_n. The induction hypothesis bounds the second by the sum through n−1. Appending δ_n gives exactly the finite range sum through n.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • The hybrid chain has a fixed finite length n; this theorem says nothing about a random or infinite number of calls.
    • Each adjacent discrepancy is a real number with the displayed bound. In the paper consumer, a_i is the probability of one fixed output event under hybrid i.
    • The same event is followed through the whole telescope before taking a total-variation supremum.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Split the last endpoint difference

    Insert the penultimate value a_n between a_{n+1} and a_0. The real triangle inequality separates the newest replacement error from the accumulated earlier error.

    \[|a_{n+1}-a_0|\le|a_{n+1}-a_n|+|a_n-a_0|.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    abs_sub_le is the triangle inequality in the required three-point form.

    2. Apply the newest budget and the induction hypothesis

    The step assumption bounds the first term by δ_n. The induction hypothesis bounds the second by the sum through n−1. Appending δ_n gives exactly the finite range sum through n.

    \[|a_{n+1}-a_0|\le\delta_n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_i=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\delta_i.\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    add_le_add combines the two bounds; Finset.sum_range_succ and commutativity identify the resulting expression with the canonical range sum.

    Lean proof · abs_sub_zero_le_sum_range_of_adjacent

    Induction on n, one application of the three-point absolute-value inequality, and the finite range-sum recursion.

    theorem abs_sub_zero_le_sum_range_of_adjacent
    (a δ : ℕ → ℝ) (n : ℕ)
    (hstep : ∀ i < n, |a (i + 1) - a i| ≤ δ i) :
    |a n - a 0| ≤ ∑ i ∈ range n, δ i := by
  induction n with
  | zero => simp
  | succ n ih =>
      calc
        |a (n + 1) - a 0| ≤ |a (n + 1) - a n| + |a n - a 0| := by
          exact abs_sub_le _ _ _
        _ ≤ δ n + ∑ i ∈ range n, δ i :=
          add_le_add (hstep n (Nat.lt_succ_self n))
            (ih fun i hi => hstep i (hi.trans (Nat.lt_succ_self n)))
        _ = ∑ i ∈ range (n + 1), δ i := by
          rw [sum_range_succ, add_comm]
