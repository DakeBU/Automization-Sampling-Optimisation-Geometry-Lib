D.3 Comparison of positive operators

Lemma D.4 (Comparison of positive operators).

Let `\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}` be bounded positive semidefinite self-adjoint
operators on a Hilbert space `\mathcal{H}`, and suppose that for `M\geq m>0`, `0<mI\preceq\mathsf{B}\preceq MI`.
Assume that, for every `v\in\mathcal{H}`, `\|\mathsf{A}v\|^{2}\leq\|v\|\,\|\mathsf{B}v\|`.
Then, for `K\coloneqq\frac{M}{m}`,

`\mathsf{A}^{2}\preceq\bigl(1+\frac{\log K}{\pi}\bigr)\,\mathsf{B}\,.`

Proof.

By replacing

`\mathsf{A}\leftarrow m^{-1/2}\mathsf{A}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{B}\leftarrow m^{-1}\mathsf{B}\,,`

we may assume without loss of generality that `I\preceq\mathsf{B}\preceq KI`.
For non-zero `v\in\mathcal{H}`,

`\displaystyle\langle v,\mathsf{A}^{2}v\rangle`
`\displaystyle=\langle\mathsf{B}^{1/2}v,\mathsf{B}^{-1/2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\mathsf{B}^{-1/2}\mathsf{B}^{1/2}v\rangle\leq\|\mathsf{B}^{-1/2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\mathsf{B}^{-1/2}\|\,\langle v,\mathsf{B}v\rangle=\|\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|\,\langle v,\mathsf{B}v\rangle\,.`

Also,

`\displaystyle\|\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|`
`\displaystyle=\Bigl\lVert\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{A}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1}\mathsf{A}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\rVert\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathsf{A}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|\,\mathrm{d}t\,.`

Moreover, by hypothesis,

`\displaystyle\|\mathsf{A}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|`
`\displaystyle=\|(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1/2}\mathsf{A}^{2}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1/2}\|`

`\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\mathsf{A}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1/2}v\|^{2}}{\|v\|^{2}}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\mathsf{A}u\|^{2}}{t^{2}\,\|u\|^{2}+\|\mathsf{B}u\|^{2}}`

`\displaystyle\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|\,\|\mathsf{B}u\|}{t^{2}\,\|u\|^{2}+\|\mathsf{B}u\|^{2}}\,.`

Setting `r\coloneqq\|\mathsf{B}u\|/\|u\|\in[1,K]`,

`\displaystyle\|\mathsf{A}(t^{2}I+\mathsf{B}^{2})^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|`
`\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in[1,K]}\frac{r}{t^{2}+r^{2}}=\begin{cases}1/(t^{2}+1)\,,&t<1\,,\\
1/(2t)\,,&t\in[1,K]\,,\\
K/(t^{2}+K^{2})\,,&t>K\,.\end{cases}`

Hence,

`\displaystyle\|\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}^{-1}\mathsf{A}\|`
`\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\,\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{2}+1}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1}^{K}\frac{1}{2t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{K}^{\infty}\frac{K}{t^{2}+K^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)=1+\frac{\log K}{\pi}\,.`

This completes the proof.
∎

The logarithmic dependence on `K` in Lemma D.4 is sharp in general.

Remark D.5.

Let `K>1`, take `\mathcal{H}=L^{2}([1,K],\mathrm{d}x)`, and `\mathsf{A}f(x)\coloneqq(\pi\,(K-1))^{-1/2}\int_{1}^{K}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y`, `\mathsf{B}f(x)\coloneqq xf(x)`.
Then, by the classical integral Carlson inequality,

`\|\mathsf{A}f\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\Bigl(\int_{1}^{K}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{2}\leq\|f\|\,\|\mathsf{B}f\|\,.`

Thus, the hypotheses of Lemma D.4 are met.
If we take `f(x)\coloneqq 1/x`, then `\|\mathsf{A}f\|^{2}=(\log K)^{2}/\pi`, whereas `\langle f,\mathsf{B}f\rangle=\log K`, so the best possible constant in Lemma D.4 can be no better than `\max\{1,(\log K)/\pi\}`, where the maximum with `1` arises from the trivial case `\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{B}=I`.
