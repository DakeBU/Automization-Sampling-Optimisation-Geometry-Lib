B.1 Indistinguishability: Proof of Lemma 3.2

For every randomized first-order algorithm Alg\mathrm{Alg} with query budget T<n/2T<n/2,

𝔼A∼𝒟+1​[ρAAlg]=𝔼A∼𝒟−1​[ρAAlg].\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\mathcal{D}_{+1}}[\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}]=\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\mathcal{D}_{-1}}[\rho_{A}^{\mathrm{Alg}}]. (13)

Transcript indistinguishability.

The residual Haar laws give a response kernel independent of the determinant sign. Iterating this kernel yields identical transcript laws.

Proof of Lemma 3.2.

Let ξ\xi be the algorithm’s entire random seed. For each fixed seed, the oracle reduction gives a deterministic primitive-query algorithm with at most 2​T<n2T<n queries per block. Applying Lemma B.4 and integrating over the common seed law yields

ℒ+1​(ξ,HM)=ℒ−1​(ξ,HM),\mathcal{L}_{+1}(\xi,H_{M})=\mathcal{L}_{-1}(\xi,H_{M}),

where M=2​T​kM=2Tk. The output is a Borel function Ψ⁡(ξ,HM)\Psi(\xi,H_{M}), so its law is identical under both signs. Hence
