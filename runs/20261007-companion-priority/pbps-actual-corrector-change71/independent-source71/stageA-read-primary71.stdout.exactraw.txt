1 Introduction

We study the problem of sampling from the probability measure

[MATH S1.p1.m1] \mu(\mathrm{d}x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
 on 
[MATH S1.p1.m2] \mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

[MATH S1.p1.m3] V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
 satisfies, for 
[MATH S1.p1.m4] 0<\alpha\leq\beta
,

[MATH S1.E1.m1] \alpha I\preceq\nabla^{2}V(x)\preceq\beta I\,,\hskip 20.00003ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\kappa\coloneqq\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\,.

(1.1)

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem (Main theorem; informal).

Assume (1.1), and suppose that the initial law 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m1] \mu_{0}

satisfies the warm start condition in order-
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m2] 2
 Rényi divergence: 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m3] D_{2}(\mu_{0}\|\mu)=O(1)
. For every

[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m4] 0<\varepsilon<1/4
, there is a randomized algorithm that, given

[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m5] X_{0}\sim\mu_{0}
 and query access to 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m6] \nabla V
, returns

[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m7] \widehat{X}\sim\widehat{\mu}
 satisfying

[MATH S1.Ex1.m1] \operatorname{TV}(\widehat{\mu},\mu)\leq\varepsilon\,,

and whose expected number of queries to 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m8] \nabla V
 is at most

[MATH S1.Ex2.m1] \widetilde{O}\bigl(\sqrt{\kappa}\,d^{1/4}\bigr)\,.

Here 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m9] \widetilde{O}
 hides universal powers of logarithms in

[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m10] d
, 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m11] \kappa
, and 
[MATH Thmunnumberedtheoremx1.p1.m12] 1/\varepsilon
.

The precise statement and parameter choices appear in
Theorems 4.3 and 4.4.

We compare our result with high-accuracy samplers, that is,
samplers whose complexity depends polylogarithmically on

[MATH S1.p3.m1] 1/\varepsilon
. Under (1.1), the state of the art
until recently was the Metropolis-adjusted Langevin algorithm
(MALA) [1, 10, 27]
and the proximal sampler [6, 15], which achieved

[MATH S1.p3.m2] \widetilde{O}(\kappa d^{1/2})
 complexity.
Very recently, Chen et al. [8] used the first-order
rejection sampling (FORS) mechanism to develop a method for exact
simulation of diffusions. Together with accelerated entropic
hypocoercivity [19, 22], this yielded
a high-accuracy sampler with complexity

[MATH S1.p3.m3] \widetilde{O}(\kappa^{2/3}d^{1/3})
.
Also, Lu and Luo [20] introduced windowed thinning,
an exact simulation method for the bouncy particle sampler (BPS) and the zigzag sampler.
Their estimates give
expected complexities of 
[MATH S1.p3.m4] \widetilde{O}(\kappa^{1/2}d)
 gradient queries
for BPS and 
[MATH S1.p3.m5] \widetilde{O}(\kappa d^{5/4})
 partial derivative queries
for zigzag, from a warm start.
These results were significant in part because they constituted progress
toward acceleration for log-concave sampling.

From a warm start, our
main theorem achieves complexity

[MATH S1.p4.m1] \widetilde{O}(\kappa^{1/2}d^{1/4})
. It matches the condition number
dependence of BPS while improving its dimension
dependence, and always improves upon the computational cost of the zigzag sampler (even if we consider one gradient query to be 
[MATH S1.p4.m2] d
 times as expensive as a partial derivative query).
It also improves over all other prior results in both parameters. In particular, it achieves full
acceleration together with 
[MATH S1.p4.m3] d^{1/4}
 dependence on dimension.

In a companion work [7], we will show how to obtain a warm start using

[MATH S1.p5.m1] \widetilde{O}(\kappa^{7/6}d^{1/6})
 gradient queries. We do not discuss the warm start
question further in this paper.

Proximal Bouncy Particle Sampler.

Our algorithm, the Proximal Bouncy Particle Sampler (Proximal
BPS), combines two ideas. The proximal sampler [17]
breaks the sampling problem into a sequence of easier local problems
by introducing a Gaussian perturbation of the current position.
The bouncy particle sampler [5] explores a distribution
by moving a particle along straight lines, with random changes of direction
chosen to preserve the desired distribution. We bring these ideas
together by designing local particle motions that retain a memory of
previous steps. This memory acts as momentum, allowing the algorithm
to make sustained progress across successive local problems and giving
the accelerated convergence rate in our main theorem. The local motions
follow explicitly computable curved paths, with occasional “bounces”
that correct for the shape of the target distribution. A gradient
evaluation at a nearby reference point makes these corrections inexpensive:
since the gradient varies little over a small region, only a small
number of bounces is needed. Balancing the size of the local problems
against the cost of these corrections yields the

[MATH S1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.m1] \widetilde{O}(\kappa^{1/2}d^{1/4})
 query bound. We feel that the construction of the sampler (with the help of GPT-5.6 Sol) contains some surprising ingredients.
We describe the construction and its geometry in
Section 3.1, and give the implementation and complexity
analysis in Section 4.

Related work.

This work makes progress on the complexity of log-concave sampling, for which the textbook [11] provides an introduction and overview.

The proximal sampling framework is from Lee, Shen, and
Tian [17], with subsequent analyses and implementations
in [6, 9, 15]. These methods
alternate independent draws of the position and the auxiliary variable
from their conditional distributions. Our update reflects the auxiliary
point through the current position, a step related to the Gaussian
overrelaxation method of Neal [25]. We combine
this reflection with a local piecewise deterministic particle motion run for half of its
oscillation period. As we shall explain, this helps useful information to persist across iterations.

The rule for the bounces builds on the BPS of Bouchard-Côté,
Vollmer, and Doucet [5]. The use of a quadratic
reference potential to generate curved paths, with bounces correcting
for the remaining force, is adapted from the boomerang sampler of
Bierkens, Grazzi, Kamatani, and Roberts [4]. This separation
of the force also fits the general framework for Hamiltonian piecewise
deterministic Markov processes of Andrieu, Durmus, Nüsken, and
Roussel [2].

Existing convergence analyses of these processes
[2, 13, 21] concern continuous-time dynamics
with repeated random refreshment of the velocity. Our local motion has
no such refreshment: it stops after a half-period, and convergence
comes from combining it with the auxiliary reflection step with occasional
resampling. In particular, the local motion alone need not converge to
its conditional equilibrium. Thus, the continuous-time results do not
directly give a convergence guarantee for our discrete chain.
Our analysis instead adapts the modified 
[MATH S1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p4.m1] L^{2}
 hypocoercivity method of
Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser [14], in the sharpened formulation
of Fan, Li, and Lu [16], to this discrete setting. The technical details of the hypocoercivity mechanism in Appendices B and C should be of interest to specialists.

AI usage.

The algorithm and its original analysis were found by interactions with GPT-5.6 Sol. The authors recast the arguments into a more standard hypocoercive framework, prepared the manuscript, and take full responsibility for its contents.

B.3 Modified 
[MATH A2.SS3.m1] L^{2}
 contraction and proof of the main theorem

We now prove Theorem 3.5 using a discrete modified 
[MATH A2.SS3.p1.m1] L^{2}

argument. For 
[MATH A2.SS3.p1.m2] f\in L_{0}^{2}(\pi_{\eta})
, write

[MATH A2.Ex6.m1] f_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}f\,,\hskip 20.00003ptf_{\perp}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}f\,.

Here 
[MATH A2.SS3.p1.m3] f_{\mathsf{P}}
 and 
[MATH A2.SS3.p1.m4] f_{\perp}
 are, respectively, the macroscopic
and microscopic parts of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p1.m5] f
.

Fix 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m1] 0<\rho\leq 1/2
. The ordinary 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m2] L^{2}
 energy does not by itself give
the required one-step contraction. Indeed, since 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m3] \mathsf{U}
 is unitary and

[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m4] \mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}
 is a contraction,

[MATH A2.Ex7.m1] \displaystyle\|\mathsf{K}f\|^{2}-\|f\|^{2}

[MATH A2.Ex7.m2] \displaystyle=(1-\rho)^{2}\,\|\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp}\|^{2}-\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}

[MATH A2.E18.m1] \displaystyle\leq-\rho\,(2-\rho)\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\leq-\rho\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\,.

(B.18)

This estimate controls 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m5] f_{\perp}
 but gives nothing when

[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m6] f=f_{\mathsf{P}}
.
Reflection moves part of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m7] f_{\mathsf{P}}
 into the
microscopic subspace, but this transfer is not captured by the 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m8] L^{2}
 norm,
which is a standard difficulty in hypocoercivity. The modified 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m9] L^{2}

method addresses this difficulty by adding a corrector to the Lyapunov
function. The corrector records the transfer from 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m10] f_{\mathsf{P}}
 to

[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m11] f_{\perp}
, allowing the contraction in
(B.18) to also control 
[MATH A2.SS3.p2.m12] f_{\mathsf{P}}
.
This idea goes back to Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser [14]; here we
follow the refinement of Fan, Li, and
Lu [16].

To construct the corrector, we first isolate the part of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m1] f_{\perp}
 that
couples to 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m2] f_{\mathsf{P}}
 under reflection. Define

[MATH A2.Ex8.m1] f_{\mathsf{V}}\coloneqq\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}\,.

By (B.16),

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m3] \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}
, while the component of

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m4] f_{\perp}
 orthogonal to 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m5] \operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}})
 is annihilated by

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m6] \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}
. Since 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m7] \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}
 is an isometry,

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m8] \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}
 is the orthogonal
projection of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m9] f_{\perp}
 onto 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m10] \operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}})
, and hence

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m11] \|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|\leq\|f_{\perp}\|
. We work with 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m12] f_{\mathsf{V}}
, rather
than with this projection itself, because 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m13] f_{\mathsf{V}}
 and

[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m14] f_{\mathsf{P}}
 both belong to 
[MATH A2.SS3.p3.m15] \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}
 and can therefore be
paired in the corrector.

With this notation, let 
[MATH A2.SS3.p4.m1] \omega>0
 be a weight to be chosen below and
define the modified Lyapunov function

[MATH A2.E19.m1] \mathscr{L}_{\omega}(f)\coloneqq\|f\|^{2}+\omega\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})\,,

(B.19)

where the corrector is

[MATH A2.E20.m1] \mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\,\bigl(\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}-\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\bigr)-\bigl\langle\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}}\bigr\rangle\,.

(B.20)

Here 
[MATH A2.SS3.p4.m2] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}
 is applied only on 
[MATH A2.SS3.p4.m3] \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}
, where

[MATH A2.SS3.p4.m4] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\succeq\gamma_{\rm gap}I
, so the corrector is well-defined.

To motivate this choice, recall from (B.7) that

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m1] \mathsf{K}
 acts in two stages: it first maps

[MATH A2.Ex9.m1] f_{\mathsf{P}}+f_{\perp}\longmapsto f_{\mathsf{P}}+(1-\rho)\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp}

and then applies 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m2] \mathsf{U}
. For the moment, set 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m3] \rho=0
 and replace

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m4] \mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}
 by 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m5] -I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}}
. The error

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m6] I+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}
 introduced by this replacement is controlled by
Proposition B.2. Denote the resulting idealized operator
by

[MATH A2.Ex10.m1] \mathsf{K}_{\rm id}\coloneqq\mathsf{U}(\mathsf{P}-\mathsf{P}^{\perp})\,.

Set 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m7] g\coloneqq\mathsf{K}_{\rm id}f
, and define the corresponding components by

[MATH A2.Ex11.m1] g_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}g\,,\hskip 20.00003ptg_{\mathsf{V}}\coloneqq\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}g\,.

In terms of these components, 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m8] \mathsf{K}_{\rm id}
 is represented by the
operator-valued rotation matrix

[MATH A2.Ex12.m1] \begin{pmatrix}g_{\mathsf{P}}\\
g_{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\\
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{\mathsf{P}}\\
f_{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

Indeed, the first component follows immediately from the block representation
of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m9] \mathsf{U}
 and the polar decomposition
(B.16):

[MATH A2.Ex13.m1] \displaystyle g_{\mathsf{P}}

[MATH A2.Ex13.m2] \displaystyle=\mathsf{P}\mathsf{U}(f_{\mathsf{P}}-f_{\perp})=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,.

For the second component, substitute

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m10] \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}
 into

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m11] \mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}
 from
(B.5). Since 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m12] \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}
 commutes with

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m13] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}
, this gives

[MATH A2.Ex14.m1] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\,.

The ranges of 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m14] \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}
 and

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m15] \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}
 lie in 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m16] \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}
, where

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m17] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}
 is bounded. Applying 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m18] \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}
 to the
displayed identity therefore gives

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m19] \mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}
. Hence

[MATH A2.Ex15.m1] \displaystyle g_{\mathsf{V}}

[MATH A2.Ex15.m2] \displaystyle=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{U}(f_{\mathsf{P}}-f_{\perp})=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}(\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp})=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,.

This proves the claimed matrix formula. Since

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m20] \mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}+\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}=I
, the block matrix acts as a rotation on

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m21] \mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}
. Under this rotation,
direct substitution into (B.20) gives

[MATH A2.Ex16.m1] \displaystyle\mathscr{C}(g_{\mathsf{P}},g_{\mathsf{V}})-\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})

[MATH A2.Ex17.m1] \displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\mathscr{C}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,,\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}})-\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})

[MATH A2.E21.m1] \displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=-\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}+\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\,.

(B.21)

For 
[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m22] 0<\omega<\rho
, combining the leading terms from
(B.18) and
(B.21), and using

[MATH A2.SS3.p5.m23] \|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|\leq\|f_{\perp}\|
, gives

[MATH A2.Ex18.m1] \displaystyle-\rho\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}+\omega\,\bigl(-\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}+\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\bigr)\leq-\omega\,\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}-(\rho-\omega)\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\,.

 hypocoercivity

D.1 Hilbert space and Markov operator conventions

Definition D.1 (Hilbert space notation).

Let 
[MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m1] \zeta
 be a probability measure. The real Hilbert space

[MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m2] L^{2}(\zeta)
 consists of equivalence classes of measurable functions

[MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m3] f
 such that 
[MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m4] \int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<\infty
. Its inner product and
norm are

[MATH A4.E1.m1] \langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\int fg\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|f\|_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}^{1/2}\,.
