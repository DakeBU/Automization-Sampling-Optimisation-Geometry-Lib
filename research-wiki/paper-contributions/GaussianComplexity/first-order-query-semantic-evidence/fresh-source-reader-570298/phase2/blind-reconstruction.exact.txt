For every pair of natural numbers d,n with 3 <= n <= d, every real number kappa >= 4, and every family (O_j) indexed by j = 0,...,q-1 of real orthogonal n-by-n matrices, where q = floor(d/n) and r = d mod n, the following holds. Put alpha = (kappa+1)/2 and beta = (kappa-1)/4. Identify the d coordinates with q full blocks of n coordinates followed by r remaining coordinates: the local coordinate i of block j has global coordinate j*n+i, and remaining coordinate i has global coordinate q*n+i. Define B_j = alpha I_n - beta (O_j + O_j^T), let A be the d-by-d matrix with these q diagonal blocks and the identity I_r on the remaining coordinates, with all intervening blocks zero, and define f(x) = (x^T A x)/2 on real Euclidean d-space. For v in real Euclidean n-space define Q(j,v,false) = O_j v and Q(j,v,true) = O_j^T v. For x in real Euclidean d-space, let x_j be its vector of coordinates in full block j and define g_j(x) = alpha x_j - beta (Q(j,x_j,false) + Q(j,x_j,true)). Define g(x) by placing g_j(x) in full block j and copying x on the r remaining coordinates. Then, for every x in real Euclidean d-space, f has a Frechet derivative at x represented by g(x), namely h maps to the Euclidean inner product of g(x) with h, and the simultaneous equality (f(x), gradient f(x)) = ((inner product of x with g(x))/2, g(x)) holds. In particular, the derivative and gradient assertions are conclusions for every x. The orthogonal matrices may have either determinant sign; the assertion is universal over these families.
