3.2 The ideal conditional half-turn process

Fix $Y=y$ . Recall from (2.8) that the position
$X$ to be sampled has conditional law

$\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-V_{y}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\hskip 20.00003ptV_{y}(x)\coloneqq V(x)+\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\eta}\,.$

We construct the half-turn process so that its induced transition
preserves this law. The
construction starts from vanilla BPS for the conditional target, absorbs
the quadratic coupling potential into the deterministic motion, and then
introduces
an independent conditional reference point to localize the remaining
bounce rate. We begin by introducing a Gaussian momentum
$p\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ . The phase space law to be preserved is

$\nu_{\eta,y}(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p)\coloneqq\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)(\mathrm{d}p)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V_{y}(x)-\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p\,.$

(3.1)

Recall from (2.4) that $R_{h}$ denotes specular
reflection across the hyperplane orthogonal to $h$ .

A natural first attempt is to apply vanilla BPS directly to this
conditional distribution. Its infinitesimal generator acts on a smooth
test function $f$ as

$\begin{split}(\mathsf{L}_{y}^{\rm vanilla}f)(x,p)&=\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}\nabla V_{y}(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{\nabla V_{y}(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split}$

(3.2)

The common factor $\sqrt{\eta}$ only fixes the unit of time; it does not
change the invariant law. We choose this unit so that the harmonic motion
introduced below has period $2\pi$ .
The first term gives straight-line motion, while the second reflects the
momentum against the full conditional gradient. This process is exact,
but it can be expensive: even when $V=0$ , the quadratic coupling potential
$\|{\cdot}-y\|^{2}/(2\eta)$ produces a stationary mean bounce rate of order
$\sqrt{d}$ .

To reduce the number of bounce events, we put the quadratic part of the
conditional gradient into the deterministic dynamics. This gives the
generator

$\begin{split}(\mathsf{L}_{y}^{\rm harm}f)(x,p)&=\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)-\frac{(x-y)^{\top}}{\sqrt{\eta}}\nabla_{p}f(x,p)\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}\nabla V(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{\nabla V(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split}$

(3.3)

The first line is the Hamiltonian flow associated with the quadratic
coupling potential: between bounces, it rotates $(x-y)/\sqrt{\eta}$ and
$p$ . This use of a quadratic reference flow, with the remaining force
handled by bounce events, is analogous to the Boomerang
sampler [4].
The direct harmonic generator (3.3)
still bounces against the full gradient $\nabla V$ , so its bounce rate
can remain large.

To further reduce the bounce rate and the resulting computational
cost, we introduce a conditional reference point and use the smoothness of
$V$ to control the local gradient difference. Specifically, draw
$\widetilde{X}\sim\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}$ independently and write the exact
decomposition

$\nabla V(x)=\nabla V(\widetilde{X})+\{\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{X})\}\,.$

Conditionally on $\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{x}$ , we incorporate the fixed
reference term into the deterministic dynamics and retain only the gradient
difference in the BPS jump term. Define the equilibrium position of this
deterministic harmonic flow and the residual gradient by

$c_{y,\widetilde{x}}\coloneqq y-\eta\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\,,\hskip 20.00003pth_{\widetilde{x}}(x)\coloneqq\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\,.$

(3.4)

The resulting generator is

$\begin{split}\mathsf{L}_{y,\widetilde{x}}f(x,p)&\coloneqq\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)-\frac{(x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})^{\top}}{\sqrt{\eta}}\nabla_{p}f(x,p)\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)]_{+}\bigl\{f(x,R_{h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)}p)-f(x,p)\bigr\}\,.\end{split}$

(3.5)

The harmonic and residual terms sum to the full conditional force
$\sqrt{\eta}\,\nabla V_{y}(x)$ . Thus $\widetilde{x}$ changes only how this
force is divided between deterministic motion and bounces. By the
$\beta$ -smoothness of $V$ ,

$\|h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)\|=\|\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{x})\|\leq\beta\,\|x-\widetilde{x}\|\,.$

Drawing the reference point from the same localized conditional law keeps
the residual gradient small and reduces the expected number of bounces per
run of the half-turn process to order $\beta\eta\sqrt{d}$ ; see
Proposition 3.3.

Having specified the dynamics, it remains to choose the integration
horizon. For fixed $(y,\widetilde{x})$ , the deterministic part of
(3.5) is harmonic motion about its equilibrium
position $c_{y,\widetilde{x}}$ . In the absence of bounces, its position at
time $t$ satisfies

$x_{t}-c_{y,\widetilde{x}}=(x_{0}-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})\cos t+\sqrt{\eta}\,p_{0}\sin t\,.$

(3.6)

As discussed earlier, we choose the integration horizon $t=\pi$ , corresponding to one
half-period. This is the first non-zero harmonic time at which the
position $x_{\pi}$ no longer depends on the initial momentum $p_{0}$ .
Without bounces, the resulting map is

$(x_{\pi},p_{\pi})=(2c_{y,\widetilde{x}}-x_{0},-p_{0})\,.$

(3.7)

Thus, the position is reflected through the equilibrium position
$c_{y,\widetilde{x}}$ and retains its dependence on $x_{0}$ . Bounce
events correct for the residual force along the trajectory, while the
fixed horizon $t=\pi$ retains the memory-preserving structure of the
half-turn process.

The construction is now complete. The following algorithm gives its
pathwise prescription: it draws the reference point and momentum, evolves
the process with generator (3.5) for time $\pi$ , and
returns only the final position.

Algorithm 1 Simulation of the ideal conditional half-turn process

1:

Input: state $(x,y)$

2:

Draw $\widetilde{X}\sim\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}$ and
$p_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ independently.

3:

Query $\nabla V(\widetilde{X})$ .

4:

Set $x_{0}\leftarrow x$ .

5:

Evolve $(x_{t},p_{t})$ according to

$\dot{x}=\sqrt{\eta}\,p\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\dot{p}=-\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{\eta}}-\sqrt{\eta}\,\nabla V(\widetilde{X})\,,$

(3.8)

until $t=\pi$ .

6:

During the evolution, generate bounce events at rate

$\lambda_{\widetilde{X}}(x,p)=\sqrt{\eta}\,\bigl[p^{\top}[\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(\widetilde{X})]\bigr]_{+}\,.$

(3.9)

7:

At each bounce, set
$p_{t}\leftarrow R_{\nabla V(x_{t})-\nabla V(\widetilde{X})}p_{t}$ .

8:

return $x_{\pi}$

Proposition 3.1 (Well-posedness and stationarity of the conditional phase space process).

For every fixed $y,\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and initial phase space point
$(x_{0},p_{0})$ , the event-driven dynamics in Algorithm 1, with
the reference point fixed at $\widetilde{x}$ , defines a unique
non-explosive, time-homogeneous Markov process. The phase space measure
$\nu_{\eta,y}$ is stationary for this process. In particular, the
returned position $x_{\pi}$ is well-defined almost surely.

The well-posedness and stationarity assertions are proved in
Section A.1.

The induced Markov kernel on positions also satisfies the following
reversibility property.

Proposition 3.2 (Reversibility of the conditional half-turn kernel).

For every $y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , let $\mathcal{H}_{y}$ be the Markov kernel on
$\mathbb{R}^{d}$ induced by Algorithm 1; thus
$\mathcal{H}_{y}(x,\cdot)$ is the law of the returned position $x_{\pi}$
for input $(x,y)$ . Then $\mathcal{H}_{y}$ is reversible with respect to
$\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y}$ .

The proof is deferred to Appendix A.2.

The following proposition quantifies the stationary bounce count of the
ideal half-turn process.

Proposition 3.3 (Stationary bounce count).

There exists a universal constant $K_{\rm id}\geq 1$ such that the
following holds. Suppose that $(x_{0},Y)\sim\pi_{\eta}$ . Conditional on
$Y$ , let $\widetilde{X}\sim\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y}$ be independent of
$x_{0}$ , and let $p_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ be independent of all position
variables. If $N_{\rm b}^{\rm id}$ denotes the number of bounce events
in the resulting ideal half-turn process of Algorithm 1, then

$\mathbb{E}N_{\rm b}^{\rm id}\leq K_{\rm id}\beta\eta\sqrt{d}\,.$
