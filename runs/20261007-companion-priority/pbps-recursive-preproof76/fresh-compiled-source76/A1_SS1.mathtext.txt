A.1 The fixed-reference phase space process

Proof of the well-posedness assertion in
Proposition 3.1.

Fix $y,\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ . In the notation of
(3.4), the bounce rate
(3.9) reads

$\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p)=\sqrt{\eta}\,[p^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)]_{+}\,.$

The deterministic harmonic flow, namely the solution
(4.5) of (3.8), and
the bounce map are

$\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(x,p)$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigl(c_{y,\widetilde{x}}+(x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})\cos t+\sqrt{\eta}\,p\sin t,\;-\eta^{-1/2}(x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})\sin t+p\cos t\bigr)\,,$

$\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p)$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq(x,R_{h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)}p)\,,\hskip 20.00003ptR_{0}\coloneqq I\,.$

The bounce map is an involution. Although there is a possible discontinuity at $h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)=0$ , this creates no ambiguity
because the event rate vanishes there.

Let $(E_{n})_{n\geq 1}$ be independent $\operatorname{Exp}(1)$
random variables. Starting from
$\zeta_{0}\coloneqq(x_{0},p_{0})$ at time $T_{0}\coloneqq 0$ , define recursively

$\displaystyle S_{n+1}$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\Bigl\{u\geq 0:\int_{0}^{u}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\Phi_{s}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{T_{n}}))\,\mathrm{d}s\geq E_{n+1}\Bigr\}\,,$

(A.1)

$\displaystyle T_{n+1}$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq T_{n}+S_{n+1}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\zeta_{T_{n+1}}\coloneqq\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}\bigl(\Phi_{S_{n+1}}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{T_{n}})\bigr)\,,$

(A.2)

when $S_{n+1}<\infty$ . If the set is empty, set
$T_{n+1}=S_{n+1}=\infty$ and stop the recursion. Between jumps, set
$\zeta_{T_{n}+t}=\Phi_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{T_{n}})$ for
$0\leq t<S_{n+1}$ .

To prove non-explosion, define the shifted harmonic energy

$H_{y,\widetilde{x}}(x,p)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\,\bigl\{\eta^{-1}\,\|x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}}\|^{2}+\|p\|^{2}\bigr\}\,.$

The flow and each bounce preserve this energy. Hence, with
$\mathcal{E}\coloneqq H_{y,\widetilde{x}}(x_{0},p_{0})$ , every state produced by
the recursion satisfies

$\|p\|\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}}\|\leq\sqrt{2\eta\mathcal{E}}\,.$

Because $\nabla V$ is $\beta$ -Lipschitz,
$\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p)\leq\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})$ throughout the path, where

$\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})\coloneqq\sqrt{\eta}\,\beta\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}}\,\bigl\{\sqrt{2\eta\mathcal{E}}+\|c_{y,\widetilde{x}}-\widetilde{x}\|\bigr\}\,.$

Hence,

$\int_{0}^{u}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\Phi_{s}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{T_{n}}))\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})\,u\,.$

If $\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})=0$ , the event rate
vanishes and there are no jumps. Otherwise,
(A.1) implies, for every finite waiting time,

$S_{n+1}\geq\frac{E_{n+1}}{\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})}\,.$

If the recursion does not terminate, it follows that

$T_{n}\geq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}}{\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})}\longrightarrow\infty$

almost surely by the strong law of large numbers. Thus, the jump times
cannot accumulate, and the recursive construction defines a unique
process for all $t\geq 0$ . Finally, the
memoryless property of the exponential clocks gives the time-homogeneous
Markov property. This is precisely the integrated hazard construction
of Davis [12, Section 2].
∎

With the process now constructed, we prove the stationarity assertion in
Proposition 3.1 and record the stronger operator facts used
to analyze the averaged kernel. Recall $\nu_{\eta,y}$ from
(3.1), and define momentum
reversal by

$\mathsf{R}(x,p)\coloneqq(x,-p)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt(\mathsf{R}F)(x,p)\coloneqq F(\mathsf{R}(x,p))\,.$

As usual, we use the same symbol for $\nu_{\eta,y}$ and its Lebesgue
density:

$\nu_{\eta,y}(x,p)\propto\exp\Bigl\{-V_{y}(x)-\frac{\|p\|^{2}}{2}\Bigr\}\,.$

Proposition A.1 (Path reversal, invariance, and the $L^{2}$ semigroup).

Let $(\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}})_{t\geq 0}$ be the Markov transition
operators of the process constructed in
Proposition 3.1. For every $t\geq 0$ ,

$(\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}})^{*}=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }L^{2}(\nu_{\eta,y})\,.$

(A.3)

Consequently:

(i)

$\nu_{\eta,y}$ is invariant;

(ii)

$(\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}})_{t\geq 0}$ is a strongly continuous
contraction semigroup on $L^{2}(\nu_{\eta,y})$ ;

(iii)

if $\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}:L^{2}(\nu_{\eta,y})\to L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y})$ denotes Gaussian momentum averaging, then
$\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}$ is a
self-adjoint Markov contraction on $L^{2}(\pi_{\eta}^{X\mid Y=y})$ .

Idea of the proof.

The identity (A.3) is a
momentum-reversed form of detailed balance. To see the mechanism
formally, write
$\mathsf{L}_{y,\widetilde{x}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{J}$ , where

$\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x,p)$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}\nabla_{x}f(x,p)-\frac{(x-c_{y,\widetilde{x}})^{\top}}{\sqrt{\eta}}\nabla_{p}f(x,p),$

$\displaystyle\mathcal{J}f(x,p)$
$\displaystyle\coloneqq\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p)\bigl\{f(\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p))-f(x,p)\bigr\}.$

Momentum reversal reverses the harmonic flow, so
$\mathsf{R}\mathcal{A}\mathsf{R}=-\mathcal{A}$ . Moreover,

$\mathcal{A}\log\nu_{\eta,y}(x,p)=-\sqrt{\eta}\,p^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)=-\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p)+\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(x,-p).$

Formal integration by parts gives

$\mathcal{A}^{*}=-\mathcal{A}+\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}-\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}\circ\mathsf{R}.$

The bounce map $\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}$ is a
$\nu_{\eta,y}$ -preserving involution, commutes with $\mathsf{R}$ , and
satisfies $\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}\circ\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}=\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}\circ\mathsf{R}$ . A formal change of variables
therefore gives

$\mathcal{J}^{*}G=(\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}\circ\mathsf{R})(G\circ\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}})-\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}G.$

Combining the two identities yields

$\mathsf{L}_{y,\widetilde{x}}^{*}G=-\mathcal{A}G+(\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}\circ\mathsf{R})(G\circ\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}-G)=(\mathsf{R}\mathsf{L}_{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R})G.$

Formally exponentiating this identity gives
$(\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}})^{*}=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R}$ ; Gaussian momentum averaging
then removes $\mathsf{R}$ and produces an ordinary self-adjoint position
kernel.

This generator calculation is only heuristic as stated, as the standard arguments are not
immediately applicable because
$\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}$ may be discontinuous where
$h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)=0$ . The pathwise argument below proves the same
identity without requiring differentiability or continuity of the
bounce map.

Proof.

Along
$\zeta_{s}=(x_{s},p_{s})=\Phi_{s}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{0})$ ,

$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\log\nu_{\eta,y}(\zeta_{s})=-\sqrt{\eta}\,p_{s}^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x_{s})=-\bigl\{\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{s})-\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\mathsf{R}\zeta_{s})\bigr\}\,.$

(A.4)

Moreover, by definition of $\mathsf{R}$ and $\Phi_{t}$ ,

$\mathsf{R}\Phi_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R}=\Phi_{-t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathsf{R}\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}=\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R}\,.$

If $\zeta^{+}=\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}\zeta^{-}$ is a non-trivial jump, then
$h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)\neq 0$ ,
$(p^{-})^{\top}h_{\widetilde{x}}(x)>0$ , and

$\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\zeta^{-})=\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\mathsf{R}\zeta^{+})\,.$

(A.5)

The flow and $\mathsf{R}$ preserve Lebesgue measure, as does the possibly
discontinuous bounce map: by Fubini’s theorem and orthogonality, for
every integrable $F$ ,

$\int F(\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}(x,p))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p=\int F(x,p)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p\,.$

(A.6)

To pass from these identities to complete trajectories, let

$\Delta_{n}(t)\coloneqq\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}):0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}<t\}\,.$

Given $\zeta_{0}$ and $\mathbf{t}\in\Delta_{n}(t)$ , let
$\zeta_{s}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}}$ be the path obtained by following
$\Phi^{y,\widetilde{x}}$ and applying $\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}$ at the
prescribed times. Iterating the exponential clock construction gives,
for every bounded measurable functional $\Psi$ of the path up to time
$t$ ,

$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\zeta_{0}}\Psi(\zeta_{[0,t]})$
$\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t)}\Psi(\zeta_{[0,t]}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}})\exp\Bigl\{-\!\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{s}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}})\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{t_{j}-}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}\,.$

(A.7)

The series is absolutely convergent because
$\lvert\Delta_{n}(t)\rvert=t^{n}/n!$ and the rate
is bounded by $\bar{\lambda}_{\mathcal{E}}(y,\widetilde{x})$ .
Integrate (A.7) against
$\nu_{\eta,y}(\mathrm{d}\zeta_{0})$ and apply

$(\zeta_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t}\longmapsto(\mathsf{R}\zeta_{t-s})_{0\leq s\leq t}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\longmapsto(t-t_{n},\ldots,t-t_{1})\,.$

(A.8)

For completeness, we justify this change of variables despite the
possible discontinuity of the bounce map. For fixed
$\mathbf{t}\in\Delta_{n}(t)$ , the endpoint map
$\zeta_{0}\mapsto\zeta_{t}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}}$ is a finite composition
of the Borel involution $\mathsf{S}_{\widetilde{x}}$ and the affine
harmonic flows $\Phi_{u}^{y,\widetilde{x}}$ . By
(A.6) and the explicit flow formula, each factor is
a measure-preserving Borel bijection. Hence the endpoint map, and
therefore
$\zeta_{0}\mapsto\mathsf{R}\zeta_{t}^{\zeta_{0},\mathbf{t}}$ , is a Borel
measure-preserving bijection. The identities preceding
(A.7) show that its inverse is obtained by
using the reversed ordered times in (A.8). The
map on $\Delta_{n}(t)$ is linear with absolute determinant one.
Thus the full change of variables is measure preserving; no Jacobian is needed.

Integrating (A.4) over the deterministic arcs
and using that a reflection preserves $\nu_{\eta,y}$ gives

$\nu_{\eta,y}(\zeta_{0})\,e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s}=\nu_{\eta,y}(\zeta_{t})\,e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\mathsf{R}\zeta_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s}\,.$

This identity matches the initial density and survival factor of a
forward path with those of its reversal, while
(A.5) matches every jump-rate factor. Hence,
each $n$ -jump term in (A.7) is unchanged by
(A.8). Tonelli’s theorem justifies the termwise
change of variables for non-negative bounded functions; decomposing
signed functions into positive and negative parts then gives, for
bounded measurable $F,G$ ,

$\int F\,\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}G\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\eta,y}=\int(\mathsf{R}G)\,\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}(\mathsf{R}F)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\eta,y}\,.$

Taking $F=1$ proves invariance, and the identity extends by density to
(A.3) on $L^{2}(\nu_{\eta,y})$ .

Invariance and Jensen’s inequality give
$\|\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}F\|_{2}\leq\|F\|_{2}$ . It remains to prove
strong continuity. For $F\in C_{\rm c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})$ , the deterministic flow
converges to the identity as $t\searrow 0$ , and the probability of a jump before time
$t$ is

$1-\exp\Bigl\{-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{\widetilde{x}}(\Phi_{s}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\longrightarrow 0\,.$

Dominated convergence therefore gives
$\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}F\to F$ in $L^{2}$ . Density of
$C_{\rm c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})$ and contractivity extend this to all $F\in L^{2}$ .

Finally, Gaussian momentum averaging satisfies
$\mathsf{R}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}=\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}$ and
$\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}$ . Combining these identities with
(A.3) gives

$(\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*})^{*}=\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}(\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}})^{*}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}=\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}=\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}\mathsf{\Pi}_{p}^{*}\,.$

This proves self-adjointness; the Markov and contraction properties are
inherited from $\mathsf{T}_{t}^{y,\widetilde{x}}$ .
∎
