    Dyadic scalar Brownian quadratic variation converges in mean square

    AutoSamplingTheory.TechnicalLemmas.StochasticProcesses.BrownianQuadraticVariationL2.tendsto_integral_dyadic_quadraticVariationError_sq · theorem · Teaching coverage

    Read this first

    Mathematical statement

    Let B be a real pre-Brownian process and T≥0. On the regular partition of [0,T] into 2^m equal cells, the expected square of the sum of squared increments minus their cell lengths tends to zero as m tends to infinity.

    \[\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2^m-1}((B_{(i+1)T/2^m}-B_{iT/2^m})^2-T/2^m)\right)^2\right]=0\]

    Proof roadmap

    1. 1Specialize the finite-grid identity

      Apply the exact finite-grid L² theorem to the regular grid with 2^m cells and mesh δ_m=T/2^m.

    2. 2Evaluate equal cell lengths

      Every adjacent difference in the regular grid is exactly δ_m. The sum of 2^m equal squared lengths simplifies to Tδ_m.

    3. 3Let the dyadic mesh vanish

      The canonical dyadic mesh tends to zero. Multiplication by the fixed nonnegative scalar 2T preserves the limit, so the exact error moment tends to zero.

    Full assumptions and notation

    All objects and hypotheses

    • B satisfies Samplinglib's real pre-Brownian contract under μ.
    • T is an arbitrary nonnegative real horizon; the theorem includes T=0.
    • The grids are canonical deterministic dyadic regular grids. No filtration, adaptedness, usual conditions, or positive-horizon assumption is required.

    Mathematical proof

    1. Specialize the finite-grid identity

    Apply the exact finite-grid L² theorem to the regular grid with 2^m cells and mesh δ_m=T/2^m.

    \[\mathbb E[Q_m^2]=2\sum_{i=0}^{2^m-1}\delta_m^2\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use integral_quadraticVariationError_sq with regularGridTimes_monotone.

    2. Evaluate equal cell lengths

    Every adjacent difference in the regular grid is exactly δ_m. The sum of 2^m equal squared lengths simplifies to Tδ_m.

    \[\sum_{i=0}^{2^m-1}\delta_m^2=T\delta_m\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Use regularGridTimes_cellLength and sum_dyadic_cellLengths_sq.

    3. Let the dyadic mesh vanish

    The canonical dyadic mesh tends to zero. Multiplication by the fixed nonnegative scalar 2T preserves the limit, so the exact error moment tends to zero.

    \[2T\delta_m\longrightarrow 0\]
    Corresponding Lean step

    Apply FiniteTimeGrid.dyadicMesh_tendsto_zero T and Tendsto.const_mul.

    The proof rewrites the exact finite-grid second moment as 2T times the dyadic mesh and passes to the known zero-mesh limit.

    theorem tendsto_integral_dyadic_quadraticVariationError_sq
    (hB : IsPreBrownianReal B mu) (T : ℝ≥0) :
    Tendsto
      (fun level =>
        ∫ omega,
          (quadraticVariationError B
            (regularGridTimes (dyadicMesh T level) (2 ^ level)) omega) ^ 2 ∂mu)
      atTop (nhds 0) := by
  have hexact : ∀ level : ℕ,
      (∫ omega,
          (quadraticVariationError B
            (regularGridTimes (dyadicMesh T level) (2 ^ level)) omega) ^ 2 ∂mu) =
        2 * (T : ℝ) * ((dyadicMesh T level : ℝ≥0) : ℝ) := by
    intro level
    rw [integral_quadraticVariationError_sq hB
      (regularGridTimes_monotone
        (dyadicMesh T level) (2 ^ level))]
    rw [sum_dyadic_cellLengths_sq]
    ring
  have hlimit := (FiniteTimeGrid.dyadicMesh_tendsto_zero T).const_mul (2 * (T : ℝ))
  have hfun :
      (fun level =>
        ∫ omega,
          (quadraticVariationError B
            (regularGridTimes (dyadicMesh T level) (2 ^ level)) omega) ^ 2 ∂mu) =
        fun level => 2 * (T : ℝ) * ((dyadicMesh T level : ℝ≥0) : ℝ) := by
    funext level
    exact hexact level
  rw [hfun]
  simpa using hlimit
