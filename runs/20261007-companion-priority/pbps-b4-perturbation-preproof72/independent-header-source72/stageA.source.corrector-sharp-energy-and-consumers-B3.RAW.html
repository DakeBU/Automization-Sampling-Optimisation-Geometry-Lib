B.3 Modified L2L^{2} contraction and proof of the main theorem

We now prove Theorem 3.5 using a discrete modified L2L^{2} argument. For f∈L02​(πη)f\in L_{0}^{2}(\pi_{\eta}), write

f𝖯≔𝖯​f,f⟂≔𝖯⟂​f.f_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}f\,,\hskip 20.00003ptf_{\perp}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}^{\perp}f\,.

Here f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}} and f⟂f_{\perp} are, respectively, the macroscopic and microscopic parts of ff.

Fix 0<ρ≤1/20<\rho\leq 1/2. The ordinary L2L^{2} energy does not by itself give the required one-step contraction. Indeed, since 𝖴\mathsf{U} is unitary and 𝖧⟂⁣⟂\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp} is a contraction,

‖𝖪​f‖2−‖f‖2\displaystyle\|\mathsf{K}f\|^{2}-\|f\|^{2} =(1−ρ)2​‖𝖧⟂⁣⟂​f⟂‖2−‖f⟂‖2\displaystyle=(1-\rho)^{2}\,\|\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp}\|^{2}-\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}
≤−ρ⁡(2−ρ)​‖f⟂‖2≤−ρ​‖f⟂‖2.\displaystyle\leq-\rho\,(2-\rho)\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\leq-\rho\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\,. (B.18)

This estimate controls f⟂f_{\perp} but gives nothing when f=f𝖯f=f_{\mathsf{P}}. Reflection moves part of f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}} into the microscopic subspace, but this transfer is not captured by the L2L^{2} norm, which is a standard difficulty in hypocoercivity. The modified L2L^{2} method addresses this difficulty by adding a corrector to the Lyapunov function. The corrector records the transfer from f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}} to f⟂f_{\perp}, allowing the contraction in (B.18) to also control f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}}. This idea goes back to Dolbeault, Mouhot, and Schmeiser [14]; here we follow the refinement of Fan, Li, and Lu [16].

To construct the corrector, we first isolate the part of f⟂f_{\perp} that couples to f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}} under reflection. Define

f𝖵≔𝖵⟂𝖯∗​f⟂.f_{\mathsf{V}}\coloneqq\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}\,.

By (B.16), 𝖴⟂𝖯∗​f⟂=Γ𝖯​f𝖵\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}, while the component of f⟂f_{\perp} orthogonal to ran⁡(𝖵⟂𝖯)\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}) is annihilated by 𝖴⟂𝖯∗\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}. Since 𝖵⟂𝖯\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}} is an isometry, 𝖵⟂𝖯​f𝖵=𝖵⟂𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗​f⟂\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp} is the orthogonal projection of f⟂f_{\perp} onto ran⁡(𝖵⟂𝖯)\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}), and hence ‖f𝖵‖≤‖f⟂‖\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|\leq\|f_{\perp}\|. We work with f𝖵f_{\mathsf{V}}, rather than with this projection itself, because f𝖵f_{\mathsf{V}} and f𝖯f_{\mathsf{P}} both belong to ℌ𝖯,0\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0} and can therefore be paired in the corrector.

With this notation, let ω>0\omega>0 be a weight to be chosen below and define the modified Lyapunov function

ℒω​(f)≔‖f‖2+ω​𝒞​(f𝖯,f𝖵),\mathscr{L}_{\omega}(f)\coloneqq\|f\|^{2}+\omega\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})\,, (B.19)

where the corrector is

𝒞⁡(f𝖯,f𝖵)≔12​(‖f𝖯‖2−‖f𝖵‖2)−⟨𝖴𝖯𝖯​Γ𝖯−1​f𝖯,f𝖵⟩.\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\,\bigl(\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}-\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\bigr)-\bigl\langle\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}}\bigr\rangle\,. (B.20)

Here Γ𝖯−1\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1} is applied only on ℌ𝖯,0\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}, where Γ𝖯⪰γgap​I\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\succeq\gamma_{\rm gap}I, so the corrector is well-defined.

To motivate this choice, recall from (B.7) that 𝖪\mathsf{K} acts in two stages: it first maps

f𝖯+f⟂⟼f𝖯+(1−ρ)​𝖧⟂⁣⟂​f⟂f_{\mathsf{P}}+f_{\perp}\longmapsto f_{\mathsf{P}}+(1-\rho)\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp}

and then applies 𝖴\mathsf{U}. For the moment, set ρ=0\rho=0 and replace 𝖧⟂⁣⟂\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp} by −Iℌ⟂-I_{\mathfrak{H}_{\perp}}. The error I+𝖧⟂⁣⟂I+\mathsf{H}_{\perp\perp} introduced by this replacement is controlled by Proposition B.2. Denote the resulting idealized operator by

𝖪id≔𝖴⁡(𝖯−𝖯⟂).\mathsf{K}_{\rm id}\coloneqq\mathsf{U}(\mathsf{P}-\mathsf{P}^{\perp})\,.

Set g≔𝖪id​fg\coloneqq\mathsf{K}_{\rm id}f, and define the corresponding components by

g𝖯≔𝖯​g,g𝖵≔𝖵⟂𝖯∗​𝖯⟂​g.g_{\mathsf{P}}\coloneqq\mathsf{P}g\,,\hskip 20.00003ptg_{\mathsf{V}}\coloneqq\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}g\,.

In terms of these components, 𝖪id\mathsf{K}_{\rm id} is represented by the operator-valued rotation matrix

(g𝖯g𝖵)=(𝖴𝖯𝖯−Γ𝖯Γ𝖯𝖴𝖯𝖯)​(f𝖯f𝖵).\begin{pmatrix}g_{\mathsf{P}}\\ g_{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}&-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\\ \mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}&\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{\mathsf{P}}\\ f_{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

Indeed, the first component follows immediately from the block representation of 𝖴\mathsf{U} and the polar decomposition (B.16):

g𝖯\displaystyle g_{\mathsf{P}} =𝖯𝖴⁡(f𝖯−f⟂)=𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖯−𝖴⟂𝖯∗​f⟂=𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖯−Γ𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗​f⟂=𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖯−Γ𝖯​f𝖵.\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}\mathsf{U}(f_{\mathsf{P}}-f_{\perp})=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,.

For the second component, substitute 𝖴⟂𝖯∗=Γ𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*} into 𝖴⟂𝖯∗​𝖴⟂⁣⟂=−𝖴𝖯𝖯​𝖴⟂𝖯∗\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*} from (B.5). Since 𝖴𝖯𝖯\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}} commutes with Γ𝖯\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}, this gives

Γ𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗​𝖴⟂⁣⟂=−Γ𝖯​𝖴𝖯𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗.\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\,.

The ranges of 𝖵⟂𝖯∗​𝖴⟂⁣⟂\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp} and 𝖴𝖯𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*} lie in ℌ𝖯,0\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}, where Γ𝖯−1\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1} is bounded. Applying Γ𝖯−1\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1} to the displayed identity therefore gives 𝖵⟂𝖯∗​𝖴⟂⁣⟂=−𝖴𝖯𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}=-\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}. Hence

g𝖵\displaystyle g_{\mathsf{V}} =𝖵⟂𝖯∗​𝖯⟂​𝖴​(f𝖯−f⟂)=𝖵⟂𝖯∗​(𝖴⟂𝖯​f𝖯−𝖴⟂⁣⟂​f⟂)=Γ𝖯​f𝖯+𝖴𝖯𝖯​𝖵⟂𝖯∗​f⟂=Γ𝖯​f𝖯+𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖵.\displaystyle=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}\mathsf{P}^{\perp}\mathsf{U}(f_{\mathsf{P}}-f_{\perp})=\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}(\mathsf{U}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{U}_{\perp\perp}f_{\perp})=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{V}_{\perp\mathsf{P}}^{*}f_{\perp}=\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,.

This proves the claimed matrix formula. Since 𝖴𝖯𝖯2+Γ𝖯2=I\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}^{2}+\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}^{2}=I, the block matrix acts as a rotation on ℌ𝖯,0⊕ℌ𝖯,0\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{\mathsf{P},0}. Under this rotation, direct substitution into (B.20) gives

𝒞⁡(g𝖯,g𝖵)−𝒞⁡(f𝖯,f𝖵)\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(g_{\mathsf{P}},g_{\mathsf{V}})-\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})
=𝒞⁡(𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖯−Γ𝖯​f𝖵,Γ𝖯​f𝖯+𝖴𝖯𝖯​f𝖵)−𝒞⁡(f𝖯,f𝖵)\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\mathscr{C}(\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}-\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}}\,,\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{P}}+\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{P}\mathsf{P}}f_{\mathsf{V}})-\mathscr{C}(f_{\mathsf{P}},f_{\mathsf{V}})
=−‖f𝖯‖2+‖f𝖵‖2.\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=-\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}+\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\,. (B.21)

For 0<ω<ρ0<\omega<\rho, combining the leading terms from (B.18) and (B.21), and using ‖f𝖵‖≤‖f⟂‖\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|\leq\|f_{\perp}\|, gives

−ρ​‖f⟂‖2+ω⁡(−‖f𝖯‖2+‖f𝖵‖2)≤−ω​‖f𝖯‖2−(ρ−ω)​‖f⟂‖2.\displaystyle-\rho\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}+\omega\,\bigl(-\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}+\|f_{\mathsf{V}}\|^{2}\bigr)\leq-\omega\,\|f_{\mathsf{P}}\|^{2}-(\rho-\omega)\,\|f_{\perp}\|^{2}\,.

Thus the leading terms control both components. This calculation makes explicit the role of the corrector in the modified L2L^{2} hypocoercivity argument; see also Fan, Li, and Lu [16].

We now turn from the idealized calculation to the contraction proof. The modified L2L^{2} argument has two steps: we first show that the Lyapunov function is equivalent to the squared L2L^{2} norm and then establish its one-step decay.
