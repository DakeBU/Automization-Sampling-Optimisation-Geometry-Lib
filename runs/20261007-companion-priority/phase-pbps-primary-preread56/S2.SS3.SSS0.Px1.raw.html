Notation.

For a random variable WW, ℒ⁡(W)\mathcal{L}(W) denotes its law, and for a probability measure PP on a product space, PXP^{X} denotes its XX-marginal. Markov kernels are denoted by calligraphic symbols. If 𝒦\mathcal{K} is a Markov kernel, P​𝒦P\mathcal{K} denotes the law obtained by applying 𝒦\mathcal{K} to PP, and 𝒦m\mathcal{K}^{m} denotes its mm-fold iterate. Linear operators and state-space transformations are denoted by sans-serif symbols, scalar functionals by script letters, and finite-dimensional matrices by ordinary uppercase symbols. We write [u]+≔max⁡{u,0}[u]_{+}\coloneqq\max\{u,0\}. The notation ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\| denotes the Euclidean norm for vectors, the induced operator norm for matrices and bounded linear operators, and the ambient L2L^{2}-norm for functions; inner products are interpreted analogously from context. Unsubscripted letters c,C>0c,C>0 denote universal constants whose values may change from line to line, while constants denoted by KK, with or without a descriptive subscript, are fixed within the result in which they are introduced. The notation O~​(B)\widetilde{O}(B) means BB times a fixed universal power of logarithms in the displayed problem parameters; no polynomial dependence is hidden.
