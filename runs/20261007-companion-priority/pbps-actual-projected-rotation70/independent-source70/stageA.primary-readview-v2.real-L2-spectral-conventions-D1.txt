
D.1 Hilbert space and Markov operator conventions

Definition D.1 (Hilbert space notation).

Let 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m1 RAW[1349584,1349784) \zeta
 be a probability measure. The real Hilbert space

MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m2 RAW[1349834,1350199) L^{2}(\zeta)
 consists of equivalence classes of measurable functions

MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m3 RAW[1350256,1350447) f
 such that 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m4 RAW[1350458,1350929) \int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta<\infty
. Its inner product and
norm are

MATH A4.E1.m1 RAW[1351181,1353032) \langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\int fg\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\|f\|_{L^{2}(\zeta)}\coloneqq\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\zeta)}^{1/2}\,.

(D.1)

We write

MATH A4.E2.m1 RAW[1353541,1354639) L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)\coloneqq\Bigl\{f\in L^{2}(\zeta):\int f\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=0\Bigr\}\,.

(D.2)

When 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m5 RAW[1354925,1355205) \zeta=\pi_{\eta}
, we abbreviate the inner product and norm by

MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m6 RAW[1355251,1355650) \langle f,g\rangle
 and 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem1.p1.m7 RAW[1355655,1355925) \|f\|
.

Two functions equal 
MATH A4.SS1.p1.m1 RAW[1356037,1356229) \zeta
-almost everywhere represent the same
vector. A closed linear subspace 
MATH A4.SS1.p1.m2 RAW[1356300,1356769) \mathcal{M}\subseteq L^{2}(\zeta)

has orthogonal complement

MATH A4.SS1.p1.m3 RAW[1356796,1357790) \mathcal{M}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{g:\langle f,g\rangle=0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{M}\}
.
Its orthogonal projection is the unique bounded operator

MATH A4.SS1.p1.m4 RAW[1357849,1358139) \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}
 for which 
MATH A4.SS1.p1.m5 RAW[1358150,1358601) \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}
 and

MATH A4.SS1.p1.m6 RAW[1358606,1359137) f-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{M}}f\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
.

All operators in the convergence analysis are bounded linear operators
on the indicated Hilbert spaces unless explicitly stated otherwise.
The adjoint 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m1 RAW[1359373,1359610) \mathsf{L}^{*}
 is characterized by

MATH A4.SS1.p2.m2 RAW[1359631,1360466) \langle\mathsf{L}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathsf{L}^{*}g\rangle
.
An operator is self-adjoint if 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m3 RAW[1360499,1360794) \mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}^{*}
, a contraction
if 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m4 RAW[1360813,1361283) \|\mathsf{L}f\|\leq\|f\|
, and unitary if it is onto and preserves
norms. A self-adjoint involution, meaning 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m5 RAW[1361367,1361639) \mathsf{L}^{2}=I
, is
unitary. For self-adjoint operators 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m6 RAW[1361680,1362004) \mathsf{L}_{1},\mathsf{L}_{2}
,

MATH A4.E3.m1 RAW[1362225,1363262) \mathsf{L}_{1}\preceq\mathsf{L}_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\langle f,(\mathsf{L}_{2}-\mathsf{L}_{1})f\rangle\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }f

(D.3)

is the Loewner order. If 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m7 RAW[1363560,1363764) \mathsf{L}
 is self-adjoint, the
spectral theorem defines 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m8 RAW[1363811,1364130) \psi(\mathsf{L})
 for bounded Borel functions

MATH A4.SS1.p2.m9 RAW[1364159,1364349) \psi
; in particular 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m10 RAW[1364365,1364756) \mathbf{1}_{E}(\mathsf{L})
 is the orthogonal
projection onto the spectral set 
MATH A4.SS1.p2.m11 RAW[1364808,1364992) E
, and a non-negative operator has
a unique non-negative square root.

Definition D.2 (Markov operators and density evolution).

Let 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m1 RAW[1365588,1366091) \mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})
 be a Markov kernel preserving 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m2 RAW[1366122,1366322) \zeta
. Its
Markov operator on observables is

MATH A4.E4.m1 RAW[1366581,1367748) (\mathsf{K}f)(z)\coloneqq\int f(z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}(z,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})\,.

(D.4)

It is a contraction on 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m3 RAW[1368052,1368417) L^{2}(\zeta)
. Its adjoint

MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m4 RAW[1368431,1368676) \mathsf{K}^{*}
 governs density evolution: if a law has density 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m5 RAW[1368725,1368916) h
 with
respect to 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m6 RAW[1368933,1369133) \zeta
, then its density after one step is 
MATH A4.Thmtheorem2.p1.m7 RAW[1369170,1369478) \mathsf{K}^{*}h
.
Constants are fixed by both operators.

If 
MATH A4.SS1.p3.m1 RAW[1369612,1369847) P\ll\zeta
 has density 
MATH A4.SS1.p3.m2 RAW[1369860,1370043) h
, then

MATH A4.E5.m1 RAW[1370269,1370911) \chi^{2}(P\|\zeta)=\|h-1\|_{\zeta}^{2}\,.

(D.5)

Consequently, an operator norm bound for 
MATH A4.SS1.p3.m3 RAW[1371225,1371429) \mathsf{K}
 on

MATH A4.SS1.p3.m4 RAW[1371433,1371814) L_{0}^{2}(\zeta)
 gives the same bound for its adjoint

MATH A4.SS1.p3.m5 RAW[1371852,1372089) \mathsf{K}^{*}
, and hence gives 
MATH A4.SS1.p3.m6 RAW[1372107,1372328) \chi^{2}
 contraction.
